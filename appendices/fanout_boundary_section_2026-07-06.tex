\section{The \textsc{fanout} Boundary}
\label{app:fanout-boundary}

Every section of this paper has leaned on one operation. The self-referential argument turned on
what a system can and cannot copy of its own description; the continuity section defined its regime
as the interval during which no copy has been made; the Born rule fell out of what a copy cannot
carry; Wigner's friend was a story about who has copied what; and the particle sections' deepest
claim --- that colour never enters a record --- is a claim about copying. The operation is
\textsc{fanout}, the classical copy, and this section pays the debt of using it: what it is, where
the boundary it draws sits, why the two sides of that boundary look the way they do, and what the
firing of a copy commits.

The definition is categorical and exact. A special commutative Frobenius algebra on a
finite-dimensional Hilbert space is a copying--deleting pair $(\Delta, \varepsilon)$ with its
adjoints, and the theorem of Coecke, Pavlovi\'{c} and Vicary says that such structures are in
bijection with orthonormal bases: every classical structure \emph{is} a basis, and the
comultiplication acts as $\Delta\lvert i\rangle = \lvert i\rangle\lvert i\rangle$
\autocite{coeckepavlovicvicary2013orthogonal, CoeckeKissinger2017}. \textsc{fanout} in basis $B$ is
the firing of that comultiplication with the second copy released as a record; on states it is
$F_B(\rho) = \sum_i \langle i\rvert\rho\lvert i\rangle\, \lvert i\rangle\langle i\rvert$, which is
decoherence in $B$, and operationally it is the production of a classical record of $\rho$ in $B$.
Its dynamical character is sharper than ``stochastic'': the comultiplication is an isometry and not
a unitary --- $\Delta^{\dagger}\Delta = I$ while $\Delta\Delta^{\dagger}$ is a projector --- so it
embeds without erasing and cannot be run backwards from within its image. The non-surjectivity
\emph{is} the irreversibility. No-cloning is the same fact read in the other direction: the quantum
category has no natural diagonal, so a copy exists only where a classical structure has been
committed, and committing one is exactly what firing means.

\subsection{One boundary, synchronised signatures}

The boundary \textsc{fanout} draws has two sides, and the framework's claim is that everything
strange about the far side and everything familiar about the near side are one algebraic fact seen
in different vocabularies.

\begin{proposition}[The \textsc{fanout} correspondence]\label{prop:fanout-correspondence}
Let $F_B$ be \textsc{fanout} in basis $B$ on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space, and let $\rho$ be
a state. (a) Post-firing, the output $F_B(\rho)$ carries a measure on the Boolean lattice generated
by $B$ satisfying the Kolmogorov axioms, and admits a Boolean truth-value homomorphism consistent
with classical logic for every outcome. (b) Pre-firing --- whenever $\rho$ has a non-zero
off-diagonal element in $B$ --- any tomographically complete quasi-probability representation
respecting $B$ is signed, and the propositional structure on the projective lattice is
anhomomorphic with respect to $B$: no Boolean homomorphism extends the eigenstate truth assignments
to all projectors. (c) The off-diagonal elements $\langle i\rvert\rho\lvert j\rangle$ are the
common algebraic source of both pre-firing signatures, so the two vanish simultaneously --- at the
instant $F_B$ fires.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) is immediate: $F_B(\rho)$ is diagonal, its diagonal entries are non-negative and sum to one, so
they define a classical probability measure on $B$'s Boolean lattice, and each outcome fixes a
two-valued homomorphism in the ordinary way. (b) assembles two established results basis-relatively:
Spekkens' theorem that negativity of quasi-probability and generalised contextuality are equivalent
\autocite{spekkens2008negativity} supplies the measure-theoretic signature, and the Kochen--Specker
obstruction in its co-event form \autocite{sorkin2007anhomomorphic, dowkerghazitabatabai2008}
supplies the logical one. (c) is inspection: both signatures are functions of exactly the
off-diagonal data, and $F_B$ annihilates exactly that data. The individual ingredients are each
established in the cited literature; the identification of the \emph{common} boundary --- that the
logical and measure-theoretic signatures are synchronised, with \textsc{fanout} as the shared
operational criterion both programmes lacked --- is this framework's.
\end{proof}

The proposition earns its keep as a reframing of two research traditions. Sorkin's anhomomorphic
logic, developed with Dowker and collaborators for quantum measure theory, met its sharpest
difficulty in the failure of modus ponens for co-events; on the present reading that failure is not
a defect of the scheme but the correct signature of the regime it describes --- classical logic is
the post-\textsc{fanout} regime by construction, and anhomomorphism is the proper logic of the
interval in which no record has yet been formed, with the ``missing privilege'' of the one realised
co-event supplied by the copy that fixes a record. Spekkens' negativity, likewise, is the measure
face of the same interval, and the operational criterion for when positivity returns --- which the
negativity theorem by itself does not supply --- is the firing. Two programmes, one boundary,
and the boundary is an operation.

The unification runs deeper than the two signatures, and it is worth one plain paragraph. Four
things this paper has treated in four places are readings of the single fact that firing
annihilates off-diagonals: the Born rule is the boundary condition on the measure side, signed
amplitudes becoming positive probabilities (\S\ref{sec:complex-amplitudes},
\S\ref{sec:born-rule}); bivalence is the boundary condition on the logic side, anhomomorphic
becoming Boolean; decoherence is the mechanism, the off-diagonals going to zero; and the classical
record is the operational content, a clonable copy released into the environment. One event, four
faces. The framework's habit of deriving each face where it arose and then discovering them to be
one is, by now, its signature move, and this is the move's cleanest instance.

A corollary deserves a paragraph because it touches something usually taken as primitive. The
Boolean predicate $x = y$ is operationally a comparison, and a comparison decomposes as a copy
followed by an agreement test; the copy is \textsc{fanout}. By no-cloning the copy is available
exactly when the state is diagonal in the copying basis, so \emph{identity-of-reference is
Boolean-bivalently meaningful precisely in the post-\textsc{fanout} regime}. ``This particular
particle'' is a phrase whose truth-conditions exist on one side of the boundary and not on the
other --- which is why the indistinguishability of identical quantum particles needs no further
postulate here: it is the pre-firing face of the same fact.

\subsection{Three more faces of the same boundary}

The categorical definition above is one derivation of the boundary; three others arrive from
independent directions, and their convergence is the same over-determination this paper has leaned
on elsewhere.

The diagonal reading returns the boundary to the paper's founding argument. The copy map \emph{is}
the diagonal $\Delta: A \to A \times A$, and Lawvere's fixed-point theorem --- the engine of
\S\ref{sec:self-reference} --- is precisely a statement about what follows when a diagonal exists
\autocite{Lawvere_1969, Yanofsky2003}. The absence of a natural diagonal in the quantum category is
therefore the categorical face of the self-reference obstruction: the describer's private data is
exactly the data for which no diagonal is available, and Proposition~\ref{prop:f1-dictionary} is
the one-qubit instance --- the copier fibre-committed, the fibre uncopyable. We flag the honest
residue: the full chain from the Lawvere--Yanofsky obstruction to specifically \emph{complex}
rather than merely signed quasi-probability runs through the analytic continuation of
\S\ref{sec:complex-amplitudes} and is one step upstream of the proposition above, which treats
signedness only; the two-step structure is deliberate and the join is part of
Open Problem~\ref{op:sigma-functor}'s cargo.

The operational reading is redundancy. A measurement has happened, for a community of agents,
exactly when the outcome has been \textsc{fanout}-copied into at least one record they can share,
and the strength of the resulting fact grades with the number of independent copies --- the
redundancy $R$ that quantum Darwinism measures \autocite{zurek_2009} and that the Wigner's-friend
section used as its ladder (\S\ref{sec:wigners-friend}): the friend at small $R$, Wigner's
``no record'' as the $R \approx 1$ idealisation, objectivity-for-a-community as large $R$. The
boundary is not a metaphysical curtain but a countable quantity.

The thermodynamic reading is Landauer's. A record that can later be reset or overwritten carries an
entropy bill of $k_B T \ln 2$ per bit \autocite{Landauer_1961}, and the bill falls due at
amplification --- at the copying into stable, resettable records --- not at the microscopic
interaction, which is where the Wigner's-friend section placed it. The boundary is where the
thermodynamic arrow first grips: pre-firing there is nothing to pay for, because there is nothing
that counts as a record to erase.

Categorical, diagonal, operational, thermodynamic: four derivations, four vocabularies, one
boundary. None of the four is this paper's invention; the identification of the four as one is.

\subsection{The direction of the copy}
\label{sec:fanout-direction}

Everything above concerns \emph{that} a copy fires; this subsection concerns \emph{which way} it
points, and it is where the section pays the paper's largest outstanding debt. By the
basis--structure bijection, a copy cannot fire abstractly: every firing is a firing \emph{in a
basis}, which is to say every \textsc{fanout} event commits a direction. At one qubit this is not a
reading but a theorem --- Proposition~\ref{prop:f1-dictionary} exhibits the copier as
fibre-committed, the committed direction as the record direction, and the orthogonal data as what
no record carries.

The register-level claim is the candidate this section owes to the honest-debt subsection of the
geometry section (\S\ref{sec:hopf-honest-debt}). There the load-bearing assumption was named: the
selection of a preferred $\mathbb{C} \subset \mathbb{O}$ --- the split
$\mathbb{O} \cong \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C}^{3}$ on which the gauge group, the record sector and
the confinement argument all stand (\S\ref{sec:division-algebras}, \S\ref{sec:hopf-justification}
--- a deliberate forward reference at this section's recommended position). The candidate
resolution is that the selection is not an additional postulate but the register-level shadow of
classicality existing at all: a world with records is a world in which copying structures have
fired, every fired copy is a committed direction, and the ``preferred'' complex line is nothing
over and above the standing commitment of the network's record-making. No copy, no $\mathbb{C}$; a
classical world, and the split comes with it. What the candidate does not yet deliver is
uniqueness, and we price that plainly.

\begin{openproblem}[The uniqueness of the selection]\label{op:fanout-selection}
Show that the copying commitments of a connected record-forming network cohere into exactly one
global complex direction --- that the local basis commitments of separately fired \textsc{fanout}
events, each a theorem at its own site, patch into a single
$\mathbb{C} \subset \mathbb{O}$ for the register --- or characterise the obstruction to their
doing so. This is the local-to-global half of the honest debt of \S\ref{sec:hopf-honest-debt},
whose mereological half it inherits; a resolution would upgrade the candidate argument of this
subsection from ``classicality selects a direction'' to ``classicality selects \emph{the}
direction''. The problem is cross-linked with Open Problem~\ref{op:sigma-functor}, whose functorial
cargo --- the \textsc{fanout} functor from pre-firing structures to classical ones --- is the
natural setting for the patching question, and through it with
Open Problem~\ref{op:hopf-frobenius}.
 A solvable model system exists: the thin-spectrum analysis of symmetry-broken
superconductors makes the choosing of a vacuum a calculable decoherence process, exhibited as a
candidate route at \S\ref{sec:superconductor-witness}.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{The arrow, for the agent}

One consequence has been used by the spacetime sections and deserves its one-paragraph home. The
comultiplication's non-surjectivity means the copy has no two-sided inverse \emph{from within its
image}: an agent holding the record cannot, by any operation on what she holds, undo the firing
that produced it. That is a local arrow of time --- irreversibility for the record-holder, exact
and observer-indexed --- and it is the arrow the spacetime section's account of the time direction
and the predictions section's CPT discussion both lean on. Nothing global is claimed: the arrow is
as local as the record, which is the relational reading of \S\ref{sec:relationalism} applied to
time's direction rather than to position.

\subsection{What this section settles, and what it prices}

Settled: the definition (classical structure, basis, isometry-not-unitary, the non-surjectivity as
the irreversibility); the correspondence proposition, whose ingredients are established and whose
identification is the framework's; the four-readings unification --- Born boundary, bivalence,
decoherence, record as one event's four faces; the identity corollary; the one-qubit direction
theorem via Proposition~\ref{prop:f1-dictionary}. Candidate, flagged as such: the register-level
selection argument --- classicality as the origin of the preferred $\mathbb{C}$ --- delivered to
the honest-debt subsection in exactly the form promised there. Owed, and numbered: the uniqueness
of the selection (Open Problem~\ref{op:fanout-selection}); the functorial completion and the
signed-to-complex join, both carried as cargo of Open Problem~\ref{op:sigma-functor}. The
infinite-dimensional extension of the correspondence is noted and not needed: every register in
this paper is finite. What the paper has used everywhere, it now owns.
