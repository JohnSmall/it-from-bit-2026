\section{Interactions as Quantum Circuits}
\label{sec:interactions}

The previous sections gave the particles and their masses; this one gives their interactions, and it
does so in a form that is, in principle, executable. Three results already in place make this
possible: the gauge charges were cast operationally in \S\ref{sec:charges} as what a gate reads or
changes; the mass eigenstates were shown in \S\ref{sec:masses} to differ from the weak eigenstates by
the CKM rotation; and the gauge bosons were identified in \S\ref{sec:boson-masses} as bipartite
edges, with the map--state duality that lets an edge be read as a gate. Putting these together turns
a Feynman diagram into a quantum circuit on the internal qubit registers.

A word of caution before any circuit is drawn, because the diagrams that follow invite a picture
that must be refused. To speak of a fermion's ``internal qubit register'' is an accounting device,
not a description of a thing. There is no little blob called a particle with three qubits arranged
inside it; the register is not furniture in an interior. As \S\ref{sec:register} set out, the
fundamental objects of this framework are \emph{events}, and a particle is the channel of
communication inferred between them --- we observe events, in detectors and interactions, and
``particle'' is the name our classically-trained intuition gives to the connection across the
network of events that those measurements reveal. The qubit register is, correspondingly, the
\emph{information carried along that channel} between the events it links: the three qubits
$A$, $B$, $C$ are what the channel can convey about hypercharge, weak isospin and colour, not
compartments inside a sphere. When a circuit below shows a gate acting on the $B$-qubit, it depicts
an operation on the information the channel carries from one event to the next; it does not depict a
manipulation performed on the interior of an object in flight. The wires of the circuit are the
channels, the boxes are the events, and the whole diagram is a statement about how information
passes between events --- which is exactly what a Feynman diagram is, once the inherited image of
little balls tracing trajectories is set aside. We keep the register language throughout because it
is convenient and the bookkeeping is exact, but it should be read in this deflationary way at every
turn: registers track what is communicated between events, not what is contained within particles.

This section is where the framework offers the most handles to a reader, and we are explicit about
what each handle does and does not yet hold. What the circuits compute today is \emph{structure}:
which processes are allowed and which forbidden, with every conservation law holding by construction
rather than by imposed symmetry; how the entanglement classes of the participants transform through
an interaction; and where interference between contributions arises. What they do not yet compute is
\emph{rates}: the dictionary fixes which qubits each boson acts on, but not the vertex factors ---
the coupling normalisations --- and it does not include the kinematic sector (propagators, phase
space), which lives in the spacetime description that Paper~2 takes up. The boundary between the two
is stated precisely below, together with the calculations that would move it. A reader with a
quantum-computing background can run, modify and extend everything in this section on a laptop
simulator today; a reader from gauge theory can read each circuit as the internal-symmetry skeleton
of a Feynman diagram, with the spacetime flesh deliberately separated. We work one example in full
and then derive the result that gives the framework its sharpest falsifiable prediction in the
flavour sector.

\subsection{The vertex dictionary}

The translation rules are these. Each fermion in a process contributes three wires --- its $A$, $B$
and $C$ qubits, the register of \S\ref{sec:register} --- and particles created during the process
contribute wires initialised in a reference (vacuum) state. Time runs left to right. A vertex is an
edge attached to two nodes, so applying a boson is applying a gate to the definite qubits of the
fermion register that the boson's sector identifies
\autocite{coeckekissinger2010compositional}; external legs are state preparations and readouts. The
amplitude of the circuit --- the overlap of its output with a specified final register state --- is
the \emph{internal-structure factor} of the process amplitude: the part carried by gauge and
generation quantum numbers, cleanly separated from the propagator and phase-space factors that live
in the spacetime sector. The map of \S\ref{sec:charges}, read as gates:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\hline
boson & acts on & operation \\
\hline
photon $\gamma$ & $A$ (and $B$, via $Q=T_3+\tfrac12Y$) & $U(1)$ phase, if $Q\neq0$ \\
$W^\pm$         & $B$                                   & $SU(2)$ raise/lower of $T_3$ \\
$Z$             & $A$--$B$ jointly                      & neutral-current phase gate (below) \\
gluon $g$       & $C$                                   & $SU(3)$ rotation, if colour triplet \\
Higgs $H$       & doublet $\leftrightarrow$ singlet     & $L\leftrightarrow R$ chirality flip \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two of these can be made completely concrete at once, because their action is fixed by quantum
numbers the register already carries. The photon gate is the phase $e^{\mathrm{i}\varphi Q}$ with
$Q = T_3 + \tfrac12 Y$ read off the $B$ and $A$ qubits. The $Z$ gate is the two-qubit phase gate
whose phase on each fermion is proportional to $T_3 - Q\sin^2\theta_W$; with the tree value
$\sin^2\theta_W = \tfrac14$ of \S\ref{sec:charges} this is
\begin{equation}
  T_3 - Q\sin^2\theta_W \;=\; \tfrac34\,T_3 - \tfrac18\,Y,
  \label{eq:zphase}
\end{equation}
the coefficients $\tfrac34$ and $\tfrac18$ being pure consequences of the fibre-dimension value of
the mixing angle and the channel's $(T_3, Y)$. Both gates are therefore executable today, up to the
overall coupling normalisations, which are the open problem stated at the close. The charged current
is the only entry that moves a qubit between the two members of a doublet; the neutral currents act
within the gauge structure without doing so. That asymmetry is the whole of the flavour story below.

\subsection{Beta decay, simplified to the active quark}

Neutron beta decay $n\to p + e^- + \bar\nu_e$ changes exactly one quark: a down quark becomes an up
quark, $udd\to uud$, while the other two quarks are spectators. We therefore draw only the active
quark. Its charged-current vertex is a single gate --- the $W^-$ emission raising the weak isospin on
the $B$-qubit, $T_3:-\tfrac12\to+\tfrac12$ --- with the hypercharge qubit $A$ and the colour $C$ left
untouched. The hypercharge is untouched because $Y = +\tfrac13$ is the doublet label, common to both
members; the electric charge \emph{does} change, $\Delta Q = +1$, but that change \emph{is} the
$T_3$ raise, read through $Q = T_3 + \tfrac12 Y$, and it is carried off by the $W^-$. The colour is
untouched because the $W$ has no $C$-qubit action, so the colour-singlet structure that binds the
nucleon is preserved without any added constraint. The $W^-$ then decays to a lepton pair, which the
lepton sector's structure forces to be colourless: a fresh electron and antineutrino, born as a Bell
pair --- the bipartite shadow that Appendix~\ref{app:w-proof} proved is what a W-class channel shows
on any single cut, drawn here on the $B$-qubits alone.

\begin{center}
\begin{quantikz}
\lstick{$A_q$}            & \qw      & \qw      & \qw      & \rstick{$A_q$} \\
\lstick{$B_q\ (d)$}       & \gate{X} & \qw      & \qw      & \rstick{$u$} \\
\lstick{$C_q$}            & \qw      & \qw      & \qw      & \rstick{$C_q$} \\
\lstick{$B_e\ (\mathrm{vac})$}  & \qw & \gate{H} & \ctrl{1} & \rstick{$e^-$} \\
\lstick{$B_\nu\ (\mathrm{vac})$} & \qw & \qw     & \targ{}  & \rstick{$\bar\nu_e$}
\end{quantikz}
\end{center}

The top three wires are the active quark; the $X$ on $B_q$ is the $W^-$ emission. The lower two wires
are the vacuum qubits into which the $W^-$ deposits its entanglement, the $H$ and \textsc{cnot}
preparing the Bell pair. Every conservation law of the process is visible in the wiring rather than
imposed. Charge: $u\,(+\tfrac23) + e^-\,(-1) + \bar\nu\,(0) = -\tfrac13 = d$, with the books
balanced by the single $X$ gate through~$Q = T_3 + \tfrac12 Y$. Colour: the $C$ wire is untouched,
so the final state is exactly as much a singlet as the initial one. Baryon and lepton number: no
gate creates or destroys a quark register, and the lepton registers are created in a correlated
pair. A reader who wants to hold this handle directly can do four things with the runnable form in
Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit}: execute it and confirm the output state has amplitude support on exactly
two basis states --- the signature of the surviving Bell pair, in the appendix's qubit-ordering
convention; apply a partial trace over either lepton wire and confirm the other is maximally mixed,
which \emph{is} the entanglement the $W^-$ deposited; attach the two spectator-quark registers and
confirm they decouple; and attempt to violate any of the conservation laws above without leaving the
vertex dictionary, and fail. This worked example is single-generation: it sets the CKM factor
$V_{ud}\approx0.974$ to one. The full charged-current gate is the $T_3$ raise tensored with the CKM
rotation on the generation index, and making that executable is the first of the concrete
extensions below.

\subsection{What the circuits compute, and what they do not}

The capability boundary deserves its own statement, because the framework's usefulness to others
depends on it being drawn honestly. On the computing side of the line: selection rules (which
processes the dictionary admits), conservation laws (which hold by wiring, as just seen),
entanglement bookkeeping (the class of every participant before and after, which is the physical
content of \S\ref{sec:entanglement-classes}), and interference structure (where two gate sequences
contribute to the same final register state, their amplitudes add). On the other side: numerical
rates and cross-sections. Two ingredients are missing, and they are different in kind. The vertex
factors --- the numbers multiplying each gate --- are an open problem \emph{within} the present
framework, stated below. The propagators and phase space belong to the spacetime sector, which this
paper deliberately excludes and Paper~2 takes up; their absence here is a division of labour, not a
gap.

\begin{openproblem}[Vertex factors]\label{op:vertex-factors}
Fix the coupling normalisations of the vertex dictionary --- the amplitude carried by each gate ---
from the fibre geometry, so that circuit amplitudes become numerical predictions for the
internal-structure factors of processes rather than structural skeletons. The fibre-dimension
scaling that fixed $\sin^2\theta_W$ in \S\ref{sec:charges} is the natural starting point.
\end{openproblem}

Three computations are within reach of the present dictionary and would each extend the framework's
tested surface; we state them as invitations, and deliver each as an executed Qiskit circuit in
Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-handles}. First, the CKM-extended charged current
(Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-ckm}): append a
generation register --- a qutrit, or two qubits encoding the triality sector of
\S\ref{sec:generations} --- and promote the $W$ gate to the $T_3$ raise tensored with the CKM
rotation $V$. Executing it turns the observation of \S\ref{sec:masses}, that the Koide phase
$\delta = 2/9$ sits at the Cabibbo angle (Open Problem~\ref{op:cabibbo}), into something that can be
tested inside the circuit picture: the executed two-generation form returns
$\lvert V_{ud}\rvert = \cos(2/9)$ to $+0.11\%$ and $\lvert V_{us}\rvert = \sin(2/9)$ to $-1.74\%$
against the measured values. Second, the generation-one interference
behind the up-quark discrepancy (Open Problem~\ref{op:up-quark}) is, in circuit terms, an overlap
computation between a GHZ-borne and a W-borne contribution
(Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-interference}); the executed run establishes that the cut's Bloch radius
--- the invariant the mass proxy is built from --- responds at \emph{first order} in the admixture
amplitude and is rigid in its phase, so the candidate mechanism can be modelled, and is already
sharply constrained, before it is formalised. Third, the gluonic sector: bound colour states are
GHZ-class circuits closed on themselves, and the composition laws they obey --- spider fusion for
GHZ, anti-speciality for W, and the closed colour triangle whose free legs carry exactly
$\mathrm{GHZ}_3$, the baryon emerging from the wiring --- are executed in
Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-composition}, with the meson's two-edge loop closing onto exactly the
canonical Bell pair in Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-meson}; together these are the
primitive moves of the ZX-calculus organisation of the
glueball spectrum (\S\ref{sec:predictions}). None of these required resources beyond a laptop

with the entry-point beta-decay circuit --- with the run records and their analysis closing the

limited by known noise channels, with both canonical hadron skeletons --- the baryon triangle and
the meson loop --- assembling from closed colour wiring at a two-setting fidelity near $0.89$.

\subsection{The circuits as a discipline on the framework}

There is a reason to build these circuits beyond demonstrating to a reader that the structure is
real, and it is worth stating plainly because it shaped the framework as much as it displays it.
Writing a physical claim as a circuit is a discipline: a claim either compiles into the right
numbers or it does not, and ``it does not'' is information that no amount of careful prose can talk
its way past. For a framework of this kind --- one that reaches the Standard Model through a long
chain of structural identifications --- that discipline is precisely the guard most needed, because
the besetting risk of such work is the plausible-sounding sentence that has the right shape and no
content. An executable encoding cannot be persuaded by its author's eloquence.

One instance is worth recounting, because the encoding did not merely confirm a claim we already
held; it corrected one. In developing the glueball material of Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-composition},
we had described the distinction between the colour records a quark trio produces and those a single
gluon cannot in terms of the reduced state of one constituent: a quark's colour qubit, we reasoned,
should carry a classical pointer where a gluon's should not. Written as prose, this passes --- it
has the right vocabulary and gestures correctly at decoherence. Written as code, it failed at once:
computing the one-qubit reduced state of a constituent of a GHZ triple and of a Bell pair returns,
in both cases, the maximally mixed state. The reduced density matrix simply does not distinguish the
two, and the sentence we had written was wrong.

What the failure forced was the correct mechanism. The distinction between the two is not visible in
any single reduced state; it is the \emph{copy} structure --- the special-versus-anti-special
Frobenius property of \S\ref{sec:entanglement-classes} --- which lives in how the objects compose,
not in how they reduce. The GHZ tensor \emph{is} the copy operation that broadcasts a classical
record; the Bell pair is not, and cannot be made to be. Pursuing this in code then yielded a sharper
statement than the one we had set out to make: a two-gluon network does not, on its open legs,
reconstruct the copy structure --- it returns a Bell pair --- whereas the closed three-gluon
triangle returns exactly $\mathrm{GHZ}_3$, the copy tensor itself. The closure manufactures the
record-broadcasting structure that the constituents lack, and the framework's reading became, in
consequence, that classically detectable glueball structure is tied to the three-gluon topology
rather than the two-gluon scalar. That prediction is now a definite one, and it exists in that form
only because an initial encoding failed and had to be repaired.

We record this not as an anecdote but as a methodological commitment. The encodings in the
appendices serve the framework adversarially as well as expositorily: a structural claim that cannot
be compiled into the correct numbers is thereby exposed, and several of the framework's sharper
statements reached their final form precisely because a first encoding would not run as written. The
discipline has a clear boundary, and we are equally plain about it. Executable verification catches
the claims that are sharp enough to encode; it does nothing for those still too vague to compile ---
the map from a network's geometry to a mass in physical units, the confinement potential, and the
other matters left open in this paper are exactly the claims for which no runnable check yet exists,
and so exactly the claims where plausible prose remains dangerous. The circuits raise the floor;
they do not remove the burden of honesty everywhere the floor has not yet reached. If anything, the
episode is an argument for encoding more of the framework over time: every claim that can be pushed
from prose into a check is a claim moved from sounding right to being shown right, or shown wrong.

\subsection{No flavour-changing neutral currents}

The generation index of a fermion is the triality sector of \S\ref{sec:generations} --- which of the
three associator bracketings it occupies --- and this index is carried by the entanglement
\emph{class}, not by any single gauge qubit. The charged current can change a fermion's weak isospin;
the question is whether a neutral current can change its generation. It cannot, and the reason is
structural.

\begin{proposition}[No tree-level FCNC]\label{prop:fcnc}
A neutral-current coupling is proportional to the identity in the generation index. In the mass
basis, related to the weak basis by the CKM rotation $V$, it transforms as $V^\dagger D V$ with
$D = c\,\mathbb{1}$, so $V^\dagger D V = c\,\mathbb{1} = D$. The off-diagonal, flavour-changing
entries vanish identically: there is no tree-level flavour-changing neutral current.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The neutral bosons act on the gauge qubits: the photon a $U(1)$ phase on $A$, the $Z$ an $A$--$B$
entangling gate, the gluon an $SU(3)$ rotation on $C$. None of these acts on the triality index that
labels the generation, because that index lives in the entanglement class and is orthogonal to the
single-qubit gauge action. The neutral-current coupling is therefore the same in every generation,
$D = c\,\mathbb{1}$. A rotation of basis acts by conjugation, and $\mathbb{1}$ is invariant under
conjugation: $V^\dagger (c\,\mathbb{1}) V = c\,V^\dagger V = c\,\mathbb{1}$. Hence the neutral current
is flavour-diagonal in the mass basis as well as the weak basis.
\end{proof}

This is the Glashow--Iliopoulos--Maiani mechanism \autocite{glashowiliopoulosmaiani1970weak}, but with
a difference worth stating, and stating carefully, because an earlier form of this claim was too
strong. In the Standard Model the cancellation $V^\dagger\mathbb{1}V=\mathbb{1}$ is arranged by the
structure of the theory; here it is forced, because the neutral gates have no qubit to act on that
would carry the generation. The structural claim the framework makes is about the \emph{elementary
vertices}: no neutral gate changes the triality bracketing, at tree level or under any composition
--- there is simply nothing for it to grab. What this does \emph{not} say is that flavour-changing
processes with neutral external bosons are absent. They are not: composing charged-current gates in
loops generates them, exactly as in the Standard Model --- two $W$ vertices change the flavour, the
neutral boson attaches diagonally --- and such processes are \emph{observed}: the radiative decay
$b\to s\gamma$, the dimuon decay $B_s\to\mu^+\mu^-$, and neutral-meson mixing all proceed this way,
at rates matching the charged-loop calculation. Their observation is consistency support for the
dictionary, not tension with it: the framework's vertex content composes into the same loops.

The distinguishing, falsifiable content is then this. Because the register has no qubit a
flavour-changing neutral mediator could act on, the framework admits no such mediator \emph{at any
scale} --- no $Z'$, no extra scalars with flavour-changing couplings, no vector-like quarks ---
so every flavour observable is pinned at its charged-current-loop floor. For top decays that floor
is the Standard Model's $\mathrm{BR}(t\to Zq)\sim10^{-14}$, far below the sensitivity of any
existing or planned collider (current limits sit near $10^{-4}$--$10^{-5}$, with the high-luminosity
LHC reaching $10^{-5}$--$10^{-6}$). The framework therefore predicts that every FCNC search at
every future collider remains null --- not because the rate is exactly zero, but because nothing can
lift it from an unobservable floor. A single observed signal --- a $t\to Zq$, $t\to\gamma q$ or
$t\to gq$ decay at any collider --- would refute the framework outright, whereas in the Standard
Model the same signal would be welcomed as new physics. Equally, flavour observables already
measured at their charged-loop values must stay there: any future excess in $B_s\to\mu^+\mu^-$ or in
meson-mixing observables beyond the charged-loop prediction refutes the framework just as surely.
All current null results and all current measurements are consistent with this. The epistemic status,
plainly: Proposition~\ref{prop:fcnc} establishes the tree-level result rigorously; the vertex-level
prohibition at all orders is structural and strongly motivated, and we record its promotion to a
theorem as the open problem of this subsection.

\begin{openproblem}[The all-orders vertex prohibition]\label{op:fcnc-all-orders}
Prove that the triality index is inaccessible to the neutral gates under arbitrary composition ---
that no sequence of dictionary operations equivalent to a neutral-boson insertion changes the
generation bracketing --- promoting the structural no-mediator claim, and with it the
pinned-at-the-floor prediction for all flavour observables, to a theorem.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{The Higgs vertex}

The one remaining vertex is the Higgs, which mediates the chirality flip $L\leftrightarrow R$ that
gives mass (\S\ref{sec:masses}): it couples a doublet member to its singlet partner within a single
generation. Like the neutral currents, it does not change the triality bracketing, so it too is
flavour-diagonal --- the statement that the Higgs couples each fermion to itself, the coupling being
the Yukawa of \S\ref{sec:masses}. The chirality flip is the circuit-level form of the
\emph{Zitterbewegung} turnaround: in circuit terms a gate connecting the doublet and singlet
registers of a single generation.

\subsection{What is settled, and what is owed}

Settled: the vertex dictionary, with each boson a gate on definite qubits, two of the gates
(photon and $Z$) fully explicit up to normalisation through~\eqref{eq:zphase}; beta decay as an
executable circuit on the active quark, with every conservation law holding by wiring; the
capability boundary drawn honestly --- structure computed today, rates awaiting the vertex factors
--- with three concrete extensions stated as invitations; and, rigorously at tree level, the absence
of flavour-changing neutral currents (Proposition~\ref{prop:fcnc}), strengthened to the structural
no-mediator claim that pins all flavour observables at their charged-loop floor and is falsifiable
at every collider.

Owed, and recorded as the named problems: the vertex factors (Open Problem~\ref{op:vertex-factors}),
which are what stands between circuit amplitudes and numerical predictions; the all-orders vertex
prohibition (Open Problem~\ref{op:fcnc-all-orders}); and, carried from \S\ref{sec:boson-masses}, the
state-to-gate correspondence underlying the whole translation
(Open Problem~\ref{op:state-gate}). The neutrino masses and the scalar glueball, the remaining
forward-testable predictions, are collected in \S\ref{sec:predictions}.
