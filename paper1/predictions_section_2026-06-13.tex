\section{Predictions}
\label{sec:predictions}

The framework makes predictions of three kinds, and they are worth separating because they fail in
different ways. There are sharp numerical bets, resolvable by experiments now running or soon to run;
there are structural predictions, qualitative claims that survive even if the numerical fine-tuning
needs revision; and there are null predictions, where the content is that nothing will be seen, at any
scale, for a reason. We state each with the condition that would refute it, because a prediction is
only as strong as the measurement that could overturn it. We note in passing that several of the
structural claims underlying these predictions have already been instantiated as quantum circuits and
executed on hardware (\S\ref{sec:interactions}, Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-handles}); those are not
predictions awaiting experiment but checks already carried out, and we keep them distinct from the
forward bets below.

\subsection{The neutrino sector}

The neutrino sector is the framework's most exposed prediction and the one a near-term experiment is
likeliest to settle, so we lead with it. The structure is the rank-two seesaw of
\S\ref{sec:generations}: the $K_3$ obstruction yields three chiral generations and one a-chiral
(sterile) class, and the light neutrino masses are generated by exactly two heavy sterile partners.
Three consequences are robust, in the sense that they follow from the structure rather than from any
fit.

First, the lightest neutrino is \emph{exactly massless}, $m_1 = 0$. With only two sterile partners
the light mass matrix has rank two, so one of its three eigenvalues vanishes identically; this is a
structural statement, not a tuned one. Second, the ordering is normal, $m_1 < m_2 < m_3$, forced by
the same construction. Third, the two sterile states sit at the matching scale of the boson sector,
$\sim 3.6\ \mathrm{TeV}$ (Open Problem~\ref{op:matching-scale}), so that the neutrino and electroweak
desert predictions are testable together rather than independently.

The numerical predictions, with their status shown plainly, are collected in
Table~\ref{tab:neutrino}. We give the success and the failure side by side deliberately: the heavy
splitting and the overall scale come out well, and the light splitting does not, and concealing
either would misrepresent where the framework stands.

The light-splitting failure is genuine, and we record it as an open problem with a specific candidate
cause rather than as unexplained slack.

\begin{openproblem}[The neutrino splitting]\label{op:neutrino-splitting}
The phase-dilution rule of \S\ref{sec:masses} with $n = 2$ gives a neutrino mixing angle
$\theta_\nu = 126.365^\circ$, while an independent fit to both observed mass-squared splittings
prefers $\theta_\nu \approx 126.95^\circ$. The $0.6^\circ$ discrepancy is small in angle but, because
the neutrino hierarchy is so steep, compresses $\Delta m^2_{21}$ by a factor of about thirty.
Determine the correction beyond the simple $n = 2$ dilution. A natural candidate: the neutrino's zero
electric charge makes the $\lvert Q\rvert^{3/2}$ term of the mass construction vanish, removing the
charge-dependent contribution to the geodesic that the charged fermions carry, so the neutrino
geodesic may require a distinct second-order term that the other sectors do not.
\end{openproblem}

The convergence worth noting --- and worth being careful about --- is that the prediction
$m_1 = 0$ is not unique to this framework. The CPT-symmetric cosmology of Boyle, Finn and Turok
\autocite{boylefinnturok2018cpt,boylefinnturok2022bigbang} reaches the same conclusion by a
structurally similar route: two heavy partners render the light-neutrino mass matrix rank-deficient,
forcing one exact zero, with the third sterile state left over as a dark-matter candidate. We stress
that this convergence does \emph{not} constitute support for the present framework. The two
derivations proceed from entirely different premises --- there, a global CPT symmetry imposed across
the initial cosmological singularity; here, the local $K_3$ obstruction of the entanglement
structure. Convergent predictions from independent premises are evidence about the prediction, not
about either premise, and the two frameworks differ where they can be distinguished: the sterile
masses sit at $\sim 3.6\ \mathrm{TeV}$ here and at $\sim 5\times10^{8}\ \mathrm{GeV}$ there.

The convergence does, however, raise a question we think worth posing without claiming to answer.
CPT is the composition of charge conjugation, parity and time reversal, and each has a structural
correlate in the present framework. Parity appears as the opposite-handedness of entangled pairs
that, frustrated on $K_3$, generates the three generations in the first place
(\S\ref{sec:generations}). Charge conjugation appears as the complementary handedness assignment
that produces antiparticles. And time reversal appears as the \textsc{fanout} boundary of
\S\ref{sec:fanout-boundary}, where the irreversibility of an observation --- for the agent making it
--- supplies the local arrow. Whether the global CPT symmetry of the cosmological route is, in some
precise sense, the large-scale shadow of the same local handedness-and-FANOUT structure that here
forces three generations and two steriles, or whether the resemblance is superficial --- the two
notions of CPT being symmetries of different kinds of object --- we leave open. We flag it as a
question at the boundary between this framework and the CPT-cosmology programme, of interest
precisely because the two share both a prediction and, through the division-algebra approach to the
Standard Model, a mathematical heritage. It is not yet sharp enough to pose as a formal problem; it
is a direction we expect to be worth exploring.

The kill-conditions for the neutrino sector are correspondingly sharp. An inverted ordering, which
JUNO is positioned to determine within a few years, refutes the framework outright --- this is the
single cleanest falsification in the paper. A measured neutrino-mass sum well above $\sim 60$~meV
(from DESI, Euclid, or CMB-S4 over the next several years) refutes it; a sum driven toward zero, well
below $\sim 50$~meV, is in tension with $m_3 \approx 50$~meV. Establishing a non-zero mass for the
lightest neutrino refutes the rank-two structure, and so a fortiori does any fourth light neutrino or
a third light sterile.

\subsection{The strong sector: glueballs}

The strong sector offers one forward prediction and one structural bonus. The lightest glueball, with
quantum numbers $J^{PC} = 0^{++}$, is the simplest closed colour network: a two-gluon
($\mathrm{C}$-only) Bell loop, whose mass the framework reads as the geodesic length of that loop.
The value, $\sim 1.7\ \mathrm{GeV}$, agrees with the lattice-QCD determination of the lightest
glueball. We are careful about the status of this number: as with the up quark, it is at present a
\emph{consistency} with the known value rather than an independent ab-initio prediction, because the
map from the network's geometry to a mass in $\mathrm{GeV}$ is the geodesic reconciliation still owed
(Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic}). What \emph{is} in hand is the network: the closed colour
loops on which the glueball spectrum is built --- the baryon triangle and the meson loop --- were
constructed and run on hardware (Appendices~\ref{app:qiskit-composition} and \ref{app:qiskit-meson}),
so the topology is instantiated even though the metric is not yet derived.

The structural bonus is genuinely differential, in that it predicts more than standard QCD does.
Three-gluon glueballs are a closed $K_3$ network, and they should inherit the same four-class
resolution structure that the $K_3$ obstruction imposes on the fermion generations: three frustrated
classes and one symmetric. Standard QCD organises three-gluon singlets by two couplings, the
symmetric $d^{abc}$ and the antisymmetric $f^{abc}$; the framework predicts a richer channel
structure, four classes rather than two. This is a concrete prediction to test against lattice
three-gluon spectra, and a place where the framework and standard QCD make distinguishable
statements.

\subsection{Null predictions}

The third class is the set of things the framework forbids at every scale, where the prediction is an
absence that no new physics can lift.

There are no flavour-changing neutral currents above the charged-current loop floor, at any collider,
ever. The elementary neutral vertices are generation-blind to all orders (\S\ref{sec:interactions};
Proposition~\ref{prop:fcnc} establishes the tree-level result, and
Open Problem~\ref{op:fcnc-all-orders} records the promotion of the all-orders statement to a
theorem), and the framework admits no flavour-changing mediator at any scale, so every flavour
observable is pinned at the value the charged-current loops produce. For the top quark this means
$\mathrm{BR}(t\to Zq)\sim 10^{-14}$, far below the reach of any present or planned collider. The
content of the prediction is that these searches remain null forever: a single observed
flavour-changing neutral current --- a $t\to Zq$, $t\to\gamma q$ or $t\to gq$ decay at any collider
--- refutes the framework, whereas in the Standard Model such a signal would be welcomed as new
physics. The same cuts the other way for the processes already measured: any excess in
$B_s\to\mu^+\mu^-$ or in neutral-meson mixing above the charged-loop prediction also refutes the
framework. Every current measurement is consistent with this floor.

And every Higgs coupling is exactly Standard-Model. Because the framework derives the Yukawa
couplings rather than modifying the mechanism (\S\ref{sec:associator-higgs}), every Higgs
coupling-modifier is exactly unity: $\kappa_f = \kappa_V = 1$, at every attainable precision,
permanently. The high-luminosity programme's few-per-cent third-generation determinations and the
coming second-generation measurements at the muon and charm \autocite{cepeda2019hllhc} must all
land at the Standard-Model point; a single established deviation anywhere refutes the framework
outright. Where most extensions of the Standard Model predict departures somewhere, this framework
forbids them everywhere --- the desert below the matching scale and the exactness of the couplings
are two faces of one claim.

There is no fourth generation, at any scale. The $K_3$ obstruction yields exactly three chiral
generations and one a-chiral sterile class (\S\ref{sec:generations}); a fourth chiral generation is
structurally excluded, and its appearance at any collider would refute the framework.

There is no new physics between the electroweak scale and the matching scale at
$\sim 3.6\ \mathrm{TeV}$ (Open Problem~\ref{op:matching-scale}). This desert prediction is testable
jointly with the sterile-neutrino scale, the two being the same scale, and is refuted by any new
state in that range not accounted for by the framework.

And there is no proton decay of the kind grand unification predicts. $SU(5)$ and its relatives do not
arise from the parallelisable-sphere and division-algebra route that fixes the gauge group
(\S\ref{sec:generations} and \S\ref{sec:hopf-justification}), so the framework contains no mechanism
for proton decay through unification. This is consistent with the continued non-observation of proton
decay, which the framework gets for free rather than by tuning, and it is a standing null: an
observed proton decay with the branching structure of grand unification would refute it. The
operational face of the exclusion is drawn at \S\ref{sec:absent-gate}: the leptoquark vertices
grand unification requires are precisely the gates the framework's dictionary does not stock, and
their absence is the same fact as the conservation of baryon number.

And no experiment will ever hold a record of indefinite causal order. Causal structure is
post-\textsc{fanout} bookkeeping (\S\ref{sec:no-quantised-gravity}), so laboratory
indefiniteness lives below the locking threshold and definitises at it; gravitationally induced
entanglement, if and when observed, is predicted --- as a witness of the pre-geometric layer, not
of a superposed metric --- and gravitationally driven anomalous collapse rates are excluded.

\subsection{What has already been tested}

It is worth stating separately what is not a forward prediction at all, because it changes what the
word ``testable'' means for this framework. Three of the structural claims behind the results above
have been encoded as quantum circuits and run on hardware
(Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-handles}). The charged-current mixing that carries the Cabibbo
identification (Open Problem~\ref{op:cabibbo}) was executed as a circuit returning
$\lvert V_{ud}\rvert$ and $\lvert V_{us}\rvert$ from the framework's phase
(Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-ckm}). The interference mechanism proposed for the up-quark mass
(Open Problem~\ref{op:up-quark}) was run as the overlap computation that confirms its enabling
properties, on hardware (Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-interference}). And both canonical hadron
skeletons --- the closed colour networks on which the strong-sector predictions rest --- were
assembled from their wiring on quantum processors at a two-setting fidelity near $0.89$
(Appendices~\ref{app:qiskit-composition} and \ref{app:qiskit-meson}). These are not bets about future
experiments; they are checks that the framework's internal structure is faithfully realisable and
behaves as claimed, already carried out. The record now extends to a five-notebook batch on a
Heron-class device --- the monogamy signature, the two-gluon Bell discriminator, the leptonic
ninth, the mixing core's first rung, and the Meissner response of the superconductor witness,
sign flip measured --- collected with their noise readings at Appendix~\ref{app:hardware-fez}.

Beyond the circuits, the numerical record of the preceding sections stands as already tested by
existing data, and it belongs in this ledger even though it is postdictive rather than
predictive. The weak mixing angle is $\sin^2\theta_W = \tfrac14$ at tree level, running to
$0.2312$ against the measured $0.23122$; the Higgs mass is $m_H = v/2$, within $1.7\%$; the top
Yukawa is $y_t = 1$, within $0.9\%$; the Koide relation, at the matched phase $\delta_0 = 2/9$,
returns the charged-lepton masses to a hundredth of a per cent; the strong-CP angle is
$\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}} = 0$ exactly, by the topology of the colour class
(\S\ref{sec:entanglement-classes}); and eight of the nine charged-fermion masses land within
about one per cent of observation, the ninth being the up quark's recorded exception (Open
Problem~\ref{op:up-quark}). These are derived where they are stated (\S\ref{sec:masses},
\S\ref{sec:boson-masses}) and are not re-derived here; their epistemic weight is that of
postdiction --- the numbers were known before the framework was --- and we claim exactly that
weight, no more. What makes them worth listing beside the forward bets is the count of
adjustable constants behind them: no fitted dimensionless parameters --- the one dimensionless
number set by hand, the Koide phase $\delta_0 = 2/9$, is matched rather than derived (Open
Problem~\ref{op:koide-delta}) --- with $v$ the single dimensional input. The forward predictions
of this section are what remains to be tested by nature; the structural claims have, in this
limited but concrete sense, already been tested by construction.

\subsection{Summary}

The framework lives or dies on near-term data. The ordering of the neutrino masses alone, which JUNO
is positioned to measure within a few years, could end it; that exposure is the point. What survives
even if the numerical fine-tuning of the neutrino splitting needs revision is the structural core ---
the massless lightest neutrino, normal ordering, exactly three generations, no flavour-changing
neutral currents, no fourth generation, no proton decay --- because those follow from the
construction rather than from any fit. The numerical gaps that remain, chiefly the neutrino splitting
(Open Problem~\ref{op:neutrino-splitting}), the map from network geometry to mass
(Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic}), and the vertex normalisations that would turn the
circuit skeletons into rates, are the invitations the framework extends to others; the executable
circuits are the means by which those invitations can be taken up. Where the programme goes if these
predictions hold --- the unification of quantum mechanics and general relativity at ordinary scales,
and the cosmological consequences --- is the subject of the papers that follow.
