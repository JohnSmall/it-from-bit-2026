\begin{abstract}
Quantum states have the curious property that are exactly what's required to represent the
classically impossible states which occur in proofs of non-computability and undecidability,
which in turn rely on in self-reference. This is a pretty big clue to understand what quantum
states actually are. We follow that clue in an attempt to uncover an insight into John Wheeler's
question "How come the quantum?". If a new insight is to be worth anything then it must enable
us to answer hard questions and calculate numbers we can compare with experiment. And it does.
We find a rationale for the 3 dimensions of space, for the symmetry groups of the Standard
Model, a valuable connection between multiparticle entanglement and the Standard Model via Hopf
fibration which enables us to understand why the strong force ignores parity and do a
preliminary calculation of some free parameters. The connection also enables us to create some
simple quantum circuits for particle interactions. There are many gaps in the project and we
list a number of open problems.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The fact that a quantum state, by virtue of superposition, can represent the classically
inconsistent states which occur in self-referential paradoxes is a pretty big clue to understand
what a quantum state actually is. This fact is so obvious that it has not gone unnoticed. John
Archibald Wheeler on a flight back to America had a flash of inspiration and wrote ;-
\begin{quotation}
Add "participant" to "undecidable propositions" to arrive at physics
  \par
\hfill \autocite{wheeler_j_1974}
\end{quotation}
When he got back to Princeton he immediately went to G\"odel's office to explain the idea, but
G\"odel was so annoyed he threw him out (ref). Nevertheless, Wheeler developed his idea into the
phrase "It from bit,and bit from it". Over the years many people have approached the notion of
self-reference being in some ways fundamental. This is not the place for a full literature
review, Karl Svovil's book \enquote{Physical Acausality} does a very complete job
\autocite{svozil_k_2018}. More recently, and highly relevant are Szangolies papers
\autocite{szangolies_2020, szangolies_j_2018, szangolies2025standardmodel, szangolies_j_2021}
and my own earlier attempt at grappling with these issues in \autocite{small_2006}. There are
many more references I could have included to support the idea that non-computability and
quantum states are deeply linked, but we need just enough to delve into the idea, and no more.

Curiously, we note that the $U(1) \times SU(2) \times SU(3)$ symmetry group of the standard
model is in some peculiar way linked to the division algebras and hence to parallelisable
spheres, yet self-reference requires information flowing round in circles, which requires the
parallelisable property. Could there be a connection? The idea is clearly crazy, and yet so
tantalising as to be worthy of deeper investigation.

Following Wheeler, we take self-reference, an hence non-Turing computability, to be fundamental,
but we only ever experience causal, computable sequences. Acausality forced to appear as strict
causality. We use a simple principle, underlying self-reference, or acausality forced to appear
in a non-self-referent, strictly causal framework. Self-reference together with the negation of
self-reference. Surprisingly, this simple principle enables us to spot connections which others
have guessed at yet not followed up. We find that some of the free parameters of the Standard
Model can actually be derived. Our hypothesis is that the laws of physics are completely
constrained by requiring that a fundamentally acausal, self-referential and ungrounded, Nature
to appear as a strictly non-self-referential causal sequence.

Most of the steps taken in this chain of reasoning have already been made by others, in many
cases a very long time ago. The novelty is putting the pieces of jigsaw together to find a
coherent picture. In many areas there are missing pieces, also known as open problems, which I
have listed. Because the picture emerging around the gaps fits present experimental evidence, I
have chosen to take scary leaps of faith and assume the gaps can be filled in at some point.

\subsection{Use of AI}
This paper is very much a collaborative effort between me and Claude AI. I provide the ideas and
send Claude off to find the pieces of jigsaw needed to compose the picture. AI is exceptionally
useful in doing this because it has reasonable expertise in many different areas and finding
connections between seemingly unconnected realms is made a lot easier. So a question "That GHZ/W
split in 3 particle entanglement looks suspiciously like the quark/lepton split, tell me more
about it?" can be turned into useful theorems. If the key fits, the lock opens. Although the
derivation of the Born rule using Hopf fibrations is entirely something Claude came up with
unprompted.

It might seem that a question about relating multi-particle entanglement to the structure of the
Standard Model came completely out of the blue. But I hope to show the reader by working through
the consequences of accepting self-reference as fundamental then it becomes an obvious question
to ask.
\subsection{Paper structure}
We start with a question \enquote{What is a quantum state} \ref{sec:what_is_a_quantum_state},
then move on to Karl Popper's 1950 discussion of self-reference \ref{sec:popper}, from that
we're able deduce that self-reference must involve negative probability
\ref{sec:negative_probaility} and that the probability is due to an in principle lack of
information \ref{sec:self-ignorance} from that we're able to deduce that we must use complex
probability \ref{sec:complex-amplitudes}. Once we're arrived at that stage we go to find that
this forces reversible continuity \ref{sec:reversible-continuity} and once that is settled it
leads to the L2 metric \ref{sec:reversible-continuity-l2}. We also note that complex information
has implications for computability and go off on a tangent to explore the consequences of that
\ref{sec:space-of-computations}. We find that a network of computable and non-computable events
must have at most 3 dimensions of non-computablity, with interesting parallels to the
relationalist view of spacetime in General Relativity. Having collected a few key insights and
named some interesting open problems we return to the main thread to consider Wigner's friend in
the Hopf fibration picture \ref{sec:wf-friend}, which leads to a derivation of the quantum
postulates in \ref{sec:postulates}. That in turn leads to a rationale for Hopf fibrations in
fundamental particle interactions \ref{sec:hopf-justification}, which compels the division
algebras in particle physics \ref{sec:division-algebras}. It becomes obvious to use Hopf maps to
bridge multi-particle entanglement and fundamental particle physics and cast hard problems in
one sector to the other where they have easy solutions. Hence we find a rationale for the colour
force being chirality blind, our first interesting result \ref{sec:achirality_qcd}. Once the
bridge has been set up we can then get out quantum numbers in \ref{sec:quantum_number} and
deduce the Kiode relations \ref{sec:Kioid} as well as the weak mixing angle
\ref{sec:weak_mixing_angle}. We then move on to see that the particle zoo of the Standard Model
has an explanation rooted in the geometry of multi-particle entanglement \ref{sec:particle_zoo}.
Encouraged by this we attempt a calculation of particle mass and get some preliminary results,
with some awkward misses \ref{sec:masses}. Since we can map fundamental particles to
multi-particle entanglement we can use quantum circuits to model particle interactions which we
do in \ref{sec:quantum_circuits}.
\subsection{Principal Results}
This is a long paper, so it is wise to give the reader something to look forward to as they
trudge through verbiage. I'll list some of the results obtained. The following structural
features of the Standard Model are derived, not assumed: In most cases the results are not new,
our contribution is to present a rationale for why they can be no other way.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Gauge group.} The Standard Model gauge group
$G_{\mathrm{SM}} = SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)/\mathbb{Z}_6$ is the residual symmetry that
remains when a preferred complex direction $\mathbb{C} \subset \mathbb{O}$ is singled out ---
equivalently, the subgroup of $\mathrm{Spin}(9)$ commuting with the complex structure fixing the
split $\mathbb{O} \cong \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C}^3$ (Section~\ref{sec:hopf-justification}).
Why one direction is fixed rather than all treated symmetrically is the framework's central
assumption, resolved in candidate form in Section~\ref{sec:fanout-boundary}.

\item \textbf{Particle content.} The complete Standard Model particle spectrum is in bijection
with the entanglement primitives of four qubits, arranged in a Cayley--Dickson ladder mapped to
Hopf fibration, with exhaustiveness guaranteed by three independent termination theorems:
Hurwitz (no division algebras beyond~$\mathbb{O}$) \autocite{hurwitz1898composition}, Adams (no
Hopf fibrations beyond the octonionic case) \autocite{adams1960hopf}, and Coecke-Kissinger
(compositional completeness of GHZ and W classes) \autocite{coeckekissinger2010compositional}.
The fourth qubit is required to get 3 generations of fermions.

\item \textbf{Spacetime dimensionality.} By recognising spacelike separation to be an analog of
non-computable separation between computational states we find that the requirement that state
reduction on the Bloch sphere be consistent with Lorentz boosts demands
$\mathrm{Spin}(1,d) \cong SL(2,\mathbb{A})$ for a normed division algebra~$\mathbb{A}$. The
computability split selects $\mathbb{A} = \mathbb{C}$, giving
$SL(2,\mathbb{C}) = \mathrm{Spin}(1,3)$ and thus $d = 3$ spatial dimensions. The six internal
dimensions of $SU(3) \times SU(2) \times U(1)$ account for the reduction from $d = 9$ (the
octonionic case) to $d = 3$, reproducing the Kaluza--Klein dimensional decomposition
$9+1 = (3+1) + 2 + 4$ with a structural explanation. But note that those extra dimensions cannot
admit a metric structure.


\item \textbf{Three generations.} The triality automorphism of $\mathrm{Spin}(8)$, which
permutes the three eight-dimensional representations of the octonions, produces exactly three
generations of fermions. \textbf{wrong! to be fixed with sedenions}

\item \textbf{Chirality.} The non-associativity of $\mathbb{O}$ distinguishes left and right
multiplication, breaking parity at the algebraic level.
  \textbf{wrong! to be fixed with sedenions}

\item \textbf{Born rule.} The observer's self-referential ignorance of their own computational
state generates a global $U(1)$ phase freedom. The minimum-degree polynomial invariant under
this symmetry is $|\psi|^2$, yielding the Born rule without additional postulates
(Section~\ref{sec:born-rule}).

\item \textbf{Entanglement monogamy and Tsirelson Bound} This observation is not strictly a part
of the framework, but while putting together pieces of the jigsaw I noticed that the Hopf
fibration representation of multiparticle entanglement provides an elegant explanation for
entanglement monogamy and the Tsirelson bound. Both the Coffman--Kundu--Wootters inequality
(Section~\ref{sec:monogamy}) and the Tsirelson bound (Section~\ref{sec:tsirelson} follow
geometrically from the compactness of the $S^4$ base of the quaternionic Hopf fibration: a ball
has a unique centre, and one cannot overshoot it.
\end{enumerate}

The idea that entanglement and particle physics share the same algebraic structure is not new.
This ideas has been worked on for decades see \autocite{borsten2009blackholes}, but in the
context of string/brane theory. Taking inspiration from Szangolies
\autocite{szangolies2025standardmodel}, we can map classes of multi-particle entanglement to
Standard Model particles and hence transform problems in particle physics into problems in
entanglement where easy solutions are possible. The key is that Hopf fibrations show up both as
qubit entanglement classes and in particle physics. This has already been established, our
contribution is a rationale.

The Higgs is not an entanglement class. It is the Hopf bundle's nontriviality itself, promoted
to a dynamical field following
\autocite{szangolies2025standardmodel, finkelstein_1962, finkelstein_1963}.

This mapping enables an easy and obvious deduction. The strong CP angle
$\theta_{\mathrm{QCD}} = 0$ is topologically forced: the GHZ entanglement class characterising
quarks has maximal three-tangle ($\tau_3 = 1$) and zero pairwise concurrence, precluding the
oriented linking structure that would generate a non-trivial vacuum angle.
