\section{Confinement}
\label{sec:confinement}

Confinement is the statement that colour-charged particles are never found in isolation: no free
quark, no free gluon, only colour-singlet combinations. In the standard account this is attributed
to the dynamics of the non-Abelian colour field --- the flux tube, the linear potential, the running
coupling --- and remains, in its quantitative form, among the hardest open problems in field theory.
The framework offers a different account of \emph{why} confinement holds, one that is structural and
exact rather than dynamical and asymptotic, and it is careful about the boundary between what that
account settles and what it leaves open. We place the section here, beside the GHZ$\to$colour and
W$\to$lepton results (\S\ref{sec:entanglement-classes}, \S\ref{sec:generations}) it depends on,
because confinement in this framework is the same kind of fact as those: a consequence of the
entanglement class, read off the structure rather than computed from a force.

\subsection{Confinement as an exhausted entanglement budget}

A baryon's three quarks are bound through their colour ($C$) qubits, and the binding state is the
maximal GHZ state --- the colour singlet. The decisive property of that state is supplied by the
monogamy of entanglement. Entanglement cannot be freely shared: a system that is maximally entangled
with some partners has none left to spend on others. Quantitatively, for three qubits the
Coffman--Kundu--Wootters monogamy inequality
\autocite{coffmankunduwootters2000,osborneverstraete2006} bounds the total,
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{A(BC)} \;\ge\; \tau_{AB} + \tau_{AC},
\end{equation}
and the genuinely tripartite part --- the three-tangle $\tau_3$ --- measures what is held
irreducibly among all three. For the maximal GHZ state $\tau_3 = 1$: the budget is fully spent on
the tripartite binding, and \emph{every} bipartite tangle, internal or external, is forced to zero.

The consequence is confinement. A colour singlet has $\tau_3 = 1$, so it has no entanglement
whatever left over to share with anything outside the triple. A quark cannot be entangled with an
external colour charge because the singlet it belongs to has spent its entire colour-entanglement
budget on its two partners. There is no free quark because there is no budget for the external
colour correlation a free quark would require. This is verified directly: the closed-wiring
fusion laws are executed in Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-composition}, and the executable verification
accompanying the code release checks the rest --- the colour singlet returns $\tau_3 = 1$ with both
bipartite tangles identically zero, and an attempt to entangle one quark with an external qubit
succeeds only in lockstep with the destruction of the singlet, the external entanglement rising as
an exact function of the falling GHZ fidelity --- so a quark can acquire an outside partner only by
ceasing to be part of a colour singlet.

The reading deserves emphasis because it differs from the standard one in kind, not merely in
technique. This is not a force that grows with separation; it is a property of the \emph{state},
with no distance in it. The three-tangle $\tau_3$ is a function of the amplitudes, and a GHZ state
has $\tau_3 = 1$ whether its qubits are adjacent or galaxies apart --- entanglement is not
distance-dependent, and nothing in the argument above mentions a separation. Confinement, in this
account, is the flat structural fact that a colour singlet has no entanglement available for an
external colour partner. It is also, in consequence, \emph{exact}: a budget is either saturated or
it is not, and the maximal GHZ state saturates it completely. Where lattice QCD extracts confinement
as an asymptotic, numerically-determined property of the colour field, the framework states it as an
exact property of the singlet state.

\subsection{The other reading of the ledger}\label{sec:budget-converse}

A careful reader of the budget argument will ask the obvious question: the W class does not
exhaust its budget, so what may the leftover buy? The ledger makes the question precise. Each
qubit of the canonical W state carries one-to-rest tangle $\tau = \tfrac89$ against the ceiling
of $1$, spent as $\tfrac49$ on each internal pair with nothing on the three-tangle
\autocite{coffmankunduwootters2000}; exactly $\tfrac19$ per qubit is unspent. Since the monogamy
inequality holds for any number of parties \autocite{osborneverstraete2006}, the ledger is well
posed against any exterior, and the leftover has exactly two possible destinations.

Spent inward, it changes the species. Converting slack into three-tangle within the same register
is continuously available at the level of amplitudes, but any non-zero three-tangle places the
state in the GHZ class: the entanglement classes are discrete, and there is no such thing as a
lepton with a little internal GHZ. This is the same discipline the up-quark discussion imposes on
itself (\S\ref{sec:masses}): admixture is a perturbation off a canonical orbit, never a species
spending a surplus at home.

Spent outward, it is a theorem, and it is the converse of confinement. For any qubit that retains
the W grade of internal commitment, the external pairwise tangle is bounded by the slack,
$\tau_{\mathrm{ext}} \le 1 - \tfrac89 = \tfrac19$, while for a GHZ-class register the same
bound reads zero, exactly, always. Confinement and unconfinement are one theorem read at two
budget levels: the quark's register offers no external handle at all; the lepton's offers
precisely one ninth of one, per qubit. The bound is tight --- a one-parameter coupling family
attains $\tau_{\mathrm{ext}} = \tfrac19$ --- and the same computation shows where committed
budget goes once the handle is grasped: at maximal coupling the internal pairwise tangles have
drained to zero while the one-to-rest tangle saturates, the ledger reading $\tfrac19$ external
pairwise plus $\tfrac89$ in the multipartite residual. The leftover budget is thus the door, and
through it the whole commitment can migrate into collective, GHZ-type correlation spanning the
register and its exterior: the leftover participates in GHZ entanglement freely, provided the GHZ
is external. Whether the $\tfrac19$ ceiling is attainable with the internal
$\tfrac49 + \tfrac49$ fully intact, our family does not settle; the question is recorded below.
An executable verification of the ledger, the ceiling and the migration accompanies the code
release.

Two remarks bound the reading. First, the trade between a system's residual entanglement capacity
and its external correlations is itself theorem-shaped in quantum information
\autocite{koashiwinter2004}, which is the right footing for a structural suggestion this
framework will need later: record-broadcast structures are GHZ-shaped copy tensors, external GHZ
participation is the one mode whose pairwise cost vanishes, and the lepton's ninth is plausibly
what makes it the fermion that appears in classical records directly --- a reading that travels to
\S\ref{sec:fanout-boundary} as structure, not as claim. Secondly, at four parties the budget
question has a celebrated sharp edge: no four-qubit state maximises every one-to-rest tangle at
once \autocite{higuchisudbery2000}, the observation that founded the study of absolutely
maximally entangled states, where the four-qubit no-go now has many proofs
\autocite{hubersiewert2026}. The ledger's refusal to let everything be maximal everywhere is not
a peculiarity of this framework but a theorem of the four-party landscape it opens onto.

\begin{openproblem}[The leptonic ninth]\label{op:w-slack}
Determine whether the external ceiling $\tau_{\mathrm{ext}} = \tfrac19$ is attainable with the
internal W commitments $\tfrac49 + \tfrac49$ fully intact; characterise the four-party states
realising the boundary of the ledger; and give the ceiling a physical reading --- what operational
capacity of the lepton, record formation, binding correlation or participation in external
GHZ-type structure, is bounded by one ninth per register qubit, and whether the bound is visible
in any measurable setting. Part of the reading is already in hand: the ceiling has a geometric
identity --- the slack equals the $\mathbb{C}$-access ceiling of the Hopf base, both being the
squared marginal Bloch radius, $r^{2} = 1 - 4\det\rho = \sup C_{\mathrm{frac}}$
(\S\ref{sec:entanglement-classes}) --- so the ninth \emph{is} the lepton's singlet-direction
access seen in budget coordinates, and confinement is the $r = 0$ instance of the same identity.
What remains is the operational half, and the attainment and boundary questions above.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{Why the Bell pair is not a counterexample}\label{sec:bell-not-counterexample}

A reader who has followed the ledger to this point will now press the strongest objection
available, and it deserves to be stated at full strength before it is answered. In a Bell pair the
entanglement budget is completely spent: each party's one-to-rest tangle is $1$, each marginal is
$\tfrac12 I$, and by the identity above each party's slack is exactly zero --- the same per-party
arithmetic as a GHZ member's. The pair is exactly as fragile: measure either photon and the
entanglement is gone. And yet Bell photons are measured, singly and routinely, in every
quantum-optics laboratory on Earth. If saturation plus fragility were what confinement means, the
Bell pair would refute it. Why should a quark in a GHZ state be any different?

The first move is to sharpen what confinement claims, because the objection quietly shifts it.
Confinement is not the claim that the party cannot be measured: quarks are interrogated by the
million in deep inelastic scattering \autocite{breidenbach1969observed}, exactly as Bell photons
are counted in coincidence experiments. Fragility under measurement is symmetric between the two
cases, and it is not the claim. Confinement is the claim that no classical record ever carries bare
colour, and that no colour-charged channel ever acquires its own worldline --- its own node in the
located relational network. The question is therefore not whether a saturated party can be touched,
but what a record of the touching would have to carry, and whether the party's individuating data
can ever join the network on its own.

The first disanalogy is the sector of the would-be record. When a Bell photon is measured, the
outcome --- a polarisation, a detector index --- is data of the recordable sector: base-side,
frame-shared, Bloch-geometric. Polarisation is not a gauge charge; it is exactly the kind of thing
\textsc{fanout} copies, because the selected complex direction \emph{is}, by construction, the
direction classical records live in --- that is what the computability split
$\mathbb{O} \cong \mathbb{C} \oplus \mathbb{C}^{3}$ selects, at the epistemic status the
selection carries (\S\ref{sec:hopf-honest-debt}). A colour outcome would be a record of
$\mathbb{C}^{3}$-side data --- the non-selected directions --- and no classical wire carries them.
To copy a colour state one needs an ancilla spanning the colour space, which is to say an ancilla
carrying an $SU(3)$ representation, which is to say another coloured object: a colour-basis
\textsc{fanout} is built of quarks and gluons, not of records. This is the fibre-privacy doctrine
one level up: Propositions~\ref{prop:f1-dictionary} and~\ref{prop:trace-blind} prove that
non-record-side data cannot enter any classical record for the $U(1)$ fibre, and colour is the
$\mathbb{C}^{3}$ of the very same split. The Bell photon's measured quantity lives on the
recordable side; the quark's defining charge lives on the other. Same budget arithmetic, opposite
sectors.

The second disanalogy is the access arithmetic, and here the identity of
\S\ref{sec:entanglement-classes} does exactly the work it was drawn out for. Individuation in the
relational network --- acquiring a location, in the reading of \S\ref{sec:space-of-computations}
and \S\ref{sec:relationalism} --- means acquiring presence in the singlet-accessible sector, and
the identity says that capacity is one invariant: slack $= r^{2} = \sup C_{\mathrm{frac}}$. Run it
per party and the objection resolves into a ladder. The Bell photon's \emph{locked} wire has
$r = 0$; but the photon-as-channel carries other, unentangled wires --- spatial mode, frequency ---
at full access, so its locatability rides degrees of freedom the Bell pair never touched, and it
was an individuated channel with its own worldline from the moment of emission; after the
measurement, even the locked wire jumps to $r = 1$, and two legitimate free particles remain. The
lepton's own species wire carries $r^{2} = \tfrac19$: the ninth is literally its admission ticket
to the classical network. (Now measured: $r^{2} = 0.0935$ on hardware, the deficit exactly
depolarisation's; Appendix~\ref{app:hw-ninth}.) The quark's locked wire \emph{is} its species-defining sector, its
access is exactly zero, and there is nothing else colour-side to ride. Its electroweak wires are
accessible --- which is precisely why deep inelastic scattering can interrogate a quark in place,
the vertex turning the frame without holding the state (\S\ref{sec:frame}) --- but extraction as
a free particle would mean the quark's \emph{defining} charge acquiring a record chain, and that
sits at $r^{2} = 0$, in a sector no record can enter regardless. The hadron, whose completed colour
part is $SU(3)$-trivial and record-side clean, is the unit that carries the worldline. The Bell
case and the quark case, which the objection sets side by side, sit at opposite ends of the ladder
once the right question is asked: not ``how entangled is the locked wire?'' but ``what access does
the party's individuating data have?''

A coda makes the difference vivid. Measuring half a Bell pair leaves two definite-polarisation
photons: recordable species, genuine asymptotic states. ``Measuring'' one quark's colour, were the
wire to exist, would leave a definite-colour quark and a non-singlet remainder --- objects whose
defining data still cannot enter records. The operation would not liberate the quark; it would
relocate the impossibility. And the co-location statement of this section's opening survives the
objection refined rather than damaged: the Bell photons were never co-located in the relational
sense --- each joined the network at emission through its spatial wires --- whereas the quarks'
only network-joining data is collective, which is exactly what the ledger said in numbers.

The statuses, plainly. The ladder's arithmetic is theorem-grade throughout --- the identity, the
fibre-privacy propositions, the marginal facts --- and an executable verification of its rows
accompanies the code release. The reading of the selected direction as the record direction
inherits the status of the selection itself (\S\ref{sec:hopf-honest-debt}); the individuation
reading is the relational thesis of the spacetime section, at that section's own flags. The
objection was worth its subsection because its premise is true: the budget alone does not confine.
The budget, the sector, and the access do.

\subsection{One structure, two consequences: confinement and the vector-like colour force}

The same feature of the GHZ state that gives confinement also explains, at one stroke, why the
colour force is not chiral. The GHZ state has no oriented pairwise substructure: its entanglement is
irreducibly tripartite, carried among all three qubits at once, with --- as the budget shows --- no
bipartite content to host a relative orientation. In \S\ref{sec:generations} this was what forced
the colour sector to be vector-like and the QCD vacuum angle to vanish structurally, there being no
oriented pairwise links to carry a CP-violating phase. It is the same fact, seen from the other side,
that gives confinement: \emph{all} of the colour entanglement is tripartite and internal, which is
at once why none is available externally (confinement) and why none is pairwise and orientable
(chirality-freedom). Confinement and the vector-like character of the strong force are not two
properties of the colour sector that happen to coexist; they are one property of the GHZ class read
twice. The contrast with the W (lepton) sector, whose entanglement \emph{is} pairwise and which is
correspondingly chiral and unconfined, is the same contrast in reverse.

\subsection{The confinement potential is distance-free in origin}

The structural account establishes \emph{that} quarks cannot be isolated; it does not yet yield the
\emph{measured, distance-dependent} phenomenology --- the static quark--antiquark potential, its
linear rise, the slope that lattice QCD and Regge trajectories call the string tension. We state the
gap precisely, because it is more interesting than a missing number. Distance, in this framework, is
not fundamental: it is emergent and relational, built from the network of interactions
(\S\ref{sec:space-of-computations}). So the task is not to compute the energy of a flux tube stretched across a
pre-existing space; it is to show how a potential that \emph{depends on separation} arises at all
from a constraint that contains no separation.

The unification of the mass sector (below) fixes the form this answer must take. If, as the framework
holds, all mass is associator-geodesic length, then the interaction energy binding quarks --- which
is most of a hadron's mass --- is associator-geodesic length, and the confinement potential is that
length resolved by separation. The confinement potential and the mass map are therefore not
independent problems: the potential is what the geodesic length looks like when one asks how it
varies with the emergent distance between the charges.

\begin{openproblem}[The confinement potential]\label{op:confinement-potential}
Derive the static quark--antiquark potential --- equivalently the quantity lattice QCD and Regge
trajectories call the string tension --- as the associator-geodesic length of
Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic}, resolved by the emergent distance of
\S\ref{sec:space-of-computations}, and show that it reproduces asymptotic freedom at short range as the limit in
which little tripartite budget is yet committed. No flux tube or confining medium is posited; the
content is that the confining \emph{energy} is the same geodesic length that fixes mass, expressed as
a function of relational separation. The emergent-distance half of this engages the spacetime
programme of Paper~2 and is not required for the structural confinement result above.
\end{openproblem}

There is a further question the structural account raises but does not settle: \emph{where} the
confining energy resides. The monogamy obstruction shows that a colour singlet can host no external
correlation, but it does not, by itself, say whether the energy of binding is to be read as a
property of the quark triple's own tripartite entanglement or of the gluon Bell-pair network that
mediates the colour field between them. We commit to the position that there is no confining
\emph{medium} --- no background condensate doing the work, the entanglement being intrinsic and
relational rather than a property of the vacuum --- but that commitment does not decide between the
two intrinsic locations, and we do not exclude that the binding energy is carried by the gluon Bell
pairs rather than the quark triple.

The question can, however, be taken further than a stated commitment, because the framework's own
calculus supplies the vertex relating the two candidate loci, and computing with it settles the
form of the answer.

\begin{proposition}[The binding admits no invariant locus]\label{prop:energy-locus}
Let the closed baryon network be three Bell pairs on the triangle's edges with the register's
Frobenius fusion at each vertex (the wiring of Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-handles}). Before any
fusion the network's correlation is wholly pairwise: each edge at unit concurrence, every
cross-edge and external mutual information zero. After all three fusions it is wholly tripartite:
exactly $\mathrm{GHZ}_3$ at success probability $\tfrac14$, with three-tangle $1$ and every
pairwise concurrence zero. After a single fusion it is exactly $\mathrm{GHZ}_3 \otimes$ Bell at
success probability $\tfrac12$ --- the tripartite content appearing vertex by vertex as
contraction proceeds. The correlational locus therefore migrates with the presentation, and no
presentation-independent locus exists; what is invariant is the exclusion, the network admitting no
external colour correlation in any presentation. An executable verification of every statement
accompanies the code release.
\end{proposition}

The quark triple and the gluon network are two contraction orders of one closed tensor --- one
network, two coordinate systems, in exactly the sense of Open Problem~\ref{op:ideal-orbit}'s
pairing of module and entanglement coordinates --- so the question of \emph{which} carries the
binding dissolves, while what the physics needs survives in presentation-free form: the binding is
a property of the closed network as such, and its exclusion of external correlation ---
confinement --- holds in every presentation. The no-medium commitment is thereby strengthened: not
only is there no vacuum condensate doing the work, there is no privileged internal locus either.

\begin{openproblem}[Where the confining energy resides]\label{op:confinement-energy-locus}
Proposition~\ref{prop:energy-locus} resolves the correlational form of this question by
dissolution: quark triple and gluon network are presentations of one closed tensor, and only the
network as a whole carries the binding. What remains is the energy reading: confirm, once the mass
map of Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic} is in hand, that the associator debt attaches to
the presentation-invariant closed network rather than to any particular cut, so that the
dissolution survives the passage from correlation to energy. The binding remains in either case
intrinsic and relational, the property of no vacuum medium.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{Glueballs}

Glueballs are bound states of the colour field with no valence quarks: in the framework's language,
networks of gluon Bell pairs on $C$-qubits, closed on themselves so that the outer endpoints
contract to a colour singlet with no external fermion register to terminate on. They carry a subtlety
the quark sector does not, and it is the deepest point of the section.

A quark trio produces a \emph{classical} colour record for free, because the GHZ structure is special
Frobenius --- it possesses the copy operation that broadcasts a record (the \textsc{fanout} of
\S\ref{sec:entanglement-classes}, formalised in \S\ref{sec:fanout-boundary}). A single gluon cannot: its Bell pair is not a copy structure and
has nothing to broadcast. For a glueball to be a real, classically detectable bound state, the gluon
network must therefore \emph{manufacture} the copy structure through its closure topology, asking the
geometry of the network to do what a quark trio does intrinsically. Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-meson}
and its companion computation make this concrete, and the result is sharper than the question. The
two-gluon network does not, on its open legs, reconstruct the copy structure: it returns a Bell pair.
The closed three-gluon triangle returns exactly $\mathrm{GHZ}_3$, the copy structure itself. The
closure manufactures the record-broadcasting capacity that the constituents lack --- but it takes the
three-gluon triangle to do it, not the two-gluon scalar. The framework's reading is, in consequence,
that classically detectable glueball structure is tied to the three-gluon topology.

The three-gluon glueball carries a structural prediction that goes beyond standard QCD. Three gluons
joined as a triangle form a $K_3$ network --- the same complete graph on three vertices that is not
two-colourable, the obstruction that in \S\ref{sec:generations} generates the three fermion
generations. The four resolution classes of $K_3$ --- three frustrated classes and one symmetric ---
should therefore appear as four channel classes for three-gluon glueballs:

Standard QCD organises three-gluon singlets by two couplings, the symmetric $d^{abc}$ and the
antisymmetric $f^{abc}$. The framework predicts four channel classes where QCD sees two --- a
differential, lattice-checkable statement, and the same $K_3$ obstruction that gives the generations,
now in the colour field. That the obstruction reproduces a four-class structure independently in the
gluon sector is itself evidence that it is a genuine organising principle and not a coincidence of
the fermion counting. Two notes keep the parallel exact. The enumeration rests on the same
foundational assumption as the fermion count --- the pairwise-independent extension of the Filatov
constraint, Open Problem~\ref{op:filatov-extension} --- and inherits its status. And the labelling
convention differs benignly: the fermion table of \S\ref{sec:generations} names each class by its
odd vertex, this one by its frustrated edge, the two being complementary --- odd vertex $X$ is
frustrated edge the remaining pair.

The four-class prediction can be taken past assertion, because the framework's own vertex computes
it. Realising an unlike-oriented edge as the antisymmetric Bell state and contracting the triangle
with the same Frobenius fusion that binds the baryon yields three facts, each machine-verified.
First, the fusion enforces vertex consistency by itself: an edge pattern arises from some
orientation assignment exactly when its unlike-count is even --- the cycle parity of $K_3$ --- and
the fusion transmits precisely the even patterns while annihilating every other, so the contraction
already knows which networks are classes. Secondly, the four classes transmit, at equal probability
$\tfrac14$, to four mutually orthogonal $\mathrm{GHZ}$-type outputs --- the aligned pair for the
symmetric class and, for each frustrated class, the pair with the vertex opposite its aligned edge
flipped --- so the framework's vertex is class-resolving, the class read directly off the output's
parity pattern. Thirdly, the antiparticle member of each class transmits under a single antipode
placed on any edge, position-free, exactly as a global conjugation should behave. The dictionary to
the two QCD couplings then falls out. The symmetric class's particle--antiparticle pair corresponds
to the two cyclic orderings of the colour trace, whose even and odd parts are exactly the
couplings, $\mathrm{Tr}(T^aT^bT^c) \pm \mathrm{Tr}(T^aT^cT^b) = \tfrac12 d^{abc}$ and
$\tfrac{i}{2} f^{abc}$, verified against the explicit Gell-Mann matrices: QCD's two couplings are
the symmetric class read twice. The frustrated trio has no invariant-coupling counterpart at all
--- its content is \emph{which} edge is aligned, a datum every vertex-symmetric scalar projects
out and the orthogonal outputs retain --- which sharpens the falsifiable content of ``four where
two'': the extra channel triple is invisible to coupling space and separable exactly by
parity-resolved three-gluon correlators, which is what a lattice test should build.

\begin{openproblem}[The glueball spectrum]\label{op:glueball-spectrum}
Derive the three-gluon four-class spectrum above and the mass of the lightest ($J^{PC} = 0^{++}$)
glueball as the associator-geodesic length (Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic}) of the closed
$C$-only Bell network, the lattice value being $\approx 1.7\ \mathrm{GeV}$. The glueball mass shares
its origin with all other mass in the framework --- it is geodesic length --- but on a network living
entirely in the colour sector, with none of the hypercharge or weak-isospin activity that the fermion
masses carry, so the relevant geometry is that of the pure-colour closed network rather than the
mixed-fibre case of the charged fermions. The class half of this problem is discharged by the
dictionary above and its verification; what remains is the mass half, together with the lattice
formulation of the parity-resolved correlator that separates the frustrated trio.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{The taxonomy past the triangle}\label{sec:exotic-taxonomy}

Pentaquarks fit the framework by not being new particles at all. A quark is a GHZ register; a
hadron is a closed colour network among $C$-qubits; the hadron spectrum is therefore the taxonomy
of closed networks, and the baryon (the triangle, fusing to $\mathrm{GHZ}_3$ at success
$\tfrac14$) and the meson (the two-edge loop, closing onto the canonical Bell pair) are merely its
two minimal rows. A pentaquark --- four quarks and an antiquark closing to a colour singlet --- is
the first entry that forces the taxonomy past the minimal rows, and the live experimental question
about the observed states, which sit conspicuously close to baryon--meson thresholds
\autocite{lhcb2019pentaquark}, maps onto two distinct network topologies. The \emph{molecular}
picture is a product of two already-closed singlets, the triangle and the loop, each internally
complete; the \emph{compact} picture is one connected five-leg network --- and the compact wiring
needs something the baryon never did, an antisymmetric vertex, which the dictionary already
stocks: the W spider, the anti-special Frobenius structure behind the verified
$\mathrm{W} \circ \mathrm{W} = 0$ law. Ordinary hadrons wire only the GHZ spider; exotic hadrons
are where the second species enters hadronic wiring for the first time. The molecule-or-compact
question thereby becomes an entanglement-class dichotomy: is the contracted $C$-sector state
separable across the baryon--meson cut, or genuinely five-connected?

The budget theorem adjudicates what binding each picture is even allowed. In the molecular
topology both sub-singlets are saturated --- every qubit at one-to-rest tangle $1$ --- so by the
same monogamy that confines quarks and quantifies the leptonic ninth
(\S\ref{sec:budget-converse}), the colour-sector cross-budget between the two blocks is exactly
zero. Molecular binding therefore cannot be colour entanglement at all: it must live one level up,
in hadron-level channels, the next rung of the same relational recursion --- and a next-level
linkage is structurally weak, which is the right shape for what is observed, binding energies of a
few MeV and masses hugging the thresholds. We flag this as consonance, not derivation.

The rest is deferred, deliberately. The masses are network-geodesic targets one row past the
glueballs, owed under the widened Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic}; the first
computation is concrete and waiting in the machinery already built --- contract the compact
five-leg mixed-spider network, read its success probability and output class, and compare against
the molecular product, exactly as the glueball transmission table was constructed --- and the
tetraquark rides the identical story one row earlier. These belong to a dedicated short paper on
exotic hadrons as network topology. Here the taxonomy is opened, not walked.

\subsection{All mass is geodesic length}

The glueball mass is not a special case but an instance of a single principle the framework now
commits to: \emph{all} mass is associator-geodesic length. The masses of the individual quarks and
leptons (\S\ref{sec:generations}), the masses of glueballs, and --- the demanding case --- the masses
of hadrons are all the same geometric quantity, the length of the relevant closed network read
through the octonionic associator. One calibration keeps the commitment honest: the claim is that
all mass is one quantity --- the associator debt --- with the geodesic length as its computable
stand-in in the sense \S\ref{sec:masses} fixes (Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic},
\emph{Associator-debt and the geodesic proxy}); ``geodesic length'' below is shorthand for that
pairing, and the unification asserted here is exactly as strong as that problem's resolution. The demanding case is demanding because most of a hadron's mass is
not the mass of its constituents: the proton weighs about $938\ \mathrm{MeV}$, of which the
current-quark masses contribute some nine, the remainder being the energy of the colour binding. Under
the committed principle this binding energy must itself be associator-geodesic length --- the geodesic
length of the closed three-quark colour network --- of which the valence-quark contribution is a per
cent and the rest is the geometry of the binding. This is a sharp and falsifiable burden: the
framework claims the proton mass is the associator-geodesic length of the closed $uud$ network, a
claim that meets the one number lattice QCD has determined most precisely. It also explains why the
framework, which reproduces the charged-lepton masses to better than a per cent, does not yet produce
baryon masses: the obstacle is not the constituent masses but the binding geometry, and the binding is
where almost all of the mass lives. That single geometric quantity --- the associator-geodesic length
of Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic} --- now carries the entire mass sector, which is a real
localisation of the problem (mass is one thing, not many) with one large computation owed behind it.

One forward-looking consequence deserves a single sentence here and its proper treatment in Paper~2:
because mass is the gravitational charge, the claim that all mass --- including the binding energy that
is most of it --- is associator-geodesic length means that what gravitates is associator-geodesic
length, so that the equivalence of energy and mass and the coupling of mass to spacetime must both, in
the end, be statements about this one geometric quantity.

\subsection{The Yang--Mills mass gap}
\label{sec:mass-gap}

A reader who has followed this section to here will have noticed that its commitments walk up to
the edge of a famous problem, and it would be evasive not to name it. The Clay Millennium
formulation asks for two things \autocite{jaffewitten2006}: a mathematically rigorous construction
of quantum Yang--Mills theory on $\mathbb{R}^{4}$, for any compact simple gauge group, meeting the
Wightman axioms or standards of comparable stringency; and a proof that the constructed theory has
a mass gap $\Delta > 0$ --- that its lightest excitation is strictly massive. As physics the gap is
not in doubt: lattice computation exhibits it, and its lightest carrier is the scalar glueball near
$1.7$ GeV whose derivation this section has already recorded as
Open Problem~\ref{op:glueball-spectrum}. The Millennium problem is a problem of proof, not of
phenomenology. This section has asserted that confinement is exact and structural, that glueballs
are closed colour networks, and that all mass --- theirs included --- is associator-geodesic length.
Having gone that far, we owe the reader a plain statement of why we go no further, and of what
going further would require.

The reasons are the boundary already drawn for quantum electrodynamics in \S\ref{sec:interactions},
here made specific. The mass gap is a statement about the spectrum of a quantum dynamics, and this
framework has not constructed that dynamics: there is no Hamiltonian here whose spectrum could be
bounded, no Hilbert space of the field theory, and no continuum $\mathbb{R}^{4}$ to put one on ---
spacetime itself being emergent in this framework, its construction deferred
(\S\ref{sec:space-of-computations}). One cannot prove a spectral gap of an operator one has not
built. Beneath that lies the framework's own open centre: the map from network geometry to physical
mass is Open Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic}, and the statement ``every closed colour network
has strictly positive geodesic length'' cannot be a theorem before the functional it concerns
exists. Even were both supplied, the translation into the Millennium problem's terms crosses four
interfaces, each a research programme in its own right: the continuum construction of the emergent
spacetime; the translation of the categorical and entanglement-theoretic statements of this paper
into an axiomatic form; the passage from the algebraic obstruction of this section to the standard
criteria of confinement, the Wilson-loop area law above all; and the spectral proof itself. We claim
none of these. One further limit of scope deserves its own sentence: the Millennium problem is posed
for every compact simple gauge group, while the structural story of this section is specific to the
colour sector as it arises here, on the octonionic register --- our route, even if completed, would
speak to $SU(3)$, not to the problem in its full generality.

What the framework does supply, and what we record because it gives the gap a concrete identity, is
a structural reason to expect one. The photon is massless because the electroweak sector contains a
debt-free direction: the associator-blind $\mathbb{C}$, along which nothing is owed
(\S\ref{sec:masses}). The colour sector contains no such direction --- colour lives on the register
exactly where the associator is non-trivial --- so no closed colour network can be debt-free. And
closed networks are topologically discrete: the minimal closure is a finite structure, and there is
no continuum of ever-smaller closed networks by which geodesic lengths could accumulate towards
zero. If, as the framework's mass formulas everywhere suggest, the geodesic length of a closed
network is fixed by its discrete topology rather than by any continuously variable modulus, then the
spectrum of pure-colour states is bounded below by the length of the minimal closed network. On this
reading the gap exists because the colour sector has no massless direction and no arbitrarily small
closed structure; and $\Delta$ is then not an abstract bound but a particular number --- the mass of
the lightest glueball, precisely the object of Open Problem~\ref{op:glueball-spectrum}, with its
scale in physical units the object of Open Problem~\ref{op:qcd-scale}. Everything in this paragraph
is conditional on the open map: it is a heuristic the framework makes natural, not a theorem the
framework possesses, and we flag it as such.

Two forward directions are worth leaving on the table. The first reframes the problem's notorious
difficulty. Cubitt, P\'{e}rez-Garc\'{i}a and Wolf proved that the spectral-gap question is, in
general, undecidable: for translationally invariant lattice Hamiltonians it encodes the halting
problem, and their own statement of the result names the Yang--Mills gap conjecture as a particular
case of the general problem so shown undecidable \autocite{cubitt2015spectralgap}. For a framework
whose spine is self-referential non-computability this is orientation rather than discouragement.
It says that any proof must exploit special structure --- no general algorithmic route exists ---
and it locates the difficulty exactly where the bump-in-the-carpet principle of this framework
expects to find it. The suggestion we record, in the same register as the other prospects of this
paper --- a direction with a possible path, not a claim --- is that the special structure is the
division-algebra rigidity itself, the Hurwitz--Adams rigidity that terminated the Hopf tower, and
that a proof along this route would show the geodesic functional on closed colour networks to be
bounded below by the minimal-network length, the discreteness of network topology doing the work
that lattice regularisation does elsewhere. The second direction is nearer, and does not wait on the
emergent-distance machinery: in a fixed lattice geometry, the question of whether the
budget-saturation constraint of this section reproduces area-law Wilson loops is one that
tensor-network methods can address now, and it is the interface at which this framework's account of
confinement first becomes commensurable with the standard one. We leave the problem, and the route,
in the record of open problems rather than in the claims of this paper.

\subsection{Confinement and pre-spatial co-location}

A final reading ties confinement to the framework's account of space, and anticipates the fuller
treatment of \S\ref{sec:gr-nonlocality}. In that account entanglement is the pre-spatial relational
structure: entangled systems are not at distinct locations that some influence must bridge, but share
a relation that was never spatially separated to begin with, acquiring distinct locations only by
interacting with other systems that already hold locations in the network. Confinement is exactly
this seen in the colour sector. The three quarks of a singlet are maximally entangled, so in the
pre-spatial structure they are not separated; and the colour-singlet constraint, which forbids them
the external entanglement that location-acquisition requires, is precisely what prevents any one of
them from acquiring a distinct location of its own. A quark cannot be pulled to a place apart because,
in the only structure that is fundamental, it has no place apart to be pulled to, and the singlet will
not let it acquire one. The distance-free monogamy obstruction of this section and the pre-spatial
co-location of \S\ref{sec:gr-nonlocality} are one fact: confinement is the impossibility of giving a
colour charge a separate location while its colour entanglement is wholly spent on its partners.

\subsection{What is settled and what is owed}

Settled, and structural: confinement holds, as the saturation of the tripartite entanglement budget
by the colour singlet; it is exact rather than asymptotic; it is the same fact as the vector-like,
CP-conserving character of the colour force; glueballs are closed gluon networks that must manufacture
their own copy structure through closure, which ties classically detectable glueball states to the
three-gluon topology; and the three-gluon sector carries a four-class structure where standard QCD has
two couplings --- now computed rather than asserted: the framework's vertex enforces the $K_3$
cycle parity unaided, transmits the four classes to mutually orthogonal outputs, and locates both
QCD couplings inside the symmetric class, the frustrated trio being edge-position data
(Proposition~\ref{prop:energy-locus} and the dictionary of the glueball subsection, both
machine-verified). The binding's locus question is resolved by dissolution: one closed network, two
presentations. And the W class's unspent ninth is read as the converse
theorem --- unconfinement quantified, external pairwise correlation bounded by $\tfrac19$ per
qubit against the quark's exact zero --- with its boundary states and physical reading recorded as
Open Problem~\ref{op:w-slack}. Both sides of the budget now carry hardware witness: the GHZ register's
tomography returns collective tangle unity with pairwise concurrences zero, and the two-gluon
network's open legs measure as a Bell pair, not a copy ($\langle XX \rangle = 0.915$;
Appendix~\ref{app:hw-monogamy}, Appendix~\ref{app:hw-two-gluon}). The strongest counter-reading --- the Bell pair, saturated yet measurable ---
is answered at \S\ref{sec:bell-not-counterexample}: same budget, opposite sectors, and the access
ladder separates the cases.

Owed, and recorded as the open problems above: the distance-dependent confinement potential, as the
associator-geodesic length resolved by emergent distance (Open Problem~\ref{op:confinement-potential});
the location of the confining energy, quark triple or gluon network (Open
Problem~\ref{op:confinement-energy-locus}); and the glueball spectrum and lightest mass (Open
Problem~\ref{op:glueball-spectrum}). All three sit downstream of the single mass map, Open
Problem~\ref{op:associator-geodesic}, which the unification of this section makes responsible for the
whole of mass --- the fermion spectrum, the glueball spectrum, and the hadronic binding energy
alike. The Yang--Mills mass gap is named in \S\ref{sec:mass-gap} precisely so that no claim on it is
read into this section by implication.
