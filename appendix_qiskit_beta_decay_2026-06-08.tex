\section{A Quantum-Circuit Example}
\label{app:qiskit}

This appendix renders the constructions of \S\ref{sec:interactions} as runnable code. Both listings
have been validated on a statevector simulator and produce exactly the outputs stated; a companion
notebook with a hardware path accompanies the four executed computations of
Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-handles}. The first listing builds the beta-decay vertex of
\S\ref{sec:interactions}; the second is a direct
numerical check of the no-FCNC result of Proposition~\ref{prop:fcnc}, and rests only on linear
algebra.

\subsection{Beta decay, active quark}

A word first on what is drawn, because the picture is a deliberate projection and the framework's
register structure makes the full object considerably larger. Every fermion is a three-qubit
register $(A, B, C)$ --- hypercharge, weak isospin, colour (\S\ref{sec:register}) --- so the
complete process $n \to p + e^- + \bar\nu_e$ runs on five registers, fifteen qubits: the active
quark, two spectator quarks, the electron and the antineutrino. The charged-current ($W$) gate,
however, acts on the $B$-qubit alone; the ten $A$ and $C$ qubits across the five fermions are
spectators, untouched by any gate in the process. The listing below therefore draws only the active
sub-register of each participant --- the $B$-qubits the $W$ actually touches, plus the active
quark's own register --- and suppresses the rest. Concretely, qubits $0,1,2$ are the active quark's
$A,B,C$; qubits $3,4$ are the electron's and antineutrino's $B$-qubits, each the active wire of a
full lepton register whose $A$ and $C$ qubits are present in the singlet/spectator state and
omitted; and the two spectator quarks are omitted entirely. The truncation loses no physics
\emph{because} the suppressed wires are inert --- a claim made quantitative in the full-width
companion below, where all ten spectator qubits are shown to carry population exactly zero. A reader
meeting this circuit picture for the first time should read the full version first; once the
spectator structure is clear, the compact form is the honest shorthand.

The active quark's $B$-qubit is flipped (the $W^-$ emission, $d\to u$), and a vacuum lepton pair is
prepared as a Bell pair --- the bipartite shadow of the W-class lepton channels
(Appendix~\ref{app:w-proof}), drawn on the $B$-qubits alone.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
# Beta-decay charged-current vertex (validated: statevector, 2026-06-12).
# Compact form: active sub-registers only. d -> u + W-, then W- -> e- nu-bar.
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.quantum_info import Statevector

# qubit 0 = A (hypercharge), 1 = B (weak isospin), 2 = C (colour)
# qubit 3 = electron B-qubit, 4 = antineutrino B-qubit (from vacuum)
qc = QuantumCircuit(5)

# Vertex 1:  d -> u + W-   (charged current = T3 raise on the quark B-qubit;
# Y = +1/3 is the doublet invariant, so A is untouched -- the charge change
# Delta-Q = +1 IS the T3 raise, carried off by the W-)
qc.x(1)                 # |0> (d, T3=-1/2)  ->  |1> (u, T3=+1/2)

# Vertex 2:  W- -> e- + nu-bar   (Bell pair on the lepton B-qubits --
# the bipartite shadow of the W-class lepton channels)
qc.h(3)
qc.cx(3, 4)             # (|00> + |11>)/sqrt(2) on the lepton B-qubits

# A (hypercharge) and C (colour) of the quark are spectators: untouched,
# so hypercharge, colour and the binding colour-singlet are preserved.
sv = Statevector(qc)
print(sv.draw("text"))
# Verified: the quark B-qubit (q1) is flipped to 1 -- the up-quark
# weak-isospin slot -- and the lepton qubits (q3,q4) are maximally
# entangled.  In Qiskit's little-endian order |q4 q3 q2 q1 q0>, the
# nonzero amplitudes are |00010> and |11010>, each 1/sqrt(2).
\end{lstlisting}

\paragraph{The full-width object.} At full register width the same decay is fifteen wires --- five
$(A,B,C)$ registers --- with the $W$ still touching only the three $B$-qubits it acts on. The output
remains \emph{exactly two} basis states: the extra twelve wires add no outcomes precisely because no
gate touches them. Running this (companion notebook
\texttt{notebook\_E\_full\_beta\_decay.ipynb}) and reading the marginal of every wire confirms the
conservation laws register by register: the active quark's $B$-qubit at population $1$ (the up
slot), both spectator quarks' $B$-qubits at $0$ (still down), the two lepton $B$-qubits maximally
mixed and mutually Bell-correlated, and --- the point of drawing it --- all ten $A$ and $C$ qubits
at population exactly $0$. The charge balances as $u(+\tfrac23) + e^-(-1) + \bar\nu(0) = -\tfrac13 =
d$, carried entirely by the single $B$-qubit raise; the colour $C$-qubits, untouched, preserve the
binding singlet; baryon and lepton number hold because three quark registers enter and leave and the
lepton registers are created as a correlated pair. The compact listing above is this object with its
ten inert wires erased.

\subsection{The no-FCNC check}

Proposition~\ref{prop:fcnc} says a neutral-current coupling proportional to the identity in the
generation index stays diagonal under the CKM rotation, $V^\dagger\mathbb{1}V=\mathbb{1}$, so no
flavour change is induced; a coupling that instead \emph{read} the generation index would not. The
following confirms both, for a random CKM-like unitary on three generations, using only NumPy.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Python]
# No-FCNC check (Proposition: V^dag D V).  Requires numpy only.
import numpy as np
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)

# A random CKM-like unitary on 3 generations (QR of a complex Gaussian):
M = rng.standard_normal((3, 3)) + 1j * rng.standard_normal((3, 3))
V, _ = np.linalg.qr(M)

# Neutral current proportional to the identity in generation space:
D = np.eye(3)
NC_mass = V.conj().T @ D @ V
print("flavour-diagonal:", np.allclose(NC_mass, np.eye(3)))      # True

# Contrast: a coupling that reads the generation index would induce FCNC.
D_bad = np.diag([1.0, 1.0, 2.0])
NC_bad = V.conj().T @ D_bad @ V
off_diag = NC_bad - np.diag(np.diag(NC_bad))
print("FCNC present:", not np.allclose(off_diag, 0))             # True
\end{lstlisting}

The first print is \texttt{True}: a generation-blind neutral current is flavour-diagonal in the mass
basis, reproducing Proposition~\ref{prop:fcnc}. The second is \texttt{True} only because $D_{\mathrm{bad}}$
distinguishes generations --- exactly the dependence the framework forbids the neutral bosons from
having, since the generation index is the triality bracketing and no gauge gate acts on it.

\emph{Hardware record.} The beta-decay circuit was run on \texttt{ibm\_fez} (13 June 2026, 09:30
UTC, 8192 shots, SamplerV2), the shallowest of the suite's circuits and correspondingly the
cleanest. The quark landed in the up slot with probability $0.9954$, the lepton pair's
$Z$-correlation was $0.9827$, and its coherence $\langle XX\rangle = +0.948$ --- a lepton-pair Bell
fidelity near $0.97$, with the leakage spread over ten outcome strings at the per-cent level (the
dominant pair being the single-flip neighbours of the signal). The conservation laws written into
the wiring above are thus reproduced on hardware to within a few per cent. This circuit is the
natural entry point to the executable suite of Appendix~\ref{app:qiskit-handles}, where the same
$T_3$ raise gains a generation register and the output amplitudes become the CKM factors.
