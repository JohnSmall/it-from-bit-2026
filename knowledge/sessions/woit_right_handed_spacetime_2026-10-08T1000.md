## 0. The answer in brief

Woit's claim is more specific than "spacetime is chiral". It is that in
Euclidean signature the local symmetry $\mathrm{Spin}(4)=SU(2)\times SU(2)$
should be split asymmetrically: one factor, after analytic continuation,
is the Lorentz group and so is spacetime; the other is an internal
symmetry; the split is made by a choice of imaginary-time direction, which
he identifies with the Higgs degree of freedom, and the twistor fibre is
where that choice lives. The framework has the same group theory already,
in the two-qubit Hopf model, and it is verified here: at a point of the
Hopf base $S^{4}$ the isotropy group is $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$, the twistor
fibre over the point is the sphere of complex structures on its tangent
space, choosing one breaks $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ to $U(2)=U(1)\times SU(2)$,
with the $SU(2)$ the factor that commutes with the chosen $i$ and the
$U(1)$ the one generated by it. That $U(2)$ is Woit's electroweak group at
a point and it is the paper's hypercharge-on-the-$U(1)$-fibre,
weak-$SU(2)$-on-the-$Sp(1)$-fibre assignment seen from the base. Which
factor is "spacetime" and which is "internal" is, in the two-qubit model,
which factor is unitary for the chosen complex structure, and the two
choices are exchanged by conjugating one qubit, which is the partial
transpose, which is the reversal of Filatov's handedness.

JS's arrow should be turned round, or rather replaced by a common cause.
In Woit the chirality of spacetime explains the chirality of the weak force
(the internal $SU(2)$ acts on the spinors the Lorentz group does not use).
In the framework both descend from one selection, the amplitude's $i$,
which the record makes: the complex structure the records are written in
fixes which $Sp(1)$ is unitary, hence which is gauge (weak) and which is
spacetime. So it is not that the weak force's chirality is imposed on the
relations and hence on spacetime; it is that the relations' complex
structure, selected by the record, makes both the weak force and spacetime
chiral at once, in opposite senses. And the non-unitary chirality, the
"spacetime" one in the two-qubit model, is generated by the phase and the
spin flip, which is the time-reversal operator of a spin-$\tfrac12$. The
sentence the framework can own is: *spacetime is the chirality that contains
time reversal, and the weak force acts in the other.* Whether the record
selects the left or the right factor is `op:chirality` and is not settled by
any of this.

## 1. What Woit claims [from the abstracts; bodies not read]

*Euclidean twistor unification* (arXiv:2104.05099, April 2021). Euclidean
spacetime with local $\mathrm{Spin}(4)=SU(2)\times SU(2)$ taken as
fundamental; gauging one $SU(2)$ gives a chiral spin-connection form of
general relativity, gauging the other gives part of the Standard Model
gauge fields; "reconstructing a Lorentz signature theory requires
introducing a degree of freedom specifying the imaginary time direction,
which will play the role of the Higgs field"; twistor geometry supplies
"tautological spinor degrees of freedom", the analytic continuation between
Euclidean and Minkowski spinors, internal $U(1)$ and $SU(3)$ symmetries, and
one generation of matter; the theory is to be defined on projective twistor
space.

*Spacetime is right-handed* (arXiv:2311.00608, November 2023). Vectors and
spinors in complex spacetime described "in an unconventional chirally
asymmetric manner, using purely right-handed spinors, with Minkowski
spacetime getting Wick rotated to a four-dimensional Euclidean spacetime
with a distinguished direction"; self-dual two-forms give chiral
formulations of the Yang--Mills and gravitational actions; "Euclidean
spacetime left-handed spinors then transform under an internal $SU(2)$
symmetry, rather than the usual $SU(2)_{L}$ spacetime symmetry related by
analytic continuation to the Lorentz group $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$."

The arXiv author listing (2026-10-08) shows two further papers in the same
line, "Notes on the Twistor $\mathbf{P}^{1}$" (arXiv:2202.02657, February
2022) and "Is Space-time Really Doomed?" (arXiv:2204.02225, April 2022),
neither read; no journal references are recorded for any of the four.

So "spacetime is right-handed" means: the Lorentz group is the
complexification of $SU(2)_{R}$ alone; $SU(2)_{L}$ does not act on
spacetime and is free to be the weak isospin; the distinguished Euclidean
direction (imaginary time, the Higgs) is what breaks the symmetry between
the two. The chirality of the weak interaction is then that the internal
$SU(2)$ is the factor spacetime does not use.

## 2. The same group theory in the two-qubit Hopf model [STRUCTURAL; verified]

The two-qubit state space $S^{7}\subset\mathbb{H}^{2}$ carries three
commuting-or-not actions of $Sp(1)$ (script parts 1 and 2):

- $F$, the structure group of the quaternionic Hopf bundle, right
  multiplication $(q_{1},q_{2})\mapsto(q_{1}g,q_{2}g)$: the fibre; in the
  standard complex structure the local $SU(2)$ of the second qubit.
- $A\times D$, the isotropy group $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ of the base point
  $x=[1:0]$ of $S^{4}=\mathbb{HP}^{1}$: block-wise left multiplication
  $(q_{1},q_{2})\mapsto(aq_{1},dq_{2})$, acting on the chart
  $w=q_{2}q_{1}^{-1}$ of the tangent space $T_{x}S^{4}=\mathbb{H}$ as
  $w\mapsto d\,w\,\bar a$. This is Woit's $SU(2)\times SU(2)$ at a point of
  Euclidean spacetime, with $D$ acting by left and $A$ by right
  multiplication on the tangent space, i.e. $D$ self-dual and $A$
  anti-self-dual for the orientation $(1,i,j,k)$ (2026-10-07, part A).

$F$ commutes with both isotropy factors, and they commute with each other.
The complex structures on $T_{x}$ compatible with the orientation are the
left multiplications $L_{n}$, $n$ a unit imaginary quaternion; this sphere
is the twistor fibre over $x$. Part 3: $D$ rotates them,
$L_{n}\mapsto L_{dn\bar d}$, $A$ fixes every one of them, and the stabiliser
of a chosen $L_{n}$ in $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ is $U(1)_{D}(n)\times Sp(1)_{A}$,
which is $U(2)$. That is Woit's electroweak group at a point: the unbroken
$SU(2)$ is the isotropy factor that commutes with the chosen complex
structure, and the $U(1)$ is the one generated by it. In the paper's
assignment (`sec:charges`) hypercharge lives on the $U(1)$ fibre and the
weak $SU(2)$ on the $Sp(1)$ fibre; the same two groups, read from the base.

Part 5 adds Woit's "distinguished direction": a unit vector $v$ of
$T_{x}=\mathbb{H}$ (a Euclidean time axis) determines the complex structure
$L_{v i\bar v}$, and $v\mapsto vi\bar v$ is the Hopf map $S^{3}\to S^{2}$ with
$U(1)$ fibre, so a complex structure fixes the time axis up to a phase. The
choice of imaginary-time direction and the choice of a point in the twistor
fibre are the same choice up to that phase. Woit says this choice "will
play the role of the Higgs field"; the paper says the Higgs is "the
dynamical promotion of that preferred direction" (`sec:associator-higgs`,
crediting Szangolies). The two sentences are the same sentence.

## 3. Which chirality is spacetime: the choice of $i$ decides, and it is the handedness [STRUCTURAL; verified]

Part 4 is the point. Which of the three $Sp(1)$ actions is complex-linear,
hence unitary, depends on which complex structure on $\mathbb{H}^{2}$ is
taken as *the* complex structure of the quantum theory:

| complex structure | unitary | not unitary |
|---|---|---|
| left $i$ (the standard two-qubit phase) | $F$, the fibre, local $SU(2)$ of qubit 2 | the isotropy $A\times D$ (each factor's $j$ is a block spin flip) |
| right $i$ (the twistor packing, coordinates $\psi'=(\alpha,\bar\beta,\gamma,\bar\delta)$) | the isotropy $A\times D$ | $F$ |

and partial conjugation of qubit~2 intertwines the two structures exactly,
$K_{2}L_{i}K_{2}=R_{i}$. Woit's reading uses the second row: the complex
structure is the point chosen in the twistor fibre, the isotropy
$\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ is the symmetry of spacetime-at-a-point and is unitary,
and the split of it into "the factor that commutes with $i$" (internal,
weak) and "the factor that contains $i$" (broken to $U(1)$; in his
analytic continuation the one that becomes Lorentz) is the split of the
first column of the table by the chosen $i$. The paper's Hopf tower uses
the first row: the standard phase is $i$, the fibre is the unitary local
group. The two rows are the two Filatov handednesses (2026-10-07, section
9): choosing which row one is in is choosing which of the pair's two
spheres is the inverted one. So in the two-qubit model, "spacetime is
right-handed" and "the second sphere is the left-handed one" are the same
choice, and the choice is which complex structure the state is written in.

What the framework adds to Woit is a reason for the choice. The complex
structure is the amplitude's $i$, which is selected by the record: it is
the structure in which the real column (records, Landauer) is distinguished
from the imaginary column (phase, action) (two-columns note, 2026-09-18;
`lem:cproj`). Once records are written in a given $i$, the $Sp(1)$ that
commutes with it is the unitary gauge group and the $Sp(1)$ that contains it
is not a group of physical operations: its $U(1)$ is the phase and its $j$
is the antiunitary spin flip (part 6), which is the time-reversal operator
of a spin-$\tfrac12$, $T^{2}=-1$. So the "spacetime" chirality, in the
two-qubit model, is the one whose non-trivial elements are time reversal.
That is a sharper statement than "spacetime is chiral": spacetime is the
chirality that contains $T$, and the weak force acts in the one that does
not. It is consistent with the framework's reading that the Lorentz group's
non-compact part (boosts) is not unitary either: in the spacetime section
boosts are the two-chart description of measurement, and in Chin and Lee
2014 and Avron and Kenneth 2009 the local $SL(2,\mathbb{C})$ on one qubit is
filtering, not a symmetry. The chirality that becomes spacetime is the
non-unitary one in both of its parts.

## 4. JS's arrow, corrected [CANDIDATE]

JS: the weak force is chiral, therefore the relations are chiral, therefore
the inferred spacetime is chiral. In Woit the arrow runs the other way:
spacetime uses one chirality, so the other is internal, so the weak force
is chiral. In the framework neither arrow is primary; both chiralities are
read off one selection:

1. The record selects a complex structure $i$ (the amplitude's $i$,
   `lem:cproj`; the direction in which FANOUT writes).
2. At every point of the inferred spacetime the isotropy
   $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ splits by $i$ into the factor that commutes with it
   and the factor that contains it (part 3).
3. The factor that commutes with $i$ is unitary and is the weak $SU(2)$,
   with the $U(1)$ generated by $i$ as hypercharge: Woit's $U(2)$, the
   paper's fibres.
4. The factor that contains $i$ is not unitary; its elements are the phase
   and time reversal; after complexification it is the Lorentz group:
   spacetime.

So the weak force is chiral and spacetime is chiral for the same reason,
and the senses are opposite by construction: each is the chirality the
other does not use. "The weak force enforces chirality on the relations"
is true in the weak sense that both are the record's choice of $i$ seen
twice. The framework's distinctive claim, which Woit does not make, is step
1: the choice is not a Higgs field or a distinguished direction put in by
hand but the record itself, so parity violation and the arrow of time have
one origin. That is a CANDIDATE reading, and it is the framework's own.

## 8. JS's reconciliation: the two arrows are the two charts (added 2026-10-08T1300)

JS, on section 4: the spacetime of special relativity is ontological and
substantivalist, existing independently of its contents with a static
causal structure; the spacetime of general relativity is epistemological
and relationalist, its causal structure dynamic and inferred from events;
the framework treats the two as charts, each appropriate in its domain; so
Woit's "spacetime is chiral" is the substantivalist chart and "spacetime's
chirality is inferred from the weak force's" is the relationalist chart,
"different views of the same thing".

Agreed, and the computation of section 3 says exactly what the two charts
are and what connects them, so the reconciliation can be made precise
rather than left as a stance.

*The charts are the two packings.* In the twistor packing the isotropy
$\mathrm{Spin}(4)$ of a point of the base is the unitary group: one starts
from spacetime-at-a-point with its rotation group given, chooses a point
in the twistor fibre, and the weak $SU(2)$ is the factor that commutes with
it. That is Woit's construction and it is the substantivalist chart: the
base is there first, with its tangent space and its orientation, and the
fields are sorted by it. In the standard packing the fibre, the state's
own local unitary group, is the unitary group: one starts from the
relations (the two-qubit state and its local operations), the record fixes
$i$, and the base point with its isotropy is inferred. That is the paper's
construction and it is the relationalist chart. Section 3's table is the
two charts side by side, and the fact that partial conjugation of one
qubit maps one packing to the other is the statement that the chart change
is not a physical operation (it is antiunitary on one subsystem), which is
what "chart" should mean: a change of description, not a transformation
of the thing. The chart-invariant content is the split of $\mathrm{Spin}(4)$
by $i$ into the commuting factor and the containing factor, with its
stabiliser $U(2)$; the direction in which one reads the split, from the
base to the fibre or from the fibre to the base, is the chart. So "Woit's
arrow" and "JS's arrow" are both right, each in its chart, and the common
cause of section 4 is what is invariant between them.

*A precision on the labels.* "Special relativity is substantivalist,
general relativity is relationalist" is a simplification the paper should
not commit to in print. Minkowski space is a fixed background, which is
what the substantivalist chart needs, but the substantivalism debate in
general relativity (the hole argument, which `sec:relationalism` cites
through Rovelli) is exactly a debate about whether general relativity is
relational, and manifold substantivalists read it otherwise. The safer
pairing is "fixed-background chart" versus "relational chart": in the
first the causal structure is given and static and the chirality of
spacetime is an input that sorts the fields; in the second the causal
structure is inferred from records and the chirality of spacetime is read
off the chirality of the records. The framework's claim is then the one
`sec:relationalism` already makes, that the relational chart is the
fundamental one because distances are not pre-computable, with the
fixed-background chart valid wherever the records have locked; and the
present note's addition is that the chirality transfers between the charts
by the same rule as everything else, through the record's $i$.

*What the relational chart adds that the substantival one cannot.* In the
fixed-background chart the chirality of spacetime is a brute fact (Woit
puts in the distinguished direction by hand and names it the Higgs). In
the relational chart it is inferred, so it can be explained: the records
are written by interactions, the chiral interaction writes chiral records,
and the inferred spacetime inherits the complementary chirality. That is
JS's arrow, and it is the one with explanatory content. Its cost is the
one named in section 5: it needs the record to select a definite $i$, and
which $i$ (left or right) is `op:chirality`.

Proposed wording, for `sec:relationalism` or the paragraph after the
$SL(2,\mathbb{A})$ correspondence, JS to rule: "The chirality of spacetime
is read in two charts. In the fixed-background chart it is an input, the
choice of which $SU(2)$ factor of the Euclidean rotation group is spacetime
and which is internal, and it sorts the fields into the weak doublet and
the rest \autocite{woit2023righthanded}; in the relational chart it is
inferred, the records written by the chiral interaction fixing the complex

One distinction the computation forces, which the paper's wording does not
yet draw. "Selects a definite $i$" has a discrete part and a continuous
part. The continuous part is which complex structure *within a chirality*:
the point $n$ of the twistor fibre $S^{2}$ (or of $S^{6}$ at the octonionic

$\mathbb{C}\subset\mathbb{O}$ of `sec:fanout-direction`. Changing it moves

discrete part is whether the chosen structure is of left type or right
type, self-dual or anti-self-dual for the given orientation, which in the
two-qubit model is the standard packing versus the twistor packing, i.e.
the handedness of the pair. Only the discrete part decides which factor is
spacetime and which is the weak force. The paper's candidate is a claim
about the continuous part: a copy fires in a basis and commits a complex
line. Whether a copy also commits the type, so that the record-making
network has one handedness, is not claimed anywhere, and it is exactly

propagates down the Cayley--Dickson tower to select $SU(2)_{L}$ over
$SU(2)_{R}$"). Woit's two inputs are the same two: the chiral asymmetry in
identifying vectors with spinors (discrete, "spacetime is right-handed")
and the distinguished Euclidean direction (continuous, the Higgs). He puts
both in by hand; the paper derives the second from the record as a
candidate and leaves the first open.
