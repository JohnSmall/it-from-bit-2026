Your memory of the content is exactly right, though. In that paper Dirac introduced additional intermediate states carrying negative norm, whose contributions could cancel in loop diagrams and render the theory finite. This is the origin of the *indefinite-metric* programme. And the negative-probability-versus-greater-than-one point you make is precisely the equivalence Dirac was exploiting: he framed it as negative-norm "hypothetical" states, but since the probabilities must still sum to one, a negative probability for one outcome is a greater-than-one probability for its complement.
