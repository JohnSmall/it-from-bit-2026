**What the argument actually is**

Spencer-Brown's central move is a structural homology that he states more cleanly in the 1972 American preface than anywhere else. He pairs two paradoxes:

- The Liar: "this statement is false," which under {true, false} forces oscillation
- The arithmetical "paradox" *x = -1/x*, which under {+, 0, -} forces oscillation between +1 and -1

He notes that mathematics resolved the second by introducing a fourth class of value (imaginary) and proposes the same move for logic — a fourth class beyond {true, false, meaningless}. Chapter 11 then constructs it. He distinguishes E2 (*f = ⌐⌐f⌐*, which has marked/unmarked solutions) from E3 (*f = ⌐f*, which has none), declares E3's solution an *imaginary state*, and then immediately identifies this imaginary state with **time** — because oscillation requires duration. From there he gets frequency, velocity, and (in the Notes to Ch. 11) explicitly "a remarkable and striking precursor of the wave properties of material particles."

That is, in 1969, in 77 pages of body text, he proposes: *self-reference + finite signal speed → oscillator → wave → matter.* That's the whole arc.
