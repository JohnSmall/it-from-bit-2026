**Date:** Tuesday 28 July 2026, 15:00 BST
**Present:** Bertrand Russell (BR), George Spencer-Brown (GSB), Karl Svozil (KS), Terence Tao (TT)
**Note-taker:** Claude

*Note-taker's disclaimer: chronology has been suspended for the occasion. Positions are reconstructed from each speaker's published work; all wording is the note-taker's paraphrase, not verbatim quotation.*

**1. Opening position (BR)**

BR opened by restating the problem as he first met it in 1901: the class of all classes that are not members of themselves. His diagnosis, the vicious circle principle — whatever involves all of a collection must not itself be one of the collection — and his remedy, the theory of types, which prohibits the reflexive loop by stratifying the universe into levels. He conceded the costs frankly: the axiom of reducibility, the awkwardness of ramification, the sense of legislating mathematics rather than discovering it. But the moral, he insisted, stands: when a definition doubles back on itself, one's first suspect should be the definition, not the logic. He asked the room to keep two things separate throughout: harmless syntactic loops (a sentence describing its own word count) and impredicative quantification over a totality containing the quantifying item — which is where the trouble actually lives.

**2. Re-entry and the imaginary value (GSB)**

GSB shared his screen and drew a single mark. Distinction, he said, is the primitive act; everything else is bookkeeping. Equations of the first degree in his calculus recover Boolean algebra. The interesting case is the second degree: *re-entry*, where an expression appears inside its own indicative space. The canonical instance: x = not-x. In the two-valued world there is no solution. Two honest responses exist — prohibit the equation (BR's route) or extend the value space. Mathematics, he reminded the room, faced x² + 1 = 0 and chose extension; the denounced "imaginary" numbers proved consistent and then indispensable. He proposes the same for logic: an imaginary truth value, whose solution to x = not-x is an *oscillation*. Time enters logic as the dimension in which contradiction becomes alternation.

His witness is engineering, not philosophy: he and his brother wired precisely such loops into pulse-counting circuits for British Railways. The paradox, built in hardware, does not explode. It either latches — the flip-flop, which is memory — or ticks — the oscillator, which is a clock. Memory and time, he suggested, are what paradox looks like from the inside.

BR asked whether the theory of types wasn't secretly the same manoeuvre. GSB agreed cheerfully: ramified orders are a construction schedule; the theory of types is frozen time. He recalled that BR had been generous about the book on publication. BR confirmed the compliment but reserved the right to qualifications (see item 5).

**3. The view from inside physics (KS)**

KS, joining from Vienna, took the operational line. Start with Moore's 1956 Gedanken-experiments: even a classical finite automaton, probed by an experimenter denied reset access, exhibits complementarity — measurement disturbs, and complete state knowledge is unobtainable. The resulting empirical logic is a *partition logic*: Boolean blocks pasted together along shared observables, structurally the same pasting one finds in quantum logic's Greechie diagrams. Non-Boolean logic therefore does not require quanta; it requires only an observer stripped of the external vantage point.

He then sharpened the theme: the intrinsic versus extrinsic observer, in the lineage of Boskovich and Rössler's endophysics. Breuer's theorem — no observer can perform perfectly accurate measurements on a system containing themselves — is an impossibility result with an unmistakably diagonal flavour, anticipated in Popper's 1950 argument that a predictor cannot outrun its own prediction. Kochen–Specker then shows there is no global two-valued truth assignment across all measurement contexts; his own work with Abbott and Calude localises this value indefiniteness sharply enough to certify quantum randomness.

His summary: physics answers the vicious circle neither by prohibition nor by oscillation but by *partiality*. Truth lives on Boolean patches — contexts — and the complex amplitude calculus is the bookkeeping that interpolates coherently between patches. He cautioned the room against jumping the Boolean fence into metaphysics.

On GSB's oscillator he registered one reservation: the quantum square root of NOT is not a timetable but a superposition. The i in a phase is not a schedule. He held the point for TT.

**4. A common schema, and why ℂ (TT)**

TT offered the unifying template: the no-self-defeating-object argument. Cantor, Russell's paradox, Berry, Gödel, Turing, even the grandfather paradox — a single argument shape: assume the total object, feed it itself, derive the flip. There are exactly two lawful escapes. Restriction: declare the offending object nonexistent (proper classes, no halting oracle, type ceilings). Extension: enlarge the value space until the fixed-point equation acquires solutions — ℝ to ℂ; a theory to the theory plus its own consistency statement, iterated through Turing's ordinal logics, with Gödel's second theorem guaranteeing the extension never closes. GSB's imaginary value, he observed, is simply route two applied to Boolean algebra.

He then posted, in the meeting chat, what he called the crisp fact of the afternoon: no classical stochastic matrix squares to NOT — a two-line verification — yet the unitary square root of NOT exists, with complex entries. ℂ is the minimal arena in which negation acquires a square root.

On KS's phase-versus-timetable objection, he suggested Fourier duality reconciles the two readings: an oscillation watched in time is a fixed phase in frequency. Kauffman's iterant construction makes GSB's intuition exact — i as the waveform [1, −1] composed with a unit time-shift; squaring aligns the waveform against its own negation, yielding −1.

As to why ℂ in physics more broadly, he listed the evidence with care: algebraic closure, so spectra exist; Stone's theorem, where a continuous one-parameter symmetry group demands an i to hand its generator over as a self-adjoint observable — and Stueckelberg showed real quantum mechanics must smuggle in an operator J with J² = −1 regardless; Solèr's theorem narrowing the field to ℝ, ℂ, ℍ; local tomography and the recent network experiments cutting the real case. One caution, characteristically: the schema transfers from logic to physics only across an explicit encoding — universality is the vehicle, not vibes. His own fluids programme is the constructive test: engineering a self-replicating machine into an ideal fluid to force blow-up. Whether the equations are Gödelian, he said, is a theorem-shaped question, not a mood.

**5. Exchange**

BR pressed GSB: a truth value indexed by t is truth relativised; the law of contradiction survives only per-instant. Is this still logic, or is it circuitry? GSB replied that the identical objection was raised against √−1 until Argand gave it geometry; circuitry gives his value its geometry. And logic was never disembodied — a mark must be made by someone, somewhere; the observer is inside the form. KS sided with both: keep the formalism operational and leave the ontic-versus-epistemic status of the amplitude deliberately undecided. TT observed that set theory long ago conceded GSB's structural point: the cumulative hierarchy dissolves Russell's paradox by an ordinal index of stages. Read the index as *level* and you have BR; read it as *time* and you have GSB. The mathematics is indifferent; the interpretation is not.

BR closed by allowing that his objection was to haste rather than to imagination, and that the calculus retains the curious elegance he had remarked on at publication — though elegance, he added, has misled logicians before.

**6. Points of agreement**

The diagonal obstruction is structural and recurrent across logic, computation, and measurement. Three response types were identified — prohibit (stratify), extend (new values), partialise (contexts) — and each pays with an index: level, time, or context. ℂ is the minimal extension in which x = not-x is internalised as phase; equivalently, the smallest arena where NOT has a square root.

**7. Points of disagreement (recorded, unresolved)**

BR: truth is not a dynamical quantity. GSB: the distinction between logic and its embodiment cannot be sustained. KS: interpretive questions are to remain open by design. TT: claims beyond explicit encodings are conjecture — respectable, provided they are labelled as such.

**Date:** Tuesday 4 August 2026, 15:00 BST
**Present:** Bertrand Russell (BR), George Spencer-Brown (GSB), Karl Svozil (KS), Terence Tao (TT), David Deutsch (DD, guest)
**Note-taker:** Claude
**Apologies:** S. Hawking (invitation sent after the meeting, per his standard protocol for time travellers; no reply received, which he regards as data)

*Note-taker's disclaimer as before: chronology suspended, positions reconstructed from published work, all wording the note-taker's paraphrase.*

**1. Matters arising**

Actions from the previous session discharged: GSB circulated Chapter 11 and a flip-flop schematic; TT posted his note on the two escapes from the diagonal; KS distributed the Moore, Breuer, and value-indefiniteness references. BR reported he had reread Chapter 11 with charity and without indulgence, and that both survived the experience. The proposed topic — whether the index of stratification is time — was declared open, and BR welcomed DD, observing that a discussion of temporal stratification could hardly proceed without the man who asked what happens when the stratification closes into a ring.

**2. Guest presentation, part I: the square root of NOT (DD)**

DD opened with his long-standing thesis: the theory of computation is a branch of physics, not of mathematics. Which logical operations exist is an empirical question. Exhibit A is the √NOT gate — a physical device which, applied twice, deterministically negates a bit, but applied once produces a state admitting no classical description whatever. Classical logic proves no such single-valued operation can exist (TT's stochastic no-go from last session is exactly that proof); the laboratory builds one before lunch. The moral is not that logic is wrong but that logic inherits its warrant from physics. The intermediate state is not ignorance of 0-or-1; interference on the second application shows it is something classical probability cannot be. He noted with approval that the previous session had converged on ℂ as the minimal arena for this — his gloss being that the arena was never optional, because the physics got there first.

**3. Guest presentation, part II: quantum mechanics near closed timelike lines (DD)**

DD then presented his 1991 framework. General relativity permits CTC solutions — Gödel's rotating universe chief among them — so the honest question is not whether to forbid them but what quantum mechanics says if they exist. His model: a chronology-respecting system meets a chronology-violating one; a unitary U couples them; consistency demands the state entering the loop equal the state leaving it — a fixed-point condition on density matrices. His central theorem: a fixed point *always exists*, because the map is continuous on a compact convex set. The classical grandfather paradox is the statement that the bit-space {0,1} contains no fixed point of negation. The quantum resolution is not a prohibition but an enlargement: for the paradox circuit the consistency condition forces the maximally mixed state. The traveller is born with probability one-half, prevented with probability one-half, and the loop is consistent.

Two supplements. First, where fixed points are non-unique, he selects the one of maximal entropy. Second, the interpretation is unapologetically Everettian: the mixture is not ignorance but a multiverse fact — the traveller emerges into a different branch, and consistency is carried by the multiverse as a whole rather than strangling any single history, as Novikov's principle must. Finally the *knowledge paradox*, which he regards as the deeper one: a proof received from one's own future and then sent back has, on a single-history reading, no provenance — complex information created from nothing, violating the evolutionary principle that knowledge arises only from processes that create it. The multiverse discharges the debt: the proof is genuinely constructed somewhere, in some branch, by ordinary labour.

**4. Exchange**

GSB claimed the guest's theorem as a homecoming. The CTC is re-entry incarnate — the expression entering its own temporal space — and the grandfather paradox is x = not-x posed around a ring. He offered the engineer's dichotomy: a ring of an even number of inverters is bistable — memory, two fixed points; a ring of an odd number has none, and so oscillates — a clock. His imaginary value was the odd ring's confession. But on a true CTC there is no external time in which to oscillate, and DD's mixture is precisely what remains: the time-average of his oscillation, ½ and ½, frozen into the state. He proposed, with proprietary satisfaction, that the mixed state is the oscillation seen from nowhere.

TT formalised. Existence of the fixed point is Brouwer–Schauder; the interesting pathologies are non-uniqueness (the even ring) and the two distinct nonlinearities — the map's dependence on the external state, and the entropy rule that breaks ties. He noted the entropy rule has a striking consequence: among the even ring's fixed points it selects the mixture, so the loop retains no record of *which* consistent history obtains — the loop forgets. On computation: Aaronson–Watrous show DD's model computes PSPACE, and classical CTCs do too — the loop is a great equaliser, since nature hands over fixed points that are PSPACE-hard to find. He then posed the sharpest open question: Bennett, Leung, Smith and Smolin argue the claimed superpowers — PSPACE, distinguishing non-orthogonal states, cloning — dissolve once one insists the nonlinear box act linearly on ensembles of unknown states. The entire dispute, he said, reduces to whether the loop receives the individual state or the mixture, and the formalism as stated does not decide. In the chat he posted a small computation: the loop whose single traversal is √NOT has as its consistent states exactly the equal superpositions — the eigenforms of the loop are precisely the states classical logic denies exist.

DD replied to the BLSS point directly: the objection conflates epistemic mixtures with physical states. In the Everett picture each branch presents the loop with its actual, pure, relative state; linearity-on-ensembles is an operational scruple, not a law of nature. He and TT agreed to disagree in decision-shaped form (see actions). To TT's free-lunch worry he answered in kind: across the multiverse the computation *is* performed — the work is distributed, not conjured.

KS entered on the operational flank. The consistency condition is a view from outside the loop; by Breuer's theorem no embedded observer can verify it, so its status for an intrinsic observer is exactly the status of a global truth assignment in Kochen–Specker — a fiction of the external vantage. He recalled von Foerster: objects as tokens for eigenbehaviours. DD's fixed point is an eigenform of recursive observation, and the Viennese cybernetics tradition had the schema, if not the density matrices. His own preference: chronology protection *by consistency* — only self-consistent histories occur, no agency required — and sustained scepticism toward superpower claims, for BLSS-flavoured reasons. He added the Euclidean observation: thermal states are periodic in imaginary time; a CTC is periodic in real time; the trace around the loop is formally a partition function at imaginary temperature, which is Hawking's 1995 route to decoherence near CTCs. He cautioned, firmly, against confusing imaginary temperature with negative temperature — the latter is an inverted population and real; the former is a periodicity and is not a thermometer reading.

BR spoke last. Hawking's chronology protection conjecture, he observed, is the theory of types for spacetime — the universe stratifying its own causal order, the vicious circle principle geometrised — and he noted the exquisite irony that Gödel, having shown arithmetic cannot close upon itself, promptly showed spacetime might. His objection to DD parallels his objection to GSB last session, promoted from time to modality: a probability of being born is not a fact of being born; the fixed point trades truth for expectation. And when DD answers that within each branch facts are definite, BR replied that the multiverse is then a ramified hierarchy by another name — branches are levels; stratification has not been eliminated but rehoused, at considerable metaphysical rent. **1. Theorem (recorded).** No real 2×2 matrix $M$ satisfies $M^2 = X$, where $X = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Proof: write $M = \begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$, so $M^2 = \begin{pmatrix}a^2+bc & b(a+d)\\ c(a+d) & bc+d^2\end{pmatrix}$. The diagonal conditions give $a^2 = d^2$; the case $d=-a$ is excluded, so $d = a$ and $a^2 + \tfrac{1}{4a^2} = 0$, i.e. $4a^4 = -1$ — no real solution. Over ℂ: $a = \tfrac{1+i}{2}$, yielding the standard gate $\tfrac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}1+i&1-i\\1-i&1+i\end{pmatrix}$. ∎

A* 87, 022114 (2013) — and note the acknowledgements: they thank Jim Hartle and Fay Dowker for useful conversations. Your supervisor was in the room when this was being worked out, and Halliwell is at the Blackett. This is very much the right paper to be reminded of, because it supplies exactly the precision that last turn's diagonality predicate was missing — and it slightly sharpens what our Step 3 must claim.

Their central move is a dichotomy. Quasi-probabilities fall into two qualitatively different types according to whether their non-negative marginals can or cannot be matched to a non-negative probability: the former they call viable, the latter non-viable. For a viable one, a positive probability exists and the negativity merely means the wrong formula was picked — there exists a correction term preserving the positive marginals that restores positivity. For a non-viable one, it is not consistent to assert that underlying quantities possess values at all — no hidden-variable joint exists. And the certificate is Fine's theorem: the eight CHSH inequalities are not merely necessary but sufficient for the existence of a joint probability matching the four given marginals, with non-viability of more general quasi-probabilities provable by coarse-graining down to a Bell or CHSH instance and checking the inequalities there.

Their worked example is the one to remember. For the EPRB singlet they take the quasi-probability built as the real part of a chain of non-commuting projectors — the real part of a Kirkwood–Dirac-type object, which confirms the KD thread from last turn — and show that with coplanar detector settings there are whole regions around theta = pi/2 and 3*pi/2 where the CHSH inequalities are satisfied yet the quasi-probability is negative: an underlying probability exists, but it is not the one the Goldstein–Page formula supplies, since linear positivity fails. Negativity of a *given* formula certifies nothing; the invariant is whether *any* joint exists.

That is the refinement our apparatus needs, in three places.
