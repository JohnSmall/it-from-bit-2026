# A boundary: what gravitates, when nothing has been recorded

Date: 2026-09-21T1300. Fable session, at JS's ruling: "I think this can be
marked as a genuine boundary where the framework might fail to be able to
make any predictions and therefore needs more work to understand." The
"this" is the gravitation of matter that engages no gauge force, raised in
`sphaleron_slocc_collective_conversion_2026-09-20T2000.md` sections 18 and

(`particle_zoo_section_2026-06-08.tex`) and as the deferred dark-matter

## 0. The boundary in one paragraph

The framework says gravity is the bookkeeping of locked relations. Dark
matter is five times the baryonic mass and has left, since its production,
essentially no non-gravitational record. So either the framework's gravity
reads something other than gauge-made records, or it under-counts the
universe's mass by a factor of six. The papers give three answers and do not
agree between themselves. Paper 1's dark-matter prospect says participation
in the metric is universal, "carried by anything with a worldline and a
tick-rate", which in this thread's vocabulary is the imaginary column, the
phase rate. Paper 2's abstract says gravity lives in the post-FANOUT regime,
the real column, the records. The thread's own answer is a third: energy and
momentum are conserved charges, so the record made at production persists
and unrecorded matter gravitates as a delocalised source at the resolution
of its records. Until the count-density dictionary of Paper 2 says which
column gravity reads, the framework makes no prediction about dark matter,
and it should say so in those words. That is the boundary.

## 1. What this thread settled [STRUCTURAL as standard physics; the readings CANDIDATE]

- The sphaleron is not a channel for steriles: the 't Hooft vertex touches
  only left-handed doublets (sphaleron note, section 18).
- The channel a sterile does have is its Yukawa, the Higgs vertex. With
  seesaw-size coupling at 3.6 TeV the channel thermalises at $T \sim M$
  ($K \approx 47$) and decays in $8\times10^{-16}$ s (section 20, Part A and B):
  recorded at birth and death, and not dark matter. A stable relic must have a
  coupling far below seesaw size and is then recorded only at its
  production, by mixing, at an early epoch.
- Energy-momentum is a conserved charge and therefore a record of the kind
  that survives (high-temperature note, section 2): the recoil of production
  partners holds a particle's energy and momentum for as long as conservation
  holds, which is for ever.
- Between events a particle's position is not a fact, so its field below the
  resolution of its records is not a fact; it gravitates as a delocalised
  source. This is Paper 2's "gravity follows the resolution, not the average"
  applied to an unresolved source, and it matches halo phenomenology.
- A species with no event at all is not in the network, has no gravity, and
  has no testable existence: Mach's position, not a defect.

## 2. The fork [the disagreement is ESTABLISHED as a reading of the two papers]

| answer | what gravitates | where it is said | what it predicts for dark matter | what kills it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) records only | energy that has been written into a gauge or non-gauge record | Paper 2, "gravity lives in the post-FANOUT regime", read strictly | dark matter with no records after production does not gravitate | the dark-matter fraction, $\Omega_{\rm DM}/\Omega_{b} \approx 5.4$ |
| (b) the tick-rate | the phase rate, mass as self-relation, loop-counting "universal" | Paper 1, dark-matter prospect; op:sterile-gravity's second direction | anything with a worldline gravitates, recorded or not | contradicts Paper 2's regime statement; a phase sourcing gravity is pre-FANOUT gravity |
| (c) conserved energy-momentum as a persisting record | the production record, carried forward by conservation; delocalised between events | this thread (sphaleron note, section 20) | dark matter gravitates as a smooth source; no localised field per particle until resolved | none known; but it adds nothing to general relativity at the halo level, so it predicts nothing new there |

(b) and (c) are observationally equivalent for anything that exists, since
anything that exists was produced; they differ in bookkeeping, which column
gravity reads, and that is exactly what Paper 2's count-density dictionary
must decide. (a) is refuted as stated. The framework's honest position today
is that it has not chosen, and until it has, "what gravitates" is not a
prediction of the framework but an assumption borrowed from general
relativity.

## 3. The restated open problem [OPEN, proposed; drafted as an unrun patch]

op:sterile-gravity, retitled "The sterile channel and relational gravity: a
boundary", keeping its label and its three candidate directions, now named
as the fork above with what the thread established about each, and given
criteria. PASS: the count-density dictionary states which column gravity
reads; reproduces general relativity's coupling to $\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle$
for recorded matter; reproduces the dark-matter fraction and the smoothness
of halos; and reconciles Paper 1's "loop-counting is universal" with Paper
2's "gravity is post-FANOUT" in one sentence both papers can carry. FAIL: a
dictionary on which only gauge-recorded matter counts (refuted by the
dark-matter fraction); or the two papers left answering differently; or no
dictionary, in which case the framework makes no prediction about dark
matter and the text says so. The draft is in
`scripts/patch_op_sterile_gravity_boundary_2026-09-21T1300.py` (`--check`

deferred dark-matter paragraph naming the boundary. JS ruled 2026-09-23:

files), uncommitted, unbuilt; labels checked.

## 4. For Paper 2

The count-density dictionary (op:p2-coefficient, op:p2-chart) must say
which column it counts. The thread's recommendation, for what it is worth:
(c), because it keeps gravity post-FANOUT as Paper 2's abstract says,
explains dark matter with nothing beyond conservation, and yields the one
statement with any novelty, that unrecorded matter has no localised field.
If (b) is chosen instead, Paper 2's abstract must change. Either way the
"Dark matter as relational solitons at galactic scale" companion named in
Paper 1's summary inherits this boundary as its first problem.
