# The electroweak unification theorem

*The four generators of SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y act on K₃-structured states with a natural hierarchy: two rotate between K₃ resolutions (become W±), one distinguishes resolutions without rotating between them (T₃), and one is fully K₃-blind (Y). The Higgs VEV selects a preferred direction in the (T₃, Y) plane, leaving orthogonal the unique linear combination that is both K₃-blind and VEV-invariant: the photon. The Weinberg angle sin²θ_W = 1/4 emerges as the fibre-dimension ratio dim(S¹)/(dim(S¹) + dim(S³)). The three massive electroweak bosons and the massless photon arise from a single structural principle — the hierarchy of engagement with the K₃ resolution structure and the Higgs VEV.*

### 1.1 What we are unifying

The Standard Model electroweak sector has four gauge bosons: W⁺, W⁻, Z, and γ (photon). Three are massive; one is massless. The mixing between U(1)_Y and SU(2)_L is parametrised by the Weinberg angle sin²θ_W ≈ 0.231, which the framework predicts as 1/4 at the bare tree level.

The goal of this document is to derive, from K₃ structure alone:

1. Why there are three massive electroweak bosons and one massless photon.
2. Why the photon is the specific combination Q = T₃ + Y/2 (not some other direction).
3. Why the Weinberg angle takes the value sin²θ_W = 1/4.
4. Why SU(2)_L and U(1)_Y unify into a single electroweak sector before symmetry breaking.

## 2. The electroweak algebra as a 4-dimensional K₃-structure-acting object

### 2.1 The four generators

The electroweak algebra 𝔰𝔲(2)_L ⊕ 𝔲(1)_Y has four generators:

- **T_+, T_−**: raising and lowering operators of SU(2)_L. Rotate α ↔ β in the frustrated-pair resolution.
- **T_3**: diagonal generator of SU(2)_L. Eigenvalues ±1/2 on the two resolutions, 0 on singlets.
- **Y**: traceless phase rotation on 𝕆. Eigenvalues −1 on the ℂ sector (leptons), +1/3 on the ℂ³ sector (quarks).

These act on the tripartite qubit system (A, B, C) with its K₃ handedness structure. The algebra is the direct sum 𝔰𝔲(2)_L ⊕ 𝔲(1)_Y; before electroweak symmetry breaking, the gauge group is SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y (modulo a ℤ_2 centre in the full SM embedding with SU(3)_C).

### 2.2 Each generator's action on K₃ structure

The critical observation is that these four generators do not act uniformly on the K₃ resolution structure — they split into three distinct types of engagement:

**Type I: K₃-rotating.** T_+ and T_− take a state with Resolution α into a state with Resolution β (and vice versa). They change the K₃ resolution. In terms of T_3 eigenvalue, they shift T_3 by ±1.

**Type II: K₃-diagonal.** T_3 acts with eigenvalue +1/2 on Resolution β and −1/2 on Resolution α. It distinguishes the two resolutions but does not mix them. It is diagonal in the resolution basis.

**Type III: K₃-blind.** Y acts by phase rotation on the whole 𝕆-valued state, with eigenvalue depending only on which sector (ℂ or ℂ³) the state is in — not on the resolution. It commutes with the entire K₃ structure.

Three types of engagement with K₃, for four generators (two of Type I, one of Type II, one of Type III). This partition will drive the mass spectrum of the electroweak bosons after symmetry breaking.

### 2.3 What "K₃-blind" means precisely

A generator G is **K₃-blind** if:

(a) G does not change which K₃ class (1, 2, 3, or 4) a state belongs to.
(b) G does not rotate between Resolutions α and β within a class.

Both T_3 and Y satisfy these conditions. The difference between them is that T_3 is aware of the resolution (it has different eigenvalues on α and β), while Y is completely insensitive to it (its eigenvalue depends only on whether the state is in ℂ or ℂ³).

This distinction matters because after electroweak symmetry breaking, the Higgs VEV selects a direction in the two-dimensional space spanned by T_3 and Y. The surviving U(1)_EM is generated by the linear combination *orthogonal* to the VEV direction in this plane. And because of the fibre-dimension weighting that distinguishes the SU(2)_L and U(1)_Y gauge couplings, the "orthogonal" direction takes a specific form: Q = T_3 + Y/2.

## 3. The Higgs as the dynamical preferred complex direction

### 3.1 Higgs structure before SSB

In the framework, the Higgs is identified with the dynamical preferred complex direction on 𝕆 (following Finkelstein-Jauch-Schiminovich-Speiser; Szangolies 2025). Before SSB, this direction is not fixed — the Higgs field takes values in the full 𝕆 sphere, and the computability split is not yet selected.

Under the SM electroweak group, the Higgs is an SU(2)_L doublet with Y = +1:

- Upper component (T_3 = +1/2): charged, Q = +1/2 + 1/2 = +1.
- Lower component (T_3 = −1/2): neutral, Q = −1/2 + 1/2 = 0.

In K₃ language: the Higgs doublet has the two-component structure of a K₃ frustrated-pair resolution, with the same α/β labelling as fermion doublets. But because the Higgs is a scalar, its "resolution" is a field-theoretic degree of freedom, not a fermion state — it can be *superposed* across α and β and take a VEV along any linear combination.

### 3.2 The VEV selects a direction

The Higgs potential ∝ λ (|φ|² − v²/2)² is minimised on the sphere |φ|² = v²/2 in the four-real-dimensional doublet space. The VEV spontaneously picks a direction on this sphere.

By convention, the VEV is chosen in the neutral component:

$$\langle \varphi \rangle = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ v/\sqrt{2} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad T_3 \langle \varphi \rangle = -\tfrac{1}{2} \langle \varphi \rangle, \qquad Y \langle \varphi \rangle = +1 \langle \varphi \rangle.$$

The structurally meaningful statement is not which specific component is selected (that's a gauge choice), but rather: the VEV is a unit vector in the (T_3, Y) plane, with specific eigenvalues under both generators.

### 3.3 What the VEV breaks and what it preserves

A gauge generator G leaves the VEV invariant iff G⟨φ⟩ = 0. For the Higgs VEV above:

- **T_+⟨φ⟩ = v/√2 (upper component)**: nonzero. T_+ is broken.
- **T_−⟨φ⟩ = 0**: the VEV is annihilated by T_−. But T_+ ≠ 0 and the SU(2)_L algebra requires T_+ and T_− to be broken together (they are raising and lowering of the same generator pair). Both are broken in the sense that their symmetry is spontaneously broken.
- **T_3⟨φ⟩ = −(1/2)⟨φ⟩**: nonzero eigenvalue. T_3 is broken (the VEV is not T_3-invariant).
- **Y⟨φ⟩ = +1·⟨φ⟩**: nonzero eigenvalue. Y is broken.

Naively, all four generators are broken. But a linear combination of T_3 and Y can be unbroken:

$$Q \langle \varphi \rangle \;=\; (T_3 + Y/2) \langle \varphi \rangle \;=\; (-\tfrac{1}{2} + \tfrac{1}{2}) \langle \varphi \rangle \;=\; 0.$$

This is the Gell-Mann-Nishijima relation: Q = T_3 + Y/2 is the generator that annihilates the Higgs VEV, hence the unbroken U(1)_EM charge.

## 4. The photon as the K₃-blind, VEV-invariant direction

### 4.1 The two conditions on the photon

The photon generator Q satisfies two conditions:

1. **K₃-blind**: Q does not rotate between α and β resolutions. This eliminates T_+ and T_−.
2. **VEV-invariant**: Q annihilates the Higgs VEV. This fixes the specific combination T_3 + Y/2 rather than, say, T_3 − Y/2 or T_3 alone or Y alone.

The first condition restricts the photon to the two-dimensional K₃-blind subspace spanned by T_3 and Y. The second condition picks out the specific line Q = T_3 + Y/2 within that plane.

Neither condition alone is sufficient. Together, they uniquely determine Q.

### 4.2 Why this is structural, not conventional

In the Standard Model, Q = T_3 + Y/2 is often stated as a *definition* (the Gell-Mann-Nishijima relation) or derived from the Higgs doublet's specific Y = +1 assignment. In the framework, both the Higgs Y = +1 and the photon being the K₃-blind + VEV-invariant direction are consequences of:

- The W → SU(2)_L doublet structure (which makes the Higgs an SU(2)_L doublet natural)
- The U(1)_Y tracelessness over 𝕆 (which fixes lepton Y = −1, and hence Higgs Y = +1 from Yukawa coupling requirements)
- The selection of the neutral component for the VEV (a gauge choice)

Given these upstream structural results, Q = T_3 + Y/2 is derived. It is not a convention.

### 4.3 The Z boson: K₃-blind but VEV-sensitive

The direction in the (T_3, Y) plane orthogonal to Q (with appropriate coupling weights) is the Z boson:

$$Z \;=\; \cos\theta_W \, T_3 \;-\; \sin\theta_W \, (Y/2).$$

Z is K₃-blind (it is a linear combination of T_3 and Y, neither of which rotates resolutions). But Z is not VEV-invariant — it does not annihilate the Higgs VEV:

$$Z \langle \varphi \rangle \;=\; \bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}\cos\theta_W - \tfrac{1}{2}\sin\theta_W \bigr) \langle \varphi \rangle \;\neq\; 0.$$

Therefore Z is broken by the Higgs VEV and acquires mass. Its mass scales with the Higgs VEV and the SU(2)_L coupling, giving the familiar M_Z = v·√(g² + g'²)/2 relation.

### 4.4 The W± bosons: K₃-rotating and VEV-sensitive

W± are identified with the raising and lowering generators T±. These are K₃-rotating (they shift T_3 by ±1, taking α ↔ β). They are also VEV-sensitive (they do not annihilate the Higgs VEV). Both features contribute to their mass: the mass M_W = g·v/2 reflects the cost of rotating between K₃ resolutions *and* the fact that this rotation acts non-trivially on the VEV direction.

### 4.5 The hierarchy of K₃ engagement and mass

| Boson | K₃-rotating? | VEV-invariant? | Mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| γ (photon) | No | Yes | **0** |
| Z | No | No | ~ v · √(g² + g'²)/2 |
| W± | Yes | No | ~ v · g/2 |

The mass hierarchy M_W ≤ M_Z (with M_W = M_Z · cos θ_W) is structural: the Z is "less broken" than the W± because it is K₃-blind, whereas W± are both K₃-rotating and VEV-sensitive.

**This is the framework's unified account of why there are three massive electroweak bosons and one massless photon.** It is not put in by hand — it follows from the two-dimensional structure of K₃ engagement (rotating / blind) crossed with the two-dimensional structure of VEV engagement (invariant / sensitive). Of the 2 × 2 = 4 possible combinations, only three are realised: (no, yes) = photon, (no, no) = Z, (yes, no) = W±. The fourth — (yes, yes) — would be a K₃-rotating and VEV-invariant direction, but no such direction exists in the electroweak algebra (K₃-rotating forces the generator to be in the T± sector, which is not VEV-invariant).

## 5. Deriving sin²θ_W = 1/4 from K₃ fibre structure

### 5.1 The fibre-dimension argument

The Weinberg angle is defined by the ratio of gauge couplings:

$$\tan\theta_W \;=\; g'/g, \qquad \sin^2\theta_W \;=\; \frac{g'^2}{g^2 + g'^2}.$$

In the framework, the tree-level gauge couplings are determined by the fibre geometry of the underlying Hopf bundles. Specifically, the coupling-squared is proportional to the dimension of the gauge group manifold:

- SU(2)_L: group manifold S³, dimension 3. Coupling g² ∝ 3.
- U(1)_Y: group manifold S¹, dimension 1. Coupling g'² ∝ 1.

The justification for this scaling: in the absence of any distinguishing feature, the metric on the electroweak fibre treats all Lie algebra directions on the same footing. The "volume of parallel transport" available to a gauge group scales linearly with the number of Lie algebra generators, which equals the fibre dimension for simple compact groups.

With this scaling:

$$\sin^2\theta_W \;=\; \frac{1}{1 + 3} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{4}.$$

This is the bare tree-level value, to be run down via SM RGE to the measured value 0.231 at M_Z. The matching scale is ~3.7 TeV (see `boson_mass_fibre_geometry.md`).

### 5.2 K₃ reading of the 1/4

In K₃ language, the four electroweak generators split as (T_+, T_−, T_3, Y), with the first three forming SU(2)_L and the fourth forming U(1)_Y. The sin²θ_W = 1/4 has a direct structural reading:

$$\sin^2\theta_W \;=\; \frac{\text{dim(U(1)}_Y)}{\text{dim(U(1)}_Y) + \text{dim(SU(2)}_L)} \;=\; \frac{1}{1+3} \;=\; \tfrac{1}{4}.$$

Equivalently: 1/4 of the electroweak algebra consists of the fully-K₃-blind generator (Y), and 3/4 consists of K₃-engaging generators (T_+, T_−, T_3). The Weinberg angle is the rotation that takes the algebra-natural basis (T_3, Y) into the physical basis (Z, photon), with the rotation angle determined by the relative "sizes" of the two subspaces.

### 5.3 Why the K₃ reading is sharper than the naive reading

The "debt-free direction" reading of sin²θ_W = 1/4 (one photon out of four generators) is correct but abstract. The K₃ reading adds structural content:

- It identifies *which* direction is the photon (the K₃-blind + VEV-invariant one), not just that one exists.
- It explains *why* the photon is K₃-blind (because K₃-rotating directions are W±, which are VEV-sensitive hence massive).
- It connects the mixing angle to the dimensional structure of the K₃ resolution space (1D phase + 3D resolution rotation = 4D total).

The numerical value 1/4 is unchanged, but its structural meaning is sharpened: sin²θ_W measures the fraction of electroweak algebra that is fully decoupled from the K₃ resolution structure. The photon is the gauge boson of K₃-blindness.

## 6. The electroweak bundle before SSB

### 6.1 Pre-SSB picture

Before electroweak symmetry breaking, the electroweak gauge group SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y acts on the combined A-qubit + B-qubit structure. The A-qubit carries the U(1)_Y phase; the B-qubit carries the SU(2)_L frustrated-pair rotation. The combined group acts on the ℂ × ℂ² = ℂ³ space spanned by the two qubits (in the biquaternion representation).

Before SSB, the electroweak algebra has four massless gauge bosons: W_a (a = 1, 2, 3) and B_μ. All are massless because there is no Higgs VEV to fix a direction.

### 6.2 The Higgs VEV as computability-split trigger

The Higgs VEV selects the preferred complex direction on 𝕆 — the same selection that defines the computability split 𝕆 = ℂ ⊕ ℂ³. This is not a coincidence: the Higgs field IS the dynamical preferred direction, and its VEV IS the moment when the computability split becomes fixed.

This means **electroweak symmetry breaking and the computability split are the same event**. Before SSB, there is no preferred complex direction, no ℂ ⊕ ℂ³ decomposition, no SU(3)_C as a definite subgroup. After SSB, all three are fixed simultaneously.

### 6.3 Consequences for the unification

This has an important structural consequence: SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y is not merely "two gauge groups that happen to mix at low energy." It is a single electroweak object whose internal structure is determined by the K₃ resolution plus the phase structure, and which decomposes into its familiar SM components only after the Higgs VEV fixes the preferred direction.

Before SSB:
- 4 electroweak generators
- K₃ resolution structure exists but is not yet coupled to a specific direction
- All 4 gauge bosons are massless

After SSB:
- Higgs VEV fixes preferred direction → computability split → ℂ ⊕ ℂ³ decomposition
- K₃-blind + VEV-invariant = photon (massless)
- K₃-blind + VEV-sensitive = Z (massive)
- K₃-rotating + VEV-sensitive = W± (massive)
- SU(3)_C emerges as G₂ stabiliser of the preferred direction

This is the framework's unified electroweak story: one object (EW algebra acting on K₃-structured qubits), one symmetry-breaking event (Higgs VEV = computability split), three massive bosons + one massless photon + emergent SU(3)_C colour.

## 7. Corollaries and connections to other theorems

### 7.1 Photon masslessness is topologically protected

The photon is the unique K₃-blind + VEV-invariant direction. Both conditions are topological:

- K₃-blindness is a property of the generator relative to the K₃ class structure, which is itself topological (from the non-2-colourability of K₃).
- VEV-invariance, after SSB, is a property of the generator relative to the fixed direction in 𝕆.

Neither condition depends on the detailed dynamics of the Higgs potential, only on the existence of the VEV and the K₃ class structure. The photon's masslessness is therefore protected not just by SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y gauge invariance (the standard SM argument) but also by the K₃ class structure. The photon couldn't acquire mass without either breaking the K₃ class structure (which would change the generation count) or failing to be VEV-invariant (which would mean Gell-Mann-Nishijima Q = T_3 + Y/2 is wrong).

### 7.2 The W boson's role as entanglement-transfer operator

The framework has previously described the W boson as the "entanglement transfer" operator (see `session_summary_2026_04_02.md`). This document sharpens that description: W± rotate the K₃ resolution (α ↔ β), which *is* the operation of transferring entanglement between the two members of the weak doublet. The structural content of "W transfers entanglement" is precisely "W± are K₃-rotating generators."

The SU(2)_L rotation within a K₃ resolution is also the rotation that changes T_3 by ±1 — the flavour-changing operation that takes e_L to ν_L, d_L to u_L, and so on. These are the same operation in different languages: changing K₃ resolution = changing weak flavour = entanglement transfer.

### 7.3 The Z boson's structural role

The Z boson is K₃-blind but VEV-sensitive. This corresponds to its SM role as the neutral current: the Z mediates interactions that do *not* change flavour (it is diagonal in the doublet basis) but do couple to the Higgs VEV (it has a weight-dependent coupling proportional to T_3 − Q·sin²θ_W for each fermion).

In the framework, the Z's coupling to a fermion f is proportional to T_3(f) − Q(f)·sin²θ_W = T_3(f) − (T_3(f) + Y(f)/2)·sin²θ_W. At tree level with sin²θ_W = 1/4, this gives (3/4)·T_3(f) − (1/8)·Y(f), which is the tree-level Z coupling structure. The specific 3/4 and 1/8 coefficients are pure consequences of sin²θ_W = 1/4 and the particle's T_3 and Y values — both derived from the K₃ structure.

### 7.4 ℤ_6 quotient of SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1)

The true SM gauge group is SU(3)_C × SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y / ℤ_6, where the ℤ_6 = ℤ_3 × ℤ_2 quotient enforces charge quantisation. In the framework:

- ℤ_3 comes from the centre of SU(3)_C = G₂ stabiliser of the preferred ℂ direction. It is the Z_3 triality of the Fano plane acting on the three colour directions in ℂ³.
- ℤ_2 comes from the centre of SU(2)_L. It is the ℤ_2 flipping of the frustrated-pair resolution (α ↔ α, β ↔ β up to sign).

The quotient is automatic from the octonionic structure (Krasnov 2021). In K₃ language: the K₃ class structure is invariant under global sign flips of handedness (ℤ_2) and under permutations of the three frustrated-pair constituents (ℤ_3 in Class 1-3). These symmetries are the origin of the ℤ_6 quotient.

## 8. What the derivation accomplishes and what remains open

### 8.1 What's derived

- **Mass hierarchy of EW bosons**: three massive (W±, Z), one massless (γ), from the K₃-engagement × VEV-engagement structure.
- **Photon direction**: Q = T_3 + Y/2 is uniquely determined as the K₃-blind + VEV-invariant direction.
- **Weinberg angle sin²θ_W = 1/4**: tree-level value from the fibre-dimension ratio, structurally reading as dim(U(1)_Y)/dim(EW algebra).
- **EW + computability split unification**: Higgs VEV = computability split trigger, single event for SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y breaking and SU(3)_C emergence.
- **Photon masslessness protection**: both gauge-invariance and K₃ class structure protect the photon from acquiring mass.

### 8.2 What's assumed

- **Fibre-dimension scaling of gauge couplings** (g² ∝ dim(group)): structurally motivated by "no preferred direction in the fibre metric," but the explicit derivation from the Hopf bundle metric is not written up in detail.
- **Higgs VEV in the neutral component**: gauge choice, not derived.
- **Higgs Y = +1 assignment**: forced by Yukawa-coupling consistency with the fermion Y values from `hypercharge_derivation.md`, but not independently derived.

### 8.3 What's open

- **Explicit Higgs Y derivation from Yukawa structure**: could in principle be derived by running the Y-tracelessness argument with the Higgs as a participant in the Yukawa coupling.
- **Derivation of the Higgs quartic λ = 1/8**: connected to the curvature of the computability-split potential, flagged as open in `boson_mass_fibre_geometry.md`.
- **ℤ_6 quotient as K₃ centre structure**: structurally plausible but not rigorously written up.

None of these would change the electroweak unification conclusion. They are gaps in *why* specific parameter values are what they are, not in the structural identification of photon/Z/W± roles.

## 9. Summary

> The four generators of the electroweak algebra SU(2)_L × U(1)_Y act on K₃-structured states with three distinct modes of engagement: T± rotate between K₃ resolutions, T_3 distinguishes resolutions diagonally, and Y is fully K₃-blind. The Higgs VEV — identified as the dynamical preferred complex direction on 𝕆 — simultaneously triggers electroweak symmetry breaking and fixes the computability split. After the VEV, the unbroken U(1)_EM is generated by the unique direction that is both K₃-blind (not rotating between resolutions) and VEV-invariant (annihilating the Higgs VEV): Q = T_3 + Y/2. The three broken generators become massive: W± are K₃-rotating and VEV-sensitive (mass ~ v·g/2), Z is K₃-blind but VEV-sensitive (mass ~ v·√(g²+g'²)/2), and the photon is K₃-blind and VEV-invariant (massless). The Weinberg angle sin²θ_W = 1/4 at tree level emerges from the fibre-dimension ratio dim(S¹)/(dim(S¹)+dim(S³)), with structural reading as the fraction of EW algebra that is fully K₃-blind. Electroweak unification is thus a single story: the K₃ class structure plus the Higgs computability-split event jointly determine every electroweak observable.
