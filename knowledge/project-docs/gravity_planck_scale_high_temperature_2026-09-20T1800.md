## 0. The answer in brief

Three separate things are being asked about, and they fail at three
different places.

The Einstein equation is the equation of state of the record fluid, and it
fails where local equilibrium fails. That is not "at high temperature": the
horizon's entropy is vacuum correlation across the surface, independent of
the bath to leading order, and the thermal correction is of relative size
$T/M_{P}$, which is $10^{-17}$ at the electroweak crossover and $10^{-16}$ at
the framework's 3.6 TeV scale. The equation of state holds at every
temperature the framework owns and fails only within a factor of order one
of the Planck temperature, where the Hubble horizon holds of order one
record and there is nothing to average. So the premise is wrong for all
temperatures but one.

Gravity does not fail when its equation of state fails, any more than a gas
ceases to exist when the ideal-gas law does. What persists off equilibrium
is the substrate, records forming and dissipating, with a viscous dynamics
at first (Chirco--Liberati) and with no geometry at all when nothing locks.
"Gravity" in this framework is the bookkeeping of locked relations; where
there are none it has no subject. That is not a law failing but the absence
of what the law is about.

The Planck scale makes sense, with its meaning changed. It is the record's
grain, the area per locked relation, a property of the substrate like the
spacing of molecules: primary, temperature-independent, and the thing that
sets where the equation of state runs out of records. $G$ is derived from it
as an equilibrium coefficient and, like temperature, is undefined off
equilibrium; the Planck length is not. The Planck temperature is not a
temperature anything can have but the upper boundary of the concept, the
point at which one thermal cell is one record. And the Planck scale as "the
scale at which gravity becomes quantum" is the category error the framework
already rejects: it is the Knudsen scale of spacetime, where the
long-wavelength description ends, not where a quantised metric begins.

One further distinction falls out and is the framework's own. Above the
electroweak crossover there are no masses, hence no clocks and no rulers,
and a conformal phase has no record of the metric's scale, only of its
causal structure. So gravity splits there: the conformal (causal) part has
records and survives, the conformal factor, the size, has none and is not a
fact until clocks appear. The Planck scale is then a count at every
temperature and a length only where lengths are recorded.

## 1. Where the equation of state actually fails [STRUCTURAL as arithmetic; the reading CANDIDATE]

Jacobson's entropy is the correlation of vacuum fluctuations across the
horizon with a Planck cutoff, and it is the same in a hot bath as in the
vacuum to leading order: the bath adds a volume term, of order
$T^{3}L^{3}$ for a patch of size $L$, against an area term $L^{2}/l_{P}^{2}$.
For the largest patch that can be in equilibrium, the Hubble radius
$L_{H} \sim M_{P}/T^{2}$, the ratio is $T/M_{P}$. So the correction to the
area law, and with it to the equation of state, is $10^{-20}$ at the QCD
crossover, $10^{-17}$ at the electroweak crossover, $3\times10^{-16}$ at
3.6 TeV, and of order one only at the Planck temperature (Part A). The
count of records on the Hubble horizon tells the same story from the other
side: $N_{H} \approx \pi M_{P}^{4}/(1.66^{2}g_{*}T^{4})$, which is
$7\times10^{77}$ at the QCD crossover, $4\times10^{65}$ at the electroweak
crossover, $10^{60}$ at 3.6 TeV, and reaches one at $0.32\,M_{P}$. Local
equilibrium, the plateau of the previous notes, needs many records per
patch; it has them at every temperature the framework owns and loses them
only at the Planck temperature, where the whole horizon is one record.
Radiation-dominated expansion at the electroweak crossover is an equilibrium
solution of the equation of state, and nothing in the framework says
otherwise.

What high temperature does do, from the earlier notes, is remove clocks at
the electroweak crossover and records at the Planck temperature. Gravity as
equation of state survives the first and not the second.

## 2. What "gravity fails" would mean [CANDIDATE]

Off equilibrium the record fluid does not vanish. The first departure is
dissipative: entropy production, bulk and shear, with the shear coefficient
already computed in the literature as the horizon's viscosity at the
Kovtun--Son--Starinets value (Jacobson note, section 4). Gravity there is
still the mutual influence of energy and causal structure, no longer
described by a time-symmetric equation of state but by a viscous one, the
way a gas at high Knudsen number still transmits momentum without obeying
the sound equation. The final departure is the absence of locked relations
altogether, the pre-geometric layer with nothing above it. There the word
"gravity" has no referent, because in this framework gravity is what locked
relations do, and there are none. That is the framework's answer to "does
gravity fail": its law fails at the capacity bound, its dissipative
successor fails when records stop persisting, and its subject is absent
below that. None of these is a failure of physics; each is the end of a
description's domain, which is exactly what Jacobson's analogy predicts.

## 3. What the Planck scale is [CANDIDATE]

The logical order in the framework is the reverse of the usual one. The
usual: $G$ is fundamental, $l_{P} = \sqrt{\hbar G/c^{3}}$ is derived, and the
Planck scale is where quantum gravity begins. The framework's: the record's
size $l_{c}$ is fundamental, the area per locked relation, Jacobson's
cutoff, which his own consistency argument places at the Planck length and
which Paper 2's count-density dictionary is to fix (op:p2-coefficient);
$G = l_{c}^{2}/\hbar$ is derived from it through the equilibrium coefficient
$\eta = 1/4l_{c}^{2}$; and the Planck scale is where the equation of state
runs out of records to average, the Knudsen scale of spacetime.

Three consequences. The Planck length survives every failure discussed
above, because it is a property of the substrate and not of any equilibrium:
it is the smallest record, the densest packing, the grain. What changes
with temperature is not the grain but how densely records are packed, up to
the capacity; ordinary matter's entropy is far below the area bound because
its records are coarse, and horizons saturate it because theirs are
Planck-fine. $G$, by contrast, is an equilibrium coefficient, defined where
the equation of state holds and undefined where it does not, exactly as
temperature is; a "running $G$ at high temperature" is not a thing the
framework predicts, since off equilibrium there is no $G$ to run and on
equilibrium the coefficient is a pure number times the grain. And the Planck
temperature is a boundary of a concept rather than a value of it: it is the
temperature at which a thermal cell is one record, so that "temperature",
which needs an equilibrated patch of many records, stops being defined at
the same point as the equation of state. Nothing is at the Planck
temperature; things approach it.

The framework must also own what this leaves undetermined. Its one
structural dimensional input is the electroweak scale $v$; the record's
grain is a second scale; their ratio is $M_{P}/v = 5\times10^{16}$, a Planck
area being $4\times10^{-34}$ electroweak areas (Part B). op:p2-coefficient
fixes the pure number relating entropy to area in record units; it does not
fix the grain in units of $v$, which is the hierarchy, and no note in this
thread has touched it. Say so wherever the Planck scale is called "the
record's size", so that the grain is not mistaken for a derived quantity.

## 4. Conformal gravity above the electroweak crossover [CANDIDATE]

The high-temperature note found that above the electroweak crossover the
framework has no clocks. Add that it has no rulers either, a ruler being a
Compton length, and the consequence for gravity is precise. Massless matter
is conformally invariant; a radiation-dominated universe is conformally
flat; and in conformal time, which is the thermal time of the bath, the
expansion is invisible to everything inside. No physical system made of
massless quanta can record the scale factor, only ratios of it, and ratios
between "different times" need a clock. In the framework's own logic a
quantity of which no record exists is not a fact. So above the electroweak
crossover the metric's causal structure, its conformal class, has records
(light cones, conserved-charge hydrodynamics) and is a fact, while its
conformal factor, its size, has none and is not. Gravity's action on causal
structure, the tipping of cones by energy, survives; gravity's action on
duration and length, which is what a clock slowed by computational load
measures (op:p2-newton), has nothing to act on. The scale of the universe
becomes a fact at the electroweak crossover, when the first clocks appear
and $k_{B}T/mc^{2}$ becomes measurable from inside. This is a reading of
conformal invariance, standard physics, in the framework's terms; its value
is that it says which half of gravity each side of the crossover keeps, and
it makes the Newtonian route of Paper 2 explicitly a below-crossover
statement.

The Planck scale reads accordingly: as a count, one record, it makes sense
at every temperature; as a length, $1.6\times10^{-35}$ m, it makes sense
only where lengths are recorded, below the crossover; and at the Planck
temperature it is a count of one, with no equilibrium to be a coefficient of.

## 5. Fences and pushback

- The premise "the Einstein equation fails at high temperature" is false
  for every temperature the framework owns; it fails at the Planck
  temperature and, in the local limit, on Planck-size patches. The thread's
  earlier phrase "gravity lives in the post-FANOUT regime" should not be
  read as "gravity is fragile"; it is robust up to the capacity bound.
- "The Planck length is the record's size" is Jacobson's cutoff argument
  plus the framework's naming, and it is CANDIDATE until op:p2-coefficient
  delivers the count. The hierarchy $M_{P}/v$ is untouched.
- Section 4's "no fact of the scale factor above the crossover" rests on
  the framework's "no record, no fact" and on conformal invariance of
  massless matter; the trace anomaly and the bath's temperature as a
  potential scale are the objections to answer (the temperature is a scale
  only against a mass, so the objection fails in a massless phase, but it
  should be answered in the text).
- Nothing here is observational. It is bookkeeping about where
  descriptions end.
