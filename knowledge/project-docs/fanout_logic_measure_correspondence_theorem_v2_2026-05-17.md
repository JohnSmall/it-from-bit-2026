# FANOUT as the Common Boundary of Anhomomorphic Logic and Signed Probability

## A Theorem-Document Draft

---

## 1. Statement

**Theorem (FANOUT Correspondence).** Let H be a finite-dimensional Hilbert space equipped with a FANOUT operation F implemented as a special commutative Frobenius algebra (SCFA) spider with classical basis B = {|i⟩}. Let ρ be a density operator on H. Then:

**(a) Post-FANOUT regime.** The output F(ρ) = Σ_i ⟨i|ρ|i⟩ |i⟩⟨i| satisfies:

- The Kolmogorov axioms on the Boolean lattice 𝓑(B) generated by B (positivity, normalisation, finite additivity).
- Boolean homomorphism: a truth-value assignment φ: 𝓑(B) → {0,1} consistent with classical logic (preserving ∧, ∨, ¬, satisfying modus ponens) exists for every measurement outcome.

**(b) Pre-FANOUT regime.** If ρ has any non-zero off-diagonal element ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ for i ≠ j ∈ B, then:

- Any quasi-probability representation that is tomographically complete for quantum mechanics and respects B has signed (negative or complex) values for ρ. *(Spekkens' contextuality-negativity theorem applied basis-relatively.)*
- The propositional structure on the projective lattice of H is anhomomorphic with respect to B: there is no Boolean homomorphism extending the truth assignments on B-eigenstates to all projectors. *(Kochen-Specker via Sorkin's co-event analysis.)*

**(c) Common origin.** The off-diagonal elements ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ for i ≠ j are simultaneously the algebraic source of both failures in (b). When they vanish, both failures vanish at the same instant — namely the instant F fires.

In one sentence: **FANOUT marks a single boundary, and the two regimes either side of it have synchronised signatures — signed measure plus anhomomorphic logic on one side, positive measure plus Boolean logic on the other.**

## 2. Setup

### 2.1 SCFA spider as FANOUT

A special commutative Frobenius algebra on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space H is a tuple (Δ, ε, μ, η) where Δ: H → H ⊗ H is a comultiplication ("copy"), ε: H → ℂ is a counit ("delete"), μ and η are their Hilbert-space adjoints, satisfying coassociativity, the counit law, the Frobenius law, and the **specialness** condition μ ∘ Δ = id.

**Coecke-Pavlovic-Vicary (2013).** SCFAs on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space H are in bijection with orthonormal bases of H. The comultiplication Δ acts on basis elements as Δ|i⟩ = |i⟩ ⊗ |i⟩.

We define **FANOUT in basis B** as the operation F: ρ → Σ_i ⟨i|ρ|i⟩ |i⟩⟨i|, which is the result of a spider firing in basis B and discarding the second copy. Operationally: F is the act of producing a classical record of ρ in basis B. Physically: F is decoherence in B.

### 2.2 Anhomomorphic logic (Sorkin)

Given a histories-theoretic event algebra Σ (the Boolean algebra of subsets of a sample space Ω), a **co-event** is a function φ: Σ → ℤ₂. The co-event is **homomorphic** (classical) if it respects Boolean operations: φ(A ∩ B) = φ(A) ∧ φ(B), φ(A ∪ B) = φ(A) ∨ φ(B), φ(Ω \ A) = ¬φ(A). Otherwise it is **anhomomorphic**.

Sorkin (2007) showed that classical homomorphism cannot accommodate Kochen-Specker-type configurations, and proposed the **multiplicative scheme**: φ(A ∩ B) = φ(A) ∧ φ(B), but ∨ and ¬ are not preserved. The cost is failure of classical logical principles, including (in some schemes) modus ponens — which Dowker and Wallden have spent considerable effort trying to rescue.

### 2.3 Signed quasi-probability (Spekkens / Wigner)

A **quasi-probability representation** of QM is a map ρ → q_ρ: Λ → ℝ on some measurable set Λ such that expectation values of all observables are recovered as integrals of q_ρ against frame functions. The representation is **tomographically complete** if it determines ρ uniquely.

**Spekkens (2008).** For any tomographically complete quasi-probability representation of QM, the representation has signed values on some states if and only if the represented theory exhibits generalised contextuality. Equivalently: positivity and non-contextuality cannot coexist.

## 3. Proof

### 3.1 Part (a): Post-FANOUT yields classical regime

Standard. F(ρ) is diagonal in B, so its eigenvalues p_i := ⟨i|ρ|i⟩ are non-negative (because ρ ⪰ 0) and sum to 1 (because tr ρ = 1). The map A ↦ Σ_{i ∈ A} p_i for A ∈ 𝓑(B) is a Kolmogorov probability measure. The truth assignment φ(A) = 1 if the measurement outcome is in A, 0 otherwise, is a Boolean homomorphism: A and B are disjoint subsets of the spider's eigenbasis, so ∩, ∪, ¬ act as ordinary set operations on a finite set, and homomorphism is automatic.

### 3.2 Part (b1): Pre-FANOUT, any B-respecting quasi-probability is signed

Suppose ρ has a non-zero off-diagonal element ⟨i|ρ|j⟩, i ≠ j. Then ρ is non-classical relative to B: it cannot be written as a convex combination of projectors {|k⟩⟨k| : k ∈ B}. Such a state exhibits preparation contextuality with respect to the measurement basis B (different ensembles of B-classical states give the same averaged statistics only if their off-diagonals all vanish). By Spekkens' theorem applied to the operational restriction "preparations and measurements respecting B", any tomographically complete quasi-probability representation must take signed values on ρ.

### 3.3 Part (b2): Pre-FANOUT, the propositional structure is anhomomorphic

The projective lattice L(H) of subspaces of H, partially ordered by inclusion, is non-distributive (for dim H ≥ 2) and contains non-commuting projectors. A Boolean homomorphism φ: L(H) → ℤ₂ would require, for any two projectors P, Q with PQ ≠ QP, a consistent assignment of truth values respecting the lattice operations. The Kochen-Specker theorem (in d ≥ 3) shows no such homomorphism exists. Sorkin's analysis recasts this: the obstruction is that the homomorphism would have to commute with all bases simultaneously, but the projectors associated with non-zero off-diagonal coherences ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ in B do not commute with the B-projectors.

For d = 2, a Boolean homomorphism on L(H) does exist for each individual basis, but no single homomorphism is compatible with all bases simultaneously when the state has non-zero coherence in any basis distinct from the homomorphism's own basis. The same algebraic feature — non-zero ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ — is what makes ρ a coherent superposition relative to B and what forces anhomomorphism for any homomorphism not aligned with B.

### 3.4 Part (c): Common origin

In Sections 3.2 and 3.3, the **same** algebraic feature — non-zero off-diagonal elements ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ in basis B — is the source of both failures.

Concretely, let ρ be expressed in basis B as ρ = Σ_{i,j} ρ_{ij} |i⟩⟨j|, with ρ_{ii} = p_i. Then:

- The **Wigner function** (or any B-respecting quasi-probability) takes the form q_ρ(λ) = Σ_i p_i q_{|i⟩⟨i|}(λ) + Σ_{i≠j} ρ_{ij} q_{|i⟩⟨j|}(λ). The first sum is a classical mixture (positive). The second sum, generated by the off-diagonals, is what carries the signedness — it is the interference contribution.

- The **Boolean homomorphism failure** is the statement that propositions about *coherent superpositions* — those whose representation in B has non-zero off-diagonals — cannot have classical truth values that respect Boolean operations. The proposition "system is in the |+⟩ subspace" cannot be assigned a definite truth value compatible with both B-classical assignments and the rotated-basis classical assignments unless the off-diagonals vanish.

The FANOUT operation F sets ρ_{ij} → 0 for i ≠ j by construction. Thus F simultaneously:

- Eliminates the signed contribution to all B-respecting quasi-probabilities, leaving only the positive classical mixture Σ_i p_i q_{|i⟩⟨i|}.
- Eliminates the obstruction to Boolean homomorphism, because all surviving propositional content is now compatible with the B-eigenbasis.

The two failures cease at the same instant because they are caused by the same matrix elements. ∎

## 4. Concrete example: single qubit in |+⟩

Let H = ℂ², B = {|0⟩, |1⟩}, ρ = |+⟩⟨+| where |+⟩ = (|0⟩ + |1⟩)/√2. In B:

ρ = (1/2)|0⟩⟨0| + (1/2)|0⟩⟨1| + (1/2)|1⟩⟨0| + (1/2)|1⟩⟨1|

Off-diagonals: ρ_{01} = ρ_{10} = 1/2.

**Quasi-probability side.** In the discrete Wigner function on ℤ₂ × ℤ₂ (Wootters), the |+⟩ state has Wigner values W(0,0) = 1/2, W(1,0) = 1/2, W(0,1) = 0, W(1,1) = 0 — actually positive in this representation, but that's because Wootters is *aligned* with the Hadamard basis. In a B-respecting quasi-probability representation (e.g. one designed to make {|0⟩⟨0|, |1⟩⟨1|} positive), |+⟩ takes signed values precisely because of the off-diagonal contributions from ρ_{01}, ρ_{10}.

**Logical side.** The proposition P = "system is in state |0⟩" has projector |0⟩⟨0|. The proposition Q = "system is in state |+⟩" has projector |+⟩⟨+|. A Boolean homomorphism on {P, ¬P, Q, ¬Q} would need to assign truth values consistent with both bases, but P and Q do not commute, so no such homomorphism exists. The state |+⟩ exhibits anhomomorphism for any truth assignment respecting B.

**Post-FANOUT.** F applied in B yields F(ρ) = (1/2)|0⟩⟨0| + (1/2)|1⟩⟨1|. Off-diagonals are zero. Now:

- Any B-respecting quasi-probability is just the classical Bernoulli(1/2) distribution on {|0⟩, |1⟩}: positive, no signs.
- The Boolean lattice 𝓑(B) = {∅, {|0⟩}, {|1⟩}, {|0⟩, |1⟩}} has a homomorphic truth assignment determined by the measurement outcome.

Both the signedness and the anhomomorphism vanish in the same step.

## 5. Connection to existing programmes

**Sorkin (1994 onwards).** Quantum measure theory replaces the additive measure of classical probability with a non-additive μ(A) = D(A,A) where D is the decoherence functional. The anhomomorphic logic / co-event programme (Sorkin 2007, Dowker-Ghazi-Tabatabai 2008, Ghazi-Tabatabai-Wallden 2009) characterises the propositional structure that accompanies this generalised measure. Identified the structure but lacked an operational criterion for *when* the homomorphic regime is recovered.

**Spekkens (2008).** Identified that signed quasi-probability is mathematically equivalent to generalised contextuality. Identified the structure on the measure side, lacked an operational criterion for *when* the positive regime is recovered.

**Coecke-Kissinger and predecessors.** SCFA = classical structure in CQM. Provides the categorical machinery and identifies SCFA-firing as the formal object that produces classical data. Did not connect explicitly to either Sorkin or Spekkens.

**This theorem.** The three programmes describe the same boundary in three vocabularies. SCFA-firing (Coecke-Kissinger) is the operational implementation of the boundary. Anhomomorphism (Sorkin) is the logical signature pre-firing. Signedness (Spekkens) is the measure-theoretic signature pre-firing. The theorem above is the explicit identification: a single algebraic event (off-diagonals going to zero) closes off both signatures simultaneously, and the operational interpretation (clonability of information into a classical record) gives the boundary physical content.

## 6. Settled / new / open

**Settled (each independently established in the cited literature):**

- SCFA ↔ orthonormal basis correspondence (Coecke-Pavlovic-Vicary 2013).
- Spekkens contextuality-negativity equivalence (Spekkens 2008).
- Sorkin/Kochen-Specker via co-events (Sorkin 2007, Dowker-Ghazi-Tabatabai 2008).
- Decoherence in basis B = elimination of off-diagonals in B (textbook).
- FANOUT criterion for classicality (clonability; this framework, 2007 onwards; latent in no-cloning literature).

**New from this theorem:**

- The identification of FANOUT as the *common* boundary for anhomomorphism (logical) and signedness (measure-theoretic).
- The observation that the same off-diagonal elements ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ in B simultaneously generate both signatures, so they vanish together.
- The reframing of Sorkin/Dowker's modus ponens difficulty as a correctly-located limit of the formalism rather than a defect: anhomomorphism is the proper pre-FANOUT logic; FANOUT supplies the missing operational criterion for when classical (homomorphic) logic is available.
- A categorical bridge between Sorkin's quantum measure theory and Spekkens' negative quasi-probability programme via Coecke-Kissinger SCFA.
- **Corollary (§8): the identity predicate `x = y` is Boolean-bivalently meaningful iff FANOUT is available in some basis aligned with the state.** This gives Krause's quasi-set theory an operational origin (m-atoms = entities whose state is not post-FANOUT in any available classical basis) and inverts the causal direction of de Barros and Holik (2020): ontological indistinguishability is not primitive but downstream of FANOUT failure.

**Open:**

- A full functorial statement: a category of pre-FANOUT structures (with anhomomorphic logics and signed quasi-probabilities as two faithful representations) and a FANOUT functor to the category of classical structures (Boolean logics + Kolmogorov measures). Does the diagram commute appropriately? Plausible from the pieces above but not written down.
- Generalisation to infinite-dimensional H. The CPV theorem is finite-dimensional; SCFA structures in infinite dimensions are subtler. Quantum measure theory is comfortable with infinite sample spaces, so the identification should extend, but the proof needs care.
- The role of complex (vs merely signed) quasi-probability. This theorem treats both under "signed", but the framework's deeper claim is that complex amplitudes are forced by self-reference (seven-step). The relationship between *complex* quasi-probability (Kirkwood-Dirac) and the Lawvere-Yanofsky obstruction is not fully captured here — this theorem is one step downstream of that derivation.
- A rigorous functorial statement of the Spencer-Brown connection sketched in §8.4: that exchange phases (boson +1, fermion −1, anyon e^{iθ}) are the Spencer-Brown imaginary values of the identity predicate, with anyonic braid group representations as the logic of complex-valued identity statements. Kauffman's work on iterants and Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form* in the quantum context is the obvious starting point; a Lawvere-fixed-point reformulation of re-entry would close the gap.

## 7. Implications for the framework and Paper 1

**For Paper 1's QM-derivation section.** The FANOUT criterion is doing more work than previously stated. It is not only the boundary between epistemological and ontological information (grounding the measurement postulate) and the source of decoherence (grounding state reduction). It is *also* the boundary that simultaneously closes off pre-FANOUT signed/complex amplitudes and pre-FANOUT anhomomorphic logic. This unifies:

- Born rule (boundary condition: signed → positive)
- Bivalence (boundary condition: anhomomorphic → Boolean)
- Decoherence (mechanism: off-diagonals → 0)
- Classical record (operational: clonable copy in environment)

These are now identified as four readings of the same operational fact. A single paragraph in Paper 1 making this identification adds substantial weight to the FANOUT criterion.

**For the Sorkin/Dowker programme.** The modus ponens problem reframes from "co-event schemes failing classical logic" to "classical logic is the post-FANOUT regime by construction; pre-FANOUT, modus ponens failure is the expected signature of a regime where definite facts have not yet been formed." The "missing privilege" of one realised co-event is supplied by FANOUT: the realised co-event is the one that gets clonably copied into the environment, fixing a record. Anhomomorphic logic is not defective; it is the correct logic of the regime where no record has been formed.

**For Växjö positioning.** This adds Sorkin/Dowker as a fourth tradition in the framework's bibliographic spine, alongside Coecke-Kissinger (categorical), Aaronson (computational), and Spekkens (operational). Four independent traditions converging on FANOUT-as-boundary is harder to dismiss than three. It is also a natural conversational opener with Dowker, should that channel be pursued.

## 8. Corollary: Identity-of-Reference and Quasi-Set Theory

Added 17 May 2026, prompted by reading de Barros & Holik (2020) and the observation that quasi-set theory's restriction of the identity predicate is operationally identical to a restriction of FANOUT availability.

### 8.1 Statement

**Corollary (Identity requires FANOUT).** Let ρ be a state on H, and let B be a basis. The Boolean-bivalent meaningfulness of "*this particular* entity prepared in state ρ" with respect to B requires F_B(ρ) = ρ — i.e., ρ must be in the post-FANOUT regime of B. For ρ with non-zero off-diagonals ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ in B (i ≠ j), the identity predicate `x = y` admits no Boolean truth value consistent with B; at best it admits a Spencer-Brown imaginary value whose phase encodes the same information as the off-diagonal element.

### 8.2 Proof

The Boolean predicate `x = y` over a domain D is operationally a comparison: it requires two registers each containing a candidate referent, and a procedure that verifies their agreement on all features distinguishable within D. The two-register comparison decomposes as (i) a copy operation producing a second register guaranteed equal to the first on the relevant features, followed by (ii) a measurement returning 1 iff the candidates agree. Step (i) is FANOUT.

By the no-cloning theorem, step (i) is unavailable for arbitrary quantum states. It is available exactly when the state is diagonal in the chosen copying basis — i.e., when F_B(ρ) = ρ. For ρ with non-zero ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ in B, no procedure within the theory produces the two-register comparison required to evaluate `x = y` bivalently; the closest available operation is decoherence in B, which destroys the off-diagonals and produces a *different* state F_B(ρ) — a procedure that resolves the question only by changing what is being asked about.

Identity-of-reference is therefore well-defined Boolean-bivalently iff F_B(ρ) = ρ. This is the main theorem's part (a) applied to the identity predicate as a special case of Boolean lattice membership: the proposition "x is the same referent as y" is a member of the Boolean algebra of post-FANOUT outcomes, and is anhomomorphic with respect to any basis in which ρ has non-zero coherence.

For pre-FANOUT states, the relevant Spencer-Brown imaginary value is the phase of ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ itself: a complex number quantifying the obstruction to bivalent resolution. The Spencer-Brown re-entry — a form that contains itself, oscillating between marked and unmarked, resolved by an imaginary value — is structurally the same object as the Lawvere-Yanofsky diagonal obstruction in the basis-aligned identity question. ∎

### 8.3 Quasi-set theory as the metalinguistic shadow

Krause (1992) constructs a set theory in which the identity predicate `x = y` is restricted on a designated subclass — the m-atoms — with a weaker equivalence relation `x ≡ y` (indistinguishability) replacing it. The axioms permit cardinality assignment to collections of m-atoms but forbid demonstrative reference to particular members. This is the formal apparatus de Barros and Holik invoke to argue that ontological indistinguishability of quantum particles leads to no-signaling and thence to negative probability.

By Corollary 8.1, the m-atom restriction has an operational origin: **m-atoms are precisely those entities whose state is not post-FANOUT in any classical basis available to the theory**. The set-theoretic restriction is the metalinguistic reflection of an operational fact — namely, that no procedure within the theory produces the two-register comparison required to evaluate identity. Krause's axioms correctly describe the formal structure of pre-FANOUT identity. The ontological reading — that quantum particles possess an intrinsic feature called "indistinguishability" — is unnecessary; it is the residue of describing pre-FANOUT structure in a metalanguage that presupposes post-FANOUT identity as the default.

This relocates the order of conceptual priority:

- **Standard reading (de Barros-Holik):** indistinguishability is an ontological feature of certain entities; quasi-set theory formalises this feature; no-signaling and negative probability follow.
- **FANOUT reading:** non-availability of FANOUT is the operational primitive; restriction of the identity predicate is its metalinguistic image; quasi-set theory is the resulting set theory; indistinguishability is the description of pre-FANOUT states in a metalanguage that takes post-FANOUT identity for granted.

The two readings agree on the formal apparatus and disagree on what is fundamental. Under the FANOUT reading, **indistinguishability is the generic condition of any quantum state, and distinguishability is the special case produced *by* FANOUT at measurement**. The "ontological indistinguishability of quantum particles" is then not a separate axiom; it is the same fact as the failure of cloning for non-classical states.

### 8.4 Exchange phases as Spencer-Brown imaginary values

For two identical particles prepared in distinct states |a⟩ and |b⟩, the symmetrisation postulate gives the compound state |ψ⟩ = (|a⟩|b⟩ ± |b⟩|a⟩)/√2, with the ± sign determined by particle type. The exchange operator P_{12} satisfies P_{12}|ψ⟩ = ±|ψ⟩.

The proposition "particle 1 is the one in state |a⟩" is exactly the identity-of-reference question to which the corollary applies. It admits no Boolean truth value: under exchange, the proposition flips, but the physical state (up to global phase) does not. The ± sign in front of |ψ⟩ is the Boolean residue of the underlying Spencer-Brown imaginary value: the system records the unresolvability of "which is which" as a relative phase.

Bosons (+1) and fermions (−1) are *real-valued degenerate cases* of the Spencer-Brown structure. The phase has been projected onto the two-element subgroup {+1, −1} of U(1). Anyons, with exchange phases e^{iθ} for general θ, realise the full structure: the truth value of identity-of-reference is *genuinely complex*, and the braid group representations governing anyonic exchange are the logic of complex-valued identity statements.

The framework's prediction that anyons are pre-FANOUT identity structure made manifest has an experimental signature: topological quantum computing operates in a regime where the Spencer-Brown imaginary value is *not* projected away at measurement but remains as the computational resource. Non-Abelian anyon braiding is, on this reading, the operational realisation of complex-valued logic — Spencer-Brown's imaginary truth values made into a working computational primitive.

### 8.5 Inversion of the de Barros-Holik chain

de Barros and Holik (2020, eqs 37-38) argue: indistinguishability of properties → marginal of A is context-independent → no-signaling → (when contextual) negative probability. Their "identity card" argument is that signaling would require an observable asymmetry between Alice's marginals in contexts B and B', and such an asymmetry would constitute a label that distinguishes the supposedly-indistinguishable particles.

Corollary 8.1 endorses the formal chain but reverses its causal direction. The deep premise is not that certain particles happen to be indistinguishable, but that FANOUT is operationally restricted on non-classical states. The chain then runs:

1. FANOUT is unavailable for states ρ with non-zero ⟨i|ρ|j⟩ in any classical basis.
2. By Corollary 8.1, identity-of-reference is therefore not Boolean-bivalent for such states.
3. Quasi-set theory is the metalinguistic record of this fact, with m-atoms as its formal carriers.
4. The no-signaling condition follows because attaching de Barros-Holik's "identity card" requires precisely the two-register FANOUT comparison that is unavailable.
5. Negative probability is the correction term required when classical Boolean reasoning (which presupposes FANOUT) is applied to pre-FANOUT states.

The "miscounting" interpretation in the de Barros-Holik conclusion then takes a particularly clean form: classical Boolean reasoning is not over-counting indistinguishable particles; classical Boolean reasoning is correct for FANOUT-available states, and the apparent miscount arises from applying it to states for which FANOUT has not been performed (or cannot be). The negative probabilities are the bookkeeping required to extend Boolean counting into a regime where its operational presupposition has not been met.

### 8.6 Rhetorical use for Paper 1

A direct paragraph for Paper 1's negative-probability section:

> de Barros and Holik [2020] argue that ontological indistinguishability of quantum particles leads to no-signaling and hence to negative probabilities. We endorse the formal chain but invert the causal direction. Indistinguishability is not an ontological property of certain particles; it is the generic condition of any state for which FANOUT is unavailable. In quasi-set theory, the restriction of the identity predicate to m-atoms is a *consequence* of the failure of cloning for non-classical states, not a foundational stipulation. The de Barros-Holik "miscounting" interpretation of negative probabilities then takes a particularly natural form: classical Boolean logic presupposes FANOUT, and applying it to pre-FANOUT states requires negative corrections — which is Wigner-function negativity. The Spencer-Brown imaginary value of `x = y` in the pre-FANOUT regime is the relative phase under exchange, with its real-valued projection (boson +1, fermion −1) being the FANOUT operation rendering identity Boolean. Anyons, retaining the full complex phase, realise the pre-FANOUT identity structure most directly.

This positions the framework as endorsing the de Barros-Holik formal results while subsuming their interpretation under a more fundamental operational criterion. It also enrolls Spencer-Brown's *Laws of Form* as a precursor to the framework's treatment of complex amplitudes — a precursor with which Bertrand Russell expressed approval, giving the move some historical respectability against accusations of mysticism.
