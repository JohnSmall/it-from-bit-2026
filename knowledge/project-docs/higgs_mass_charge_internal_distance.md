## 2. The Higgs Mechanism and Non-Computability

### 2.1 The Sombrero Potential as a Computability Landscape

The standard Higgs potential V(φ) = μ²|φ|² + λ|φ|⁴ with μ² < 0 produces the sombrero shape: the symmetric point φ = 0 is a local maximum, and the minimum is a circle of degenerate vacua at |φ| = v.

**Reinterpretation**: The symmetric point φ = 0 is the non-computable state — the state where the full gauge symmetry is manifest, no preferred complex direction has been singled out, and no computability split has occurred. It corresponds to the octonionic Hopf structure before dimensional reduction from 9+1 to 3+1. This state cannot be stably occupied for the same reason a self-referential fixed point cannot be stably occupied (Lawvere-Yanofsky): the system must resolve the non-computability.

The three types of excitation around the true vacuum have clear interpretations:

- **Goldstone bosons** (massless, motion around the rim): Free movement between equivalent computable representations. These cost nothing because they do not change the distance from non-computability. They are "eaten" by the W and Z bosons in the Standard Model.

- **Radial Higgs mode** (massive, ~125 GeV): The cost of pushing back *toward* the non-computable symmetric point. Its mass measures the stiffness of the barrier between computable and non-computable regimes.

- **The vacuum expectation value** v ≈ 246 GeV: The scale at which the computability split occurs. Below this energy, we are in the computable realm. Above it, we approach the non-computable symmetric phase.

### 2.2 Imaginary Mass as Pre-Debt

In the complex information framework, S = −ln|p| − iθ, where the imaginary part is information debt. The Higgs field's imaginary mass parameter (m = i|μ|, since μ² < 0) means the mass is purely imaginary.

If mass is the logical debt accrued when forcing non-computability into the computable realm, then **imaginary mass is pre-debt** — the state before the forcing has occurred, where the information content is entirely in the imaginary channel.

Spontaneous symmetry breaking converts imaginary mass (pure debt, no classical content) into real mass (actualised debt that manifests as inertia). The W and Z boson masses, and all fermion masses via Yukawa couplings, are the real-valued debt produced by this conversion.

### 2.3 The Meissner Effect and the Entanglement Triangle

The Anderson-Higgs mechanism was discovered in condensed matter before particle physics. In superconductivity, the photon acquires an effective mass inside the superconductor, expelling magnetic fields (Meissner effect). The condensed matter context makes the entanglement explicit: Cooper pairs are manifestly entangled electron pairs.

The chain in the framework is:

1. Cooper pairs are entangled
2. Entanglement has retrocausal/loop structure (zigzag = Hopf link)
3. Retrocausal propagation is formally tachyonic
4. The tachyonic mode condenses
5. Mass is generated for the photon inside the superconductor

The particle physics Higgs mechanism is the same process at the fundamental level, but with the Hopf bundle's nontriviality playing the role that Cooper pair entanglement plays in superconductivity. Szangolies' observation — that the Higgs field is the dynamical variable promoted from the preferred imaginary direction — connects: the reason the imaginary direction must become dynamical is that the bundle is nontrivial (the direction cannot be assigned globally), and the reason the bundle is nontrivial is that the self-referential structure of quantum mechanics prevents global consistency of the computable representation.

### 2.4 Massless vs Massive: Who Carries the Debt?

This gives a clean criterion:

- **Photon** (massless): Corresponds to U(1)_EM, which survives the computability split. No debt.
- **Gluons** (massless but confined): Correspond to SU(3), which comes from the octonionic sector external to the ℂ ⊗ ℍ spacetime algebra. They do not participate in the symmetry breaking. No individual mass debt, but collective confinement debt.
- **W and Z bosons** (massive): The gauge bosons of SU(2) that had to break for the computability split to occur. Their mass IS the debt.
- **Fermions** (massive via Yukawa): Acquire debt in proportion to how strongly they couple to the computability boundary (the Higgs field). The top quark (Yukawa ≈ 1) sits right at the boundary. The electron (tiny Yukawa) barely interacts with it.

## 3. Mass as Internal Non-Computable Distance

### 3.1 The Core Proposal

Every particle traverses non-computable distance at rate c. For a massive particle, some of that traversal is happening in the fibre directions of the Hopf bundle rather than in the base space (external spacetime). From the outside, this internal traversal looks like "not moving" — the particle appears to have slowed below c.

- **Massless particle**: All traversal is external. Moves at c through space. No internal loop.
- **Massive particle at rest**: All traversal is internal. "Moves through time" at c, traversing its internal non-computable loop at the maximum rate. The Compton wavelength λ_C = 1/m is the circumference of the internal loop.
- **Massive particle in motion**: Traversal budget is split between internal (mass) and external (spatial motion). Total rate is always c.

### 3.2 Connection to Standard Relativity

This has a well-known shadow in standard relativistic kinematics. The 4-velocity of any particle has magnitude c — a particle at rest is "moving through time" at speed c. As it is boosted, some time-directed motion is redirected into spatial motion, but the 4-speed remains c. The energy-momentum relation E² = p² + m² (natural units) is the Pythagorean theorem applied to this partition.

The new content is the *interpretation*: the internal traversal is not merely a kinematic curiosity but physical motion through fibre directions of the Hopf bundle. The particle is navigating the topological obstruction created by the nontrivial bundle structure.

### 3.3 Zitterbewegung: This Already Exists in the Dirac Equation

Schrödinger (1930) discovered that the velocity operator for a Dirac electron has eigenvalues ±c — the electron is *always* moving at the speed of light. The observed sub-luminal motion is the time-averaged result of the electron zigzagging between its left-handed and right-handed Weyl components at speed c. The mass term in the Dirac Lagrangian, m(ψ̄_L ψ_R + ψ̄_R ψ_L), is precisely the coupling that flips chirality, redirecting the lightlike trajectory.

In the framework, this connects as follows:

- The **retrocausal zigzag** (information forward → backward → forward, the Hopf link) **IS the Zitterbewegung**. The L-R-L zigzag of the Dirac electron is the retrocausal loop structure viewed in the particle physics coordinate system.
- The **mass term** coupling L to R is the Yukawa coupling to the Higgs, which is the coupling to the computability boundary.
- The **Zitterbewegung frequency** ω = 2m (natural units) is the rate at which the particle traverses its internal loop.

Therefore: Zitterbewegung IS the particle traversing its internal non-computable distance. The mass term IS the mechanism that redirects lightlike traversal from external to internal. The Higgs VEV IS the scale at which external non-computable distance becomes available to be folded into internal loops.

### 3.4 The Speed Limit Falls Out Automatically

On this picture, c needs no separate postulate. If every particle's total traversal rate through non-computable distance is fixed at c (this is the Margolus-Levitin theorem — there is a maximum computational rate per unit energy), then:

- A massless particle devotes all traversal to external motion → moves at c.
- A massive particle cannot exceed c externally because part of its traversal budget is permanently committed to internal loops.
- The heavier the particle, the more budget consumed internally, and the lower the maximum achievable external speed.
- At rest, the entire budget goes internal → the particle "moves through time" at c.

### 3.5 Confinement vs Mass: A Clean Distinction

The three parallelisable sphere obstructions produce different manifestations of non-computable distance:

- **S¹ and S³ obstructions** (electromagnetism and weak force): Produce **mass** — internal loops that consume traversal budget for individual particles.
- **S⁷ obstruction** (strong force): Produces **confinement**, not mass for gluons. The non-associativity of S⁷ does not create internal loops that a single particle traverses. Instead it creates a *shared* internal space that multiple particles must traverse *together*. The non-computable distance is distributed among quarks collectively rather than being internal to any one particle.

Gluons are massless but confined: the debt is not borne individually as internal distance but collectively as the impossibility of separating colour charges.

## 4. Fractional Electric Charge Without SU(5)

### 4.1 The Problem

In the Standard Model, quarks have electric charges ±1/3 and ±2/3. The SU(5) grand unified theory explains this by embedding quarks and leptons in the same multiplet — the U(1) generator must be traceless over the representation, forcing fractional quark charges. But SU(5) also predicts proton decay (quarks can transmute into leptons via X, Y bosons), which has not been observed. Our framework explicitly excludes SU(5) because it does not arise from division algebras. We therefore need an alternative explanation for charge quantization.

### 4.2 The ℂ ⊕ ℂ³ Decomposition

The computability split singles out one imaginary octonion direction (say e₇) as the preferred complex direction. The remaining six imaginary units pair into three complex lines using the Fano plane structure:

(e₁, e₂), (e₃, e₄), (e₅, e₆)

where each pair (eₐ, e_b) forms a complex line via eₐ + e₇·e_b. This gives the decomposition 𝕆 ≅ ℂ ⊕ ℂ³ — one complex dimension (the computability/temporal direction) plus three complex dimensions (the colour directions).

### 4.3 Charge as Projection onto the Computability Axis

Electric charge (after electroweak symmetry breaking) corresponds to the U(1)_EM phase rotation in the singled-out ℂ direction. A particle's electric charge is determined by how its internal fibre path projects onto this direction.

**Leptons** live entirely in the ℂ sector. Their internal paths are aligned with the computability axis. When a lepton winds around the S¹ fibre, it traces a complete circle in the ℂ direction — the projection is total. Charges are integers: 0 (neutrino, no S¹ winding) or −1 (electron, one full winding).

**Quarks** live in one of the three ℂ¹ components of ℂ³. Their internal paths are *not* aligned with the computability axis — they are in colour directions. When a quark winds around its internal loop, only a fraction of that winding projects onto S¹. The three colour directions are symmetrically disposed around the computability axis (related by the Z₃ of the Fano plane). The U(1) generator acts on each ℂ in ℂ³ with eigenvalue −1/3, yielding fractional charge.

The −1/3 is not put in by hand. It arises from the requirement that the U(1) generator is traceless over the full octonionic space — which is the division algebra analogue of the SU(5) tracelessness condition, but now a consequence of the algebra's structure rather than a group-theoretic input.

### 4.4 The ℤ₆ Quotient Is Automatic

The exact Standard Model gauge group is SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1)/ℤ₆, not just SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1). The ℤ₆ = ℤ₂ × ℤ₃ quotient is what enforces charge quantization:

- **ℤ₃** is the center of SU(3) — cyclic permutation of the three colour directions. This is the Z₃ triality of the Fano plane.
- **ℤ₂** is the center of SU(2) — sign flip in the quaternionic sector.

The quotient identifies certain combined colour-isospin-hypercharge transformations, forcing charges to come in multiples of 1/3.

Krasnov's result (2021) makes this explicit: when the computability split selects ℂ ⊂ 𝕆, the centraliser of the complex structure J_R in Spin(9) is SU(3) × SU(2) × U(1)/ℤ₆ — the quotient is automatic. It is not an additional assumption; it falls directly out of the octonionic geometry.

### 4.5 Why This Is Stronger Than SU(5)

In SU(5), charge quantization and quark-lepton unification are the same thing — you get fractional charges *because* quarks and leptons are in the same multiplet. This entails proton decay.

In the division algebra framework, the ℂ and ℂ³ sectors are **algebraically distinct** — one is the computability direction, the other is the colour directions. There is no algebra element that maps between them while preserving the computability split. Charge quantization comes from the geometry of how ℂ embeds in 𝕆, not from quarks and leptons being unified.

This is why the framework predicts no proton decay: the 1/3 charge quantization and the absence of quark-lepton transitions are both consequences of the same octonionic structure. They are not in tension (as they are in SU(5), where one entails the other).

### 4.6 Internal-Distance Interpretation

In the "everything moves at c" picture, electric charge is the **winding number of the particle's internal path projected onto the S¹ fibre** (the computability axis).

- A lepton's path is entirely in the S¹ direction → integer winding → integer charge.
- A quark's path is in a colour direction, tilted away from S¹ → fractional projection → fractional charge.
- The total internal non-computable distance per unit time (the mass) is the same kind of quantity as the charge — both are geometric properties of the same internal path.

The quark's traversal budget is partitioned: part projects onto S¹ (electric charge), part onto S³ (weak isospin), part into S⁷ colour directions (colour charge). Fractional electric charge is the statement that a quark's internal path doesn't fully project onto S¹ — some of its "winding budget" goes sideways into colour space.

## 5. Quantum Numbers and Geodesics: Towards a Unified Picture

### 5.1 Geodesics on the Hopf Fibre

The three insights above converge on a single geometric object: a **closed geodesic on the total fibre space**, where the fibre is the appropriate subspace of S¹ × S³ × S⁷ selected by the computability split. Each particle corresponds to a different closed geodesic. The geodesic encodes:

- **Electric charge**: winding number around S¹ (projection onto computability axis)
- **Weak isospin**: winding in the S³ directions
- **Colour charge**: winding in the S⁷ directions (collective, non-associative)
- **Generation**: number of times the geodesic winds through the fibre before closing (1st, 2nd, 3rd generation = 1, 2, 3 windings, corresponding to the three iterations of self-referential measurement)
- **Mass**: total geodesic length (= total internal non-computable distance per unit time)

All quantum numbers and the mass are properties of a single object. The mass hierarchy between particles reflects different geodesic lengths; the quantum number assignments reflect different winding patterns.

### 5.2 Leptons vs Quarks

For **leptons**, the S⁷ directions are inactive (no colour charge). The internal path lives entirely in S¹ × S³. The Z₃ triality of J₃(𝕆) acts on the three winding modes in this reduced fibre, giving three lepton generations with the clean Koide structure.

For **quarks**, the S⁷ directions are active. The internal path has components in all fibre directions. The non-associativity of S⁷ means path lengths are not simply additive — the S⁷ component interacts non-trivially with the S¹ and S³ components, distorting the pure Z₃ structure. This is why quarks do not satisfy the Koide formula: the non-associative correction from colour engagement breaks the clean trigonometric structure.

The distortion is larger for up-type quarks (stronger Yukawa coupling → paths more deeply embedded in the fibre structure) than for down-type quarks, consistent with the observed Koide deviations:

| Sector | Koide ratio | Deviation from 2/3 |
|--------|------------|-------------------|
| Charged leptons (e, μ, τ) | 0.666661 | ~0 |
| Down quarks (d, s, b) | 0.731 | 0.065 |
| Up quarks (u, c, t) | 0.849 | 0.182 |

### 5.3 The Mass-Charge Relationship

Since mass (geodesic length) and charge (S¹ winding number) are both properties of the same geodesic, the mass-charge relationship for each particle is a geometric constraint. This is why particles with different quantum numbers have different masses — the quantum numbers select the geodesic, and the geodesic determines the mass.

The fermion mass hierarchy (why the top quark is so much heavier than the electron) reflects the geometry of different geodesics:

- The **electron** geodesic: confined to S¹ × S³, first-generation winding, minimal fibre engagement → small mass.
- The **top quark** geodesic: extends through S¹ × S³ × S⁷, third-generation winding, maximum fibre engagement (Yukawa ≈ 1) → large mass.
- The **electron neutrino** geodesic: barely engages the fibre at all → nearly zero mass.

### 5.4 The Angle θ_K as Geodesic Geometry

The single free parameter θ_K ≈ 13° (Higgs-triality misalignment) measures the angle between the Higgs VEV direction and the nearest triality axis in J₃(𝕆). In the geodesic picture, this is the angle between the preferred ℂ direction (computability split) and the nearest Z₃ axis — determining how the three geodesic lengths are distributed relative to each other.

The conjecture θ_K ≈ θ_Cabibbo is natural here: the Cabibbo angle measures the rotation between mass eigenstates and flavour eigenstates, which is the rotation between geodesic directions and triality axes. It should be the same angle because it is the same geometric misalignment viewed from two perspectives.
